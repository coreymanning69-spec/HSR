M57–M69: OAK CITY INTERLUDE

They weren't supposed to stay.

There was no plan, no edict, no moment where anyone sat down and decided: we'll be here for several days. It simply happened the way most things happened around the girls—by accumulation. One morning became an afternoon, an afternoon became a night, a night became a second morning, and at some point the city stopped holding its breath and the girls were just... there.

M57 — THE TELEVISION

Amara had found a television inside one of the Company-cleared buildings and spent three hours explaining to Ember why the people inside it were not real and could not hear her. Ember remained unconvinced on a philosophical level but agreed, provisionally, not to respond to them out loud. She compromised by whispering.

M58 — SERA'S APPETITE

Sera ate. This was not unusual, but the scale of it had become a point of running commentary among the Company logistics staff, who were trying to understand whether divine appetites followed any consistent pattern or whether Sera simply ate when something looked interesting, which appeared to be always. She had consumed an entire pot of something a vendor called stew, three of what someone called pies (she called them "bread pockets," which was more accurate), and a quantity of fried flatbread that the vendor later referred to as "the entire morning's supply" with an expression of pure, dazed wonder.

Soliera moved through it all the way She always did—present, quiet, taking in the texture of the world with the unhurried attention of someone who has all of time and is choosing to spend it here. She watched the traffic patterns. She watched a child argue with its mother. She watched a Company soldier eat a packed lunch alone on a curb and, without looking directly at him, ensured his coffee stayed warm longer than it should have.

Nobody noticed that last part except Bucky, who logged it without comment and then had to sit very still for a moment before continuing his rounds.

M59 — PAVEMENT CRACKS

Amara watched Ember discover the concept of pavement cracks—the old superstition, step on a crack, some mortal nonsense—and then watched Ember spend twenty minutes deliberately stepping on every crack she could find while announcing the result each time.

"Still fine," Ember reported, at the twenty-third crack.

"Yes," Amara said.

"Still fine," Ember said, at the thirty-first.

"Yes, Ember."

"This superstition," Ember said, with great authority, "is very wrong."

It was the Oak City interlude. It was several days of the world running on its new rules—softened, brightened, quietly restructured—and four figures moving through it like they belonged there, which, for the moment, they did.

M60 — AVERNUS INTERLUDE

At some point in those days, the girls also made a brief return through the Gate to the Sanctum — a matter of hours, not days — and began the first steps of a descent into Avernus. It is not written out here. It happened. They came back.

M70–M72: GATE WAKE / SANCTUM ARRIVAL / JOY IMPACT

The Gate opened on the third morning.

The plaza knew before anything came through. The air changed—a pressure shift, a deepening of the light, a sound below sound that made the birds in the nearby roofline lift and circle without landing again. The Company cordon steadied. The townspeople who had gathered at the edges of the crowd pressed back without being asked.

Niv-Saiffar stepped through first.

M71 — SANCTUM ARRIVAL

He came at a pace that could only be called deliberate—not slow, but measured, the walk of something that understood exactly what impression it was making and had decided that impression was correct. Behind him, constructs and stewards flowed in neat formation, their footfalls synchronized, their posture the architectural language of a civilization that had never once been embarrassed about what it was.

The road, within thirty seconds, had become a Sanctum lane. Not by force—no one was moved, no barrier was raised—but by the same quiet physics of displacement that causes people at a party to drift away from the most magnetic person in the room without ever deciding to. The Company personnel found better places to stand. The crowd reshuffled. The constructs moved into the space that appeared.

Niv stood in the center of the plaza and looked at the girls.

M72 — JOY IMPACT

Then Sera was running.

She hit him at a velocity that should have been a disaster. In any sane interpretation of mass and momentum, it was a disaster—two hundred and eighty pounds of ancient Firemind dragon-soul taking the full, joyful, running impact of something that could level city blocks if she put her mind to it. He went back. His feet carved twin scour-lines into the asphalt, deep enough that the grooves were still visible weeks later when someone tried to repave them and gave up.

He didn't fall. He caught her. His arms locked around her like architecture, and the impact resolved not into catastrophe but into a sound that the nearest Company soldiers would later describe, separately and without comparing notes, as: the sound of something enormous being exactly where it was supposed to be.

The crowd—what remained of the watching crowd, the ones who hadn't fled at the Gate opening—went very quiet.

Ember watched from behind Soliera's arm with bright eyes and a grin that split her face nearly in half.

The quiet was the kind that comes not from fear but from the sudden, gut-level recognition that you are watching something private. That this is a reunion happening at a scale you don't have the equipment to fully process, and the only reasonable response is to be still and let it happen.

Soliera stood at the edge of it all and watched Sera and Niv with the expression She kept for things She already knew would be fine.


M73–M75: DECONFLICT / CHURCH APPROACHES / FLANK NEUTRALIZED

Bucky and Niv fell into sync inside of sixty seconds. This was not surprising to anyone who had watched both of them operate—they were, at their cores, the same functional type: men who understood that the job was to make the space work, not to make the space about them.

Tents began blooming along the plaza perimeter. Sanctum stewards set work fronts with the quiet efficiency of people who had done this before, on scales that made this look modest. Locals came to watch and stayed to help. Two stewards were teaching a Company private how to properly tension a line before Bucky had finished his third cup of coffee.

M74 — CHURCH CONVOY

The Church convoy appeared on the eastern approach at mid-morning.

White vehicles. Clean lines. Fourteen knights walking alongside the lead truck in formation, their armor marked with the Church's sigil, their posture the particular kind of deliberate that means: we have decided to look unafraid and we are succeeding mostly. The convoy had been moving since dawn; someone in their command had made the calculation that arriving late was worse than arriving uncertain.

They got to within two hundred yards of the plaza before the road itself stopped cooperating.

No drama. No wall, no gate, no shout. One of the girls—it was Sera, it was always Sera—had simply been walking that direction at that moment, and the lead truck found it suddenly and inexplicably stalled. One draw-cut, barely more than the motion of brushing something off a sleeve, and the engine housing had a new geometry that was not compatible with the engine running.

Fourteen knights stopped.

The vehicle behind them stopped.

The convoy stopped.

"Walk," the word came easily, cheerfully even, drifting back from wherever Sera had already gotten to, "or not at all."

The knights looked at each other. They looked at the stalled truck. They looked at the plaza, where a titan's motionless form still dominated the skyline, where Sanctum constructs moved with unhurried purpose, where a small obsidian girl was currently asking a steward whether she could touch one of the tent spikes.

They walked.

M75 — FLANK NEUTRALIZED

The two black SUVs arrived later, from the south and west at angles that were clearly calculated—pincer geometry, two vehicles, coordinated timing. Professional work, or work that would have been professional against anything that could be surprised. Niv watched them on his auxiliary feeds for eleven seconds and then Mass Paralysis settled over both vehicles like a warm hand pressing gently downward. Drivers, engines, momentum—all of it paused. The SUVs coasted to quiet, harmless stops exactly where they were, as if parking was always the plan.

The drivers remained frozen inside, upright, undamaged, expressions arrested mid-determination.

Bucky walked over, looked through the window at the nearest one, and made a note in his log: third-party. Not Church. Not Company. He'd find out who.

He always found out who.


M76–M79: PLAZA ETIQUETTE / THE CATCH / THE SHED / HORIZONTAL SHEAR

Sera moved through the main square in the late morning with both hands laced behind her head, silk catching the sun, walking with the posture of someone who has never in her life worried about what's behind her.

The effect on the plaza was immediate and entirely atmospheric. Nothing was said. No instruction was given. But muzzles went down—not dramatically, not with the sudden drop of weapons that signals a confrontation resolved, but with the gradual, almost unconscious lowering of things that the hands carrying them had simply decided were less interesting than they'd been a moment ago. Safeties clicked on. Shoulders relaxed. A Company soldier who had been gripping his rifle with both hands for the better part of two hours found, to his mild surprise, that he'd transferred it to one.

Hale, watching from the Church convoy, said nothing. He filed the observation in the part of his mind where he kept things he intended to think about later.

M77 — THE CATCH

The .50 caliber round came from outside the town entirely.

Whatever contractor had set up the shot had chosen well for distance—far enough that the sound arrived after the bullet, far enough that by the time the crack reached the plaza the round was already past the halfway point of its flight. It was a professional decision made by someone who had never considered that the target might see it coming. Might not merely see it, but might track the shimmer it left in the air, step forward three casual paces, and pluck it from flight with a thumb and two fingers, the way you might pick a grape.

Sera held it up and looked at it—still hot, slightly deformed from the snatch—then turned and walked it over to Niv with her arm outstretched.

"What's this?" she asked.

Niv took it from her fingers, examined it once, and said: "A .50 BMG round. Full metal jacket. Whoever fired it is approximately eleven hundred meters in that direction."

"Hm," Sera said, as if this were mildly interesting information, and went back to what she had been doing.

M78 — THE SHED

The shed was a different matter.

It sat at the edge of a residential block the Company had partially cleared—a two-room structure with peeling siding and a door that didn't properly seat in its frame. Sera walked into it without pausing. Inside, someone had decided that a cleared building was only mostly cleared, and the claymore went off at approximately the threshold of the second room.

The blast did nothing meaningful to Sera.

Her dress, however, now had a neat crescent-shaped tear along the hemline where the spray had caught it at the right angle. She looked down at it. She looked back toward the door.

"That was rude," she said, to no one in particular, in the tone of someone who has just had a door held open wrong.

M79 — HORIZONTAL SHEAR

The sniper and spotter in the upper room had approximately one second to understand that the woman who had just walked through a claymore was now looking at the ceiling of the room below them.

"Somebody duck."

The horizontal shear happened at exactly four feet off the foundation. Everything above that line—roof, walls, the upper room, its contents, the weapon mount, the bolt-action rifle, the helmet left behind in the confusion—simply ceased to occupy the same space it had occupied before. It didn't explode. It didn't burn. The cut was clean enough that the grain of the wooden wall planks was still visible in cross-section, a straight splinter line running out into the treeline forty yards away with mathematical precision.

Gas hissed from a ruptured line somewhere in the remnants.

Sera stepped out of what remained of the structure, retrieved the rifle and the helmet from where they'd landed, and carried them back toward the plaza under one arm like groceries.

Evidence, Bucky would say. She'd shrug and hand them over.


M80–M83: CORRIDOR OPENS / TITAN OPTICS / DOMESTIC BEAT / SANCTUM SCALE

By the time the Church convoy was ready to move, the plaza had reached a working arrangement with itself.

Bucky extended the invitation to crawl through—measured, specific, no ambiguity about what "invitation" meant in terms of behavior expected in return. Hale accepted. He communicated this to Malachar and Ysbet in the back of the lead vehicle with three words and the kind of look that ends internal debates.

The convoy came in slowly, respectfully, under strict calm.

It passed under the titan's shadow.

The titan did not move. It had not moved since it arrived, had not shifted weight or changed posture or acknowledged anything that had happened in its proximity. It simply stood there—massive, still, present—the way mountains are present. Not indifferent, exactly. More like: not in the business of being otherwise.

From inside the lead vehicle, Hale watched the shadow pass over the hood and felt the temperature drop for three seconds as they moved through it. He closed his eyes briefly. His lips moved without sound.

It wasn't a prayer he'd been taught. It was something that had been waiting in him for a long time.

Malachar, beside him, looked out the window at the titan's foot—larger than the vehicle, larger than the building next to it—and said nothing. His jaw worked once, a small sideways motion, and then he was still.

In the back, Ysbet had her notebook open.

M82 — BUTTER FIRST

Sera had, at some point, acquired a pancake.

"No," she said firmly to Ember, "butter first. Then syrup. The butter needs to be on the hot part so it melts into it. If you put syrup first it just slides off."

"But the syrup is sweeter," Ember said, with perfect logic.

"The syrup goes on top of the butter."

"But why—"

"Butter. First." Sera pointed with her fork. "This is a rule."

Amara stood nearby with her morning cup of something the Company cooks had put together, watching this exchange with the expression of someone who has given up trying to assign context to their life and has achieved a kind of peace in that surrender. Ember was wearing a new shirt that someone in the Sanctum steward corps had made for her overnight after noticing the old one had acquired significant structural damage during the prior day's events. She kept looking down at it and smoothing it with both hands, delighted.

M83 — SANCTUM SCALE

The scale of the Sanctum presence had settled into its final form overnight: approximately four to five thousand personnel, flowing through the Gate in organized waves, spreading through the city's grid with the efficiency of a system that had done urban integration before and had developed opinions about how to do it correctly.

They cleaned streets—not with visible equipment, but with the unhurried care of people who thought clean streets were a reasonable thing to want. They re-timed intersections. They sat with locals, asked what needed fixing, and sent specialists. They mapped the agricultural edge of the city and sent teams to the fields with tools and knowledge.

Two stewards had gotten into a long conversation with a retired Oak City engineer about the drainage system. The engineer kept pointing at blueprints. The stewards kept asking questions. By afternoon they were all three lying on their stomachs in the street looking into a grate and having what appeared to be a genuinely excellent time.

The Company watched all of this from a careful distance and tried to figure out what the correct paperwork response was. Bucky, who had long since stopped trying to find the correct paperwork response, simply logged it as: integration proceeding. No friction. Then he went to find coffee.
