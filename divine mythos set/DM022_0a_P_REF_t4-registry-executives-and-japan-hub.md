## Kiyuru, Suzu, Gable, Executives, and Japan Hub

KIYURU
Region: Japanese Hub Executive: J Classification: Martial-spiritual combatant /
sword saint
Physical Profile: Six feet, lean. Hair black, pulled back in a simple tie. The build of a
man whose muscle exists for function rather than display. Face calm in the way that
water is calm when it’s very deep and very still.
Ability Profile: Kiyuru is the crystallization of centuries of Japanese martial culture.
Bushido, kenjutsu lineages, the cultural reverence for the blade as a spiritual object — all
of that intent pressure across generations produced a man who IS the ideal Japanese
combatant. His combat style is water and air, not stone or wood. He flows through a
room and people fall apart behind him. Cuts so clean they don’t register for a full second
— a target gets bisected and stands there before gravity figures out what happened. No
wasted motion, no excess force, no collateral damage. Faster than bullets — not
because he dodges but because he’s not where the bullet goes. The bullet arrives and
he’s already somewhere else, flowing around it like a river around a stone.
Spiritual Sensitivity: Animist in the Shinto tradition. Everything has a spirit, everything
has value. He talks to the dog on the road. He rests against a tree and the tree gives him
something back — warmth, restoration, a reciprocal relationship. The natural world
responds to him because his intent toward it is genuine and has been genuine across the
bloodline that produced him. Passive environmental bonuses: better rest near old-
growth, sharper senses near running water, animals calming in his presence. Not magic
in the Western sense — reciprocity. The land knows him because his ancestors knew the
land.
Artifacts:
Kusanagi no Tsurugi (the sword): One of Japan’s three imperial treasures. Cuts
through any supernatural entity regardless of specific resistance profile (silver
vulnerability, cold iron, blessed weapons — irrelevant). The blade cuts because
centuries of belief says it cuts. Period.
Yata no Kagami (the mirror): Reflects spells and incantations back at the caster.
Fireball returns home. Curses reverse. One hand kills everything physical, the other
turns every caster into their own worst enemy.
Yasakani no Magatama (the jewel): Third treasure. The Emperor continuously wears it
at his shrine. Its protection depends on continuous wear and can extend a life for
centuries or millennia. Japan correctly believes the Emperor to be a demigod; that
belief gives him substantial personal strength. Kiyuru carries the other two treasures.

Level-20 runtime: **DM043_D1** is the sole mechanical owner. It fixes AC 22,
220 HP, 120-foot ground speed, five attacks, five reactions, +6 to hit, and
2d10+10 Kusanagi damage. Its declared-number d8 system has no face table: a
matching declared number lands the called action; 8 returns the strike; a
declared 8 that rolls 8 does both. Damage from a committed cut resolves when
the blade returns to the sheath. Exact class chassis, ability scores, saves,
and any numbers not stated in DM043_D1 remain unassigned; do not derive them
from the fiction.

The balance thesis remains: nearly unhittable through position, speed, and
answers, but not artillery. He is a breeze through the room.
Key Combat Example: Sparring with Gable. Kiyuru’s overhead cut opened Gable’s arm
— the blade that America’s Superman thought he was immune to drew blood through
belief-reinforced cellular structure. Gable caught Kusanagi bare-handed on the second
exchange — his belief in his own strength momentarily matching four centuries of
Japanese prayer. Blood ran from his palm. He threw Kiyuru across the room. Kiyuru
landed on the wall feet-first and pushed off, already flowing toward Gable’s blind side.
Personality: Laid-back. Settled. Calm in a way that comes from genuine spiritual
groundedness, not from discipline or training. He believes everything has a spirit and
treats everything accordingly, from the puppy on the road to the tree in the courtyard.
Not performatively peaceful — authentically at rest. The violence, when it comes,
emerges from the same stillness the way a river emerges from a still lake.
Relationship to the Girls: The first T4 who understood what the girls were without
being told. He felt Oak City’s transformation through the trees and the air before he ever
saw Soliera. He bowed on sight — not from training, from spiritual recognition. “It was
not a choice.” Kusanagi went QUIET in Sera’s hand — the sword that had been humming
for four centuries found what it was looking for and went still. Sera told the mirror to say
hi. The mirror said hi back. The grass where he bowed stayed greener for years.
Relationship to J: Nineteen years. J reads reports, Kiyuru reads trees. Between them
they know everything in their hemisphere. The communication between them has
transcended the need for expression — entire conversations pass in a single glance.


SUZU HIKARI
Region: Japan Executive/Authority Context: Japanese national apparatus / Executive
J awareness Classification: Belief-built mech pilot / national protector / Sera gather
candidate
Physical Profile: Four-eight to four-ten, small frame, dark hair and eyes. Pretty in the
traditional register, composed rather than flashy. She shares Sera's height range from
the opposite origin point: Sera was designed into womanhood; Suzu simply grew into
hers.
Ability Profile: Suzu pilots the plum titan, a 120-foot belief construct projected from a
hand-built model kit. The model is not symbolic. It is the anchor. She pilots from the
chest cockpit with the physical kit in front of her, and live modifications to the kit
update the titan in real time. Panel lines, brushwork, repainted sections, and swapped
parts all scale up because the titan is the model believed outward.
Source: Two generations of giant-hero-robot myth, modern protector fantasy, and
national belief density. She is strong because she is famous and believed in, not despite
the apparatus around her. This is the same belief-intent ontology as the rest of the
Primeverse T4 system, expressed through industrial-scale myth rather than sword,
body, or probability.
Balance Rule: The titan is near-immune by mortal measure. Falls, conventional
weapons, and ordinary siege physics do not meaningfully threaten it because conviction
is doing the load-bearing. The soft target is the model kit in the cockpit. Destroy the
model and the titan is gone until Suzu builds a new one.
Power Relationship to Sera: The titan can hit the same outcome as Sera's uncommanded
baseline for a single visible act: building gone, obstacle gone, battlefield changed. This
does not make Suzu divine. The match is outcome, not source or ceiling. Sera is instant,
consent-locked, sovereign-backed, and bottomless when Soliera commands through her.
Suzu is finite, mortal, and T4.
National Apparatus: Public icon and strategic deterrent, run as the Amagatsu
Initiative — the national belief-construct program named for the amagatsu, the
protective effigy-doll that absorbs a child's misfortune (the name rhymes with the
model-kit-as-anchor mechanic). Suzu's own name for the unit is Hakubai (白梅, white
plum), matching its plum plating and white calligraphy. Japan built infrastructure
around her: engineered mega-highway corridors, outdoor staging yards, and carrier
helicopter logistics. The annual transport burden is enormous, but the doctrine is
simple: a near-invulnerable twelve-story protector can be delivered anywhere, and if
the carriers fall she becomes the impact event.
Relationship to Kiyuru/Japan: Kiyuru is the blade-line answer to centuries of martial
and spiritual prayer. Suzu is the machine-line answer to modern protector myth. Japan
now holds two named T4s from two different but compatible belief ecologies: sacred
artifact warrior and hand-built national robot. They and other Japan-hub figures
continue to communicate while off-camera; Suzu's life continues without a meeting with
the Girls. The café sketch is hypothetical and unplayed.
Relationship to the Girls: The titan recognizes divinity before Suzu commands it. When
the girls are present, the machine bows on its own. Suzu's reaction to her own avatar
moving without instruction is the tell. Sera wants her immediately because Suzu carries
power and intelligence in a small mortal body that rhymes with Sera's own scale.
Consent Rule: Sera's interest does not override Suzu's agency. Suzu is a gather
candidate, not a foregone conclusion.
Loneliness Hook: Suzu has been wanted plenty and pursued never. To date her is to
date the robot, the helicopters, the ministry budget, the national-hero schedule, and
the whole apparatus. Sera does not experience any of that as an obstacle, which is why
the first real flirt beat lands differently.
Deep-Dive Status: DM043_0 is the V8 dedicated sheet. This section remains
the power-system and Japan T4 ecosystem source; DM043_0 owns Suzu-specific character,
mechanics retrieval, ambiguous titan-personality lock, and the two independent entry
options.

Current awareness: Suzu remains active with Hakubai, fighting and training in the
Mergeverse. Much of the T4 community is now aware of the Girls, and the wider public
knows the Oak City transformation as the **Oak City Experiment**. Exact individual
reactions remain character-specific rather than a uniform worldview.


GABLE
Region: Neo-America (Washington DC) Executive: Works within Company institutional
structure (Executive unassigned) Classification: America’s apex — full-spectrum
physical enhancement
Physical Profile: Six-three, broad, built like a monument. Dark hair, American-perfect.
The jaw that implies determination, the shoulders that imply safety. Everything about his
physical presence was designed by committee — not literally, but by the collective wish
of millions of Americans across decades who wanted someone who LOOKED like a hero.
Ability Profile: America’s Superman. Strength, speed, durability, flight, heat vision,
vacuum generation — the full package. He is what happens when a civilization’s deepest
wish for a protector gets answered by a universe where wishes have weight. Decades of
comic books, movies, and the cultural mythology of the superhero — in the Primeverse,
that’s not entertainment. That’s a prayer lineage. Millions of kids across multiple
generations genuinely believing someone like that should exist, and the belief
accumulating until the world answered.

Level/attribute lock: run him at the full level-20-equivalent T4 band. Every
ability score is **22 or higher**; **STR 30** and **CON 30** are fixed. The
remaining four exact values are not supplied and must not be invented.

Cross-verse power model: if Gable enters Faerûn, he arrives at full power on a
**battery model**. Separation from Primeverse belief does not switch him off on
arrival. No depletion rate, recharge interval, or terminal empty state is yet
authored.

Two laws carry the chassis. Ordinary matter slides off him because it has no
belief weight; belief-weighted sources such as Kusanagi can land cleanly. The
other injury lane is his own strength returning from something that gives
nothing back. Sera is the fixed benchmark for that law: he punched her, she did
not move or yield, and the returned force broke his hand. She is not a rival on
his ladder and he is not built to catch her.
Suit: Red, blue, and white with a star across the chest. Not subtle. America never
prayed for subtle. The suit catches light in a way fabric shouldn’t because it’s not really
fabric — it’s belief made material. The entrance matters. You don’t just arrive. You
ARRIVE.
Double Life: Works in a government building. Sits through budget meetings. Loosens
his tie in a stairwell and goes through the roof. The civilian identity isn’t a disguise — it’s
a real job. He files expense reports. He reviews containment budgets. He sits in chairs
slightly too small for him. Everyone in the building probably knows what he is.
Combat vs. Sera (DC Incident): Punched her — hand shattered against her
cheekbone. Heat vision — stopped on contact, energy ceased to exist as relevant force.
Vacuum creation (emptied oxygen from a 20ft radius) — Sera finished her hot dog in the
vacuum. Her voice carried without a medium. Every escalation was his. She was static.
She was eating.
Key Sparring Detail: Caught Kusanagi bare-handed during the 2v2. His belief in his
own strength was strong enough to stop a four-century blade mid-strike. Blood ran from
his palm. The moment lasted one second — two belief systems in direct contest, neither
yielding. Then he threw Kiyuru across the room. First tactical action he’d taken in the
fight and it was the right one.
Personality: Boy Scout. Genuine. The hero who believes in protecting people because
that’s literally what he was made to do. His identity is built on being the protector.
Meeting Sera broke his framework because she radiates genocide-scale lethality while
giggling about frosting, and his prayer-lineage says protect people from things like that,
but the people around her don’t need protecting.
Relationship to the Girls: Ember reframed him. “You’re scared. She’s not going to hurt
you. Keep people safe by making sure nobody does anything stupid near her.” New job
description, better than the old one. He went from “fight the threat” to “manage the
perimeter so the threat never activates.” His broken hand and the story of the DC
incident will spread through the T4 network faster than any official report.


SECTION 4: EXECUTIVE REGISTRY

EXECUTIVE F
Region: Neo-America / Oak City Hub T4 Pairing: Cassian Ward + Veyren (dual pairing,
complementary) Profile: Five-eleven, lean, charcoal suit. Surrendered his personal
name for the job — “Executive F” is his identity now. Thirty years of institutional service.
Wrote the framework used to classify threats. Cataloged the first gate breach.
Institutional authority made visible — administrators go rigid when he enters, cadets
experience social vertigo clocking his face. Key Moment: After watching Soliera close
Gate Leviathan from his briefing room: “I’m feeling hopeful. For the first time. I think we
can not only survive — I think we can WIN.” Ember’s Assessment: Asked about his
name. Learned he surrendered it for the role. “That’s sad.”

ARC-STATE: #ARC-STATE-EXECUTIVE-F
- Mergeverse Arrival (baseline): Frictionless, sterile corporate efficiency. Resigned and satisfied in equal measure — surrendered his name to protect his family and has made peace with that math.
- The “That’s Sad” Epiphany: Completely stripped of corporate armor by Ember’s guileless pity. Falls into speechless silence because thirty years of institutional language has no defense against genuine divine empathy. The institution cannot file “that’s sad.”
- Leviathan Closure (M180): Worldview shatters cleanly. Eleven years of containment anxiety find a new shape — hope. Not managed hope. Not provisional hope. The real thing, first time.


EXECUTIVE J
Region: Japanese Hub T4 Pairing: Kiyuru Profile: Old-school Japanese administrator.
Physically diminished — hunched, the posture of decades at a desk. Mentally razor-
sharp. Steel-trap mind, institutional memory going back to the early gate days. His suit
is charcoal, two sizes too large in the shoulders because the shoulders were broader
once and the tailor retired and J saw no reason to find another. Glasses sit slightly too
far down his nose. Archetype: Yamamoto (Bleach) / Netero-Zeno (Hunter x Hunter).
The old man who looks like he should be retired but whose presence changes the math.
Key Quality: He WANTS a successor. Not holding the seat out of ego — out of
responsibility. Nobody can replace what’s in his head yet. Key Moment: Hearing F
describe the girls: “Win. I’ve never even thought about that before. I have managed. I
have contained. I have maintained. I have never once considered WINNING.”
Relationship to Kiyuru: Nineteen years. Entire conversations in a single glance. J reads
reports. Kiyuru reads trees. Both arrive at the same conclusions through completely
different systems.


SECTION 5: THE JAPANESE HUB #LOC-JAPAN
Location: A city grown from a historically significant shrine town (not necessarily
Tokyo). The shrines were load-bearing before the gates. After the gates they became
literal power sources.
Architecture: Early Meiji-era traditional (tatami, shoji, wooden post-and-beam)
blended with modern infrastructure. Shinjuku-density growing AROUND preserved
village cores rather than replacing them. The materials themselves carry intent from the
craftspeople who made them. You don’t replace that with drywall.
Spiritual Status: Japan (and China) already had deeply rooted spiritual traditions —
Shinto, Buddhism, kami worship, ancestor veneration. When gates opened and magic
became real, these cultures didn’t have the same crisis the West did. Their gods already
wandered. Kami inhabit rivers, mountains, intersections. Spirits are part of the
landscape. Japanese spirituality validated instantly.
Political Structure: Mirrors modern Japan — both a dynastic/shrine system (Emperor,
spiritual authority) and a diplomatic government (institutional, globalized). Both are
necessary. One person can’t run everything when interfacing with 200 countries.
Relationship to Company/Church: Cooperates with the Company but is NOT under it.
Not under the Church at all — monotheistic “believe or die” framework doesn’t map onto
a culture that was already polytheistic and animist. The Company cooperates with Japan
the way a corporation cooperates with a country that has nuclear weapons — politely,
carefully, with the understanding that cooperation is voluntary.
Cultural Response to the Girls: Japan has a CATEGORY for divine visitation. Kami
descend, do things, leave, come back. The girls’ behavior pattern (arrive, claim territory,
leave, return) maps onto established mythological patterns. The shrine system probably
incorporated them before the Company finished its first briefing. Some local priest
wrote a prayer. Some grandmother left an offering at the gate. The institutional
response lagged behind the spiritual one.
