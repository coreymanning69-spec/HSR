# M146 — OAK CITY COMMAND OBSERVER FRAME

In Oak City, the screens were running.

Cassian and Veyren had been at the display wall for hours — not because anything required it, but because neither of them had found a better reason to stop. The feeds showed the slum grid in real time: Grid 7, the alley, the feast assembling itself out of Sanctum abundance and slum need. Tehilum sitting at a table in a circle of people who had never talked to him before. The infant's father on his knees with his daughter hanging from his neck. Ember moving between tables, touching everything, asking about everything.

Niv stood behind them.

He had not sat down in several hours. He watched the feeds the way he watched everything: with the complete, unhurried attention of something that was simultaneously cataloging socio-political implications, metaphysical ramifications, and cultural resonance and finding all of it, on balance, precisely what he had expected. He had absorbed every piece of modern technology in the command post within a week of arriving and had been operating all of it simultaneously since. The screens were just another instrument.

"The ambient effect has extended," he said, not to either of them specifically. "The slum grid's baseline infrastructure is improving passively. The electrical load just dropped by eleven percent in the northeast quarter. Water pressure in Grid 9 normalized four minutes ago."

Cassian looked at the feeds showing Soliera sitting on the outer steps of Risa's building, at the quiet edge of the noise.

"She didn't do anything," he said. "She sat down."

"Yes," Niv said.

Outside the command post's front window, the mile-long Sanctum convoy that had been marching since morning was visible as a line of organized light heading south toward Neo-Moscow — constructs and stewards in formation, supply wagons, civic teams, all of it moving with the unhurried purpose of infrastructure that knows where it is needed.

Executive F had been outside watching it for the better part of an hour. He came back in carrying a cup of something and stood beside Cassian at the screens.

"They're going to ask me what to do about all this," he said.

"What are you going to tell them?" Cassian asked.

Executive F looked at the feed showing Ember explaining something to a child using hand gestures that were slightly too large for the point she was making.

"I'm going to tell them the city is getting better," he said. "And that we should let it."

M148 — NIV AND F ARRIVE

Niv and Executive F arrived later, when the torches the Sanctum crews had set along Alley 14 had burned down to a steadier, quieter light and the feast had transitioned from the loud phase to the long phase — the kind of evening where people stayed because it felt good to stay.

Niv came in on foot, which surprised everyone who didn't know him and surprised no one who did. He moved through the alley the way he moved through most spaces: with the exact amount of presence he chose to bring, which was usually just enough to be seen and no more. He found Soliera's position first, acknowledged Her with the specific nod of someone checking in with a sovereign, and then found Sera's windowsill and stood beneath it looking up at her.

"Good day?" he asked.

"Excellent day," she said. She offered him a piece of bread from the plate beside her. He took it. This was the entirety of what needed to be said between them.

Executive F arrived a half-hour later, still in his work clothes, still carrying the look of a man who had spent the day receiving reports about things he had already known were happening and composing responses that acknowledged the situation without promising anything specific. He stood at the edge of the alley for a moment, taking it in — the torches, the tables, the wiring overhead running straight for the first time, the sound of several hundred people in a place that had not had this much food or this much light or this much reason for both in longer than most of them could clearly remember.

He looked at Amara, who was sitting nearby.

"Is it always like this?" he asked.

Amara considered the question seriously. "Sort of," she said. "But every place is different."

He nodded, as if this were useful information, and went to find something to eat.

# M149 — THE WANDERING

The next day the girls wandered.

This was not a plan. It was what happened when Ember had eaten well and slept and had a new city in front of her: she wanted to see it. She wanted to see the tall buildings — the ones she'd glimpsed from the wall gap, the ones that rose out of the inner city into the low-hanging haze above, stacked with lights and glass and the particular arrogance of architecture that has been told it should be impressive and has tried hard. She wanted to see the different faces. The different shapes of people. The goblins doing business beside humans doing business, the half-forms and the quarter-forms and the beings who defied easy categorization moving through the same streets with the specific choreography of a city that had figured out, through grudging necessity, how to share.

Sera walked beside her and pointed at things and named them incorrectly and then corrected herself and then pointed at other things. They ate from a stall on a corner where a woman was making something flat and crispy in a pan of oil and Ember declared it the best thing she'd ever eaten, which she said about most things and meant completely every time.

Soliera walked behind them.

Various people joined and departed the procession throughout the day: Risa for a while, navigating her city with the quiet pride of someone who has had a reason to look at it again. Amara for most of it, keeping pace at Ember's side. Niv for a stretch in the early afternoon, matching Soliera's pace behind the others with the comfortable silence of two beings who have known each other long enough to not require conversation to be present together.

The city was beginning to understand what had happened to it. Not all at once — cities don't understand things all at once, they understand them the way stone understands water, slowly, through accumulation. But in the places the girls had been, things were different. The wiring was quiet. The walls were cleaner. People who had woken up yesterday with particular burdens found those burdens absent in ways they couldn't explain and were not going to stop to question.

And in the corporate towers above the city, in the rooms where people had screens and reports and the particular anxiety of institutional power watching something it does not control, executives were beginning to have a different set of conversations. Not about threat containment. About what you do when something has walked into your city and improved it before you had a chance to object.

Executive F was in three of those conversations. He was the most useful person in all of them, because he was the only one who had stood in the alley.

It was Ember who said it.

She said it the way she said most things — without lead-up, without consultation, as a simple statement of current preference that she expected the world to accommodate because in her experience it usually did.

# M150 — I WANT TO GO HOME / THE GOODBYES

"I want to go home."

They were on a wide street in the inner city, looking up at one of the tall buildings she'd wanted to see. It had delivered. She had looked at it thoroughly. She was ready to be somewhere else.

Sera, at her side, made a small sound that meant: yes, same. She had had a good day. She had eaten well. She had her city, her Ember, her Soliera walking behind them. She was ready to be back in the Sanctum, in the quiet of her own space, doing the warm and unhurried nothing that was her natural state when the world was not requiring her to be anything specific.

The goodbyes were warm and real and not very long.

Tehilum was there — he had been nearby for most of the day, not intruding, just present in the way of someone who has decided that wherever these beings go, being somewhere in the vicinity is a reasonable life decision. He bowed his head and held his arm out to Ember one final time, and she patted it with both hands, thoroughly, and told him she would come back, and meant it.

Risa stood at the corner of Alley 14, her home behind her, the new straightness of the electrical poles overhead throwing clean light down onto the street. She had been given a status she didn't fully understand yet. She would be learning what it meant for a while. She accepted the not-knowing with the same composure she'd accepted the terror of walking through the wall gap: because the alternative was not being here, and being here was better.

Sera waved.

It was a cheerful wave — loose-wristed, broad, entirely like her. The wave of someone who is not sad to be leaving because leaving is not loss when you can always come back, and the Sanctum door was never permanently closed to anyone she had decided belonged near it.

"Bye~" she said, to everyone, to the alley, to the city.

They walked away, and the alley watched them go.

The Sanctum did not leave.

Its personnel were already deep in Grid 7 and spreading: civic crews with the drainage project, supply teams setting up distribution points, medical stewards moving through the alley grid asking who needed what and logging the answers. By nightfall they were in Grid 9. By the following morning they were in Grid 11. By the end of the week, the electrical grid in the northeast quarter had been completely overhauled by a team that had brought the right tools and the specific expertise that comes from having done this in enough places to have stopped making the same mistakes.

Cassian took the inter-agency meetings. Veyren managed the security coordination. Hale and Ysbet, working from the Church presence that had been established in Oak City and extending it, began the longer and more careful work of integration: talking to people, listening to people, figuring out what the neighborhood needed from the Church that the Church was actually capable of providing, and letting go of the things it had been providing badly for years.

It was not fast work. It was not dramatic work. But it was the kind of work that, done correctly, left a place different in five years than it would have been otherwise — not because anything miraculous had been mandated, but because the right infrastructure had been quietly, persistently laid.

The girls returned to the Divineverse through the way they came — back through the Merge, through the crossing the Sanctum held open for them, the distance between worlds folding the same way it always did when they chose to move.

Ember went home satisfied. Sera went home ready to exist without clothing for an unspecified period. Soliera came home the way She always came home — having been entirely wherever She was, and now being entirely here.

Behind them, Neo-Moscow kept going.

The gap in the wall remained. Somebody would eventually have to decide what to do about it officially. For now, the Sanctum crews had built a gate into it — functional, clean, properly hinged — and it stood open during the day and was guarded by people who understood who it was for.

Anyone arriving on foot, in bare feet, from the direction of the badlands, was to be admitted without question.
