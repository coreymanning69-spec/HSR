M91 — OAK CITY DEPARTURE

The Sanctum was woven into Oak City now. That was the only way to describe it — not planted, not stationed, but woven, the way a thread goes into fabric and becomes part of the structure rather than a thing resting on top of it. By the morning the girls left, there were Sanctum stewards who knew the names of the market vendors. There were constructs that had been assigned, semi-permanently, to the drainage repair project with the retired engineer. There were three cooks whose presence at the central market had become, in the space of a few days, something the city would have noticed the absence of.

Veyren stayed. Niv stayed, in the way Niv stayed anywhere — meaning he was available through the multi-screen arrays in the command post and would be present when needed, which was most of the time. They would move between cities going forward, Oak City and wherever the girls landed next, and Bucky would keep the lanes clear in the middle.

The girls left in the morning with no particular ceremony, and the city let them go with the particular warmth of a place that knows the people leaving will come back.


M93 — THE BADLANDS PATH

The badlands path opened up beyond the northern edge of the city grid. It was not a road in any maintained sense — broken blacktop giving way to ripped earth, old highway fragments half-swallowed by scrub, places where things had walked close enough and recently enough that the ground was still settling back into itself. The sky out here was big and pale and the wind moved through the tall, dry grass in long, unhurried waves.

Sera walked with her hands laced behind her head. Ember walked beside Amara, a step and a half ahead the way she always did when something was interesting, which in the badlands meant: most things. Soliera walked at the center of it all, bare feet finding the dry ground with the same unhurried exactness She brought to every surface.

They did not encounter anything meaningful.

This was not an accident. Monsters have instincts that predate language, and the badlands' inhabitants had developed, through accumulated generations of trial and catastrophic error, a very specific set of responses to certain kinds of presence. The presence currently moving through their territory registered at a level that bypassed threat-assessment entirely and went straight to the part of the nervous system that governs: do not. Goblin packs that had been running the same corridor for months shifted their circuit without deciding to. Larger shapes, further out, simply faced away and held still until the sensation passed.

The journey took one day. It was uneventful by design.


M96 — EMBER SEES THE LIGHTS

It was Ember who saw the lights first — not because she was looking, but because the horizon had changed and she noticed changes before other people had finished deciding whether to bother.

"What's that?" she asked.

She was pointing at a glow: distant, low, and wrong-colored for sunset. Orange and blue and the hard white of things that did not turn off at night.

Amara followed her finger and looked for a moment.

"That's a city," she said.

Ember considered this. "Bigger than Oak City?"

"Much bigger."

Ember looked at the glow. The glow did not change. It simply sat there on the horizon, contained and massive, the kind of light that meant two million people had decided this was where they lived and had spent decades building something around that decision.

"Okay," Ember said, and started walking toward it.

The others followed. Soliera's eyes found the horizon for a long moment, taking in the glow the way She took in everything — not with urgency, not with analysis, but with the particular attention of someone deciding whether something is interesting. Then She walked.


M101–M107: THE HILL

From the low hills outside the city, the four of them watched the highway breathe.

Headlights and taillights rolled in chains along the asphalt, a glowing river cutting through trees and strip malls. The wind up here was dry and warm, tugging at Sera's silk and Ember's hair, carrying up the faint stink of exhaust and hot rubber. Sodium lamps on tall poles threw yellow light down the lanes in long pools, each one stretching and shrinking as vehicles moved through them.

The city didn't look like a cluster of buildings. It looked like a single, contained organism — two million people sealed behind a ring of stone, steel, and humming wards.

A wall circled it all. Not quaint battlements. A sheer, brutal curtain of reinforced concrete and obsidian-black alloy, forty meters high, topped with repeating teeth of turret and rune. Neon traced its edges — soft blues and toxic pinks — turning the whole perimeter into a glowing crown in the dark.

Outside the wall was the badlands they had just walked through. Inside it, the city burned.

There were only three ways in. You could see them from the hill: the North Gate Causeway, a raised eight-lane bridge ramming straight into the wall, flanked by watchtowers and suspension pylons laced with glowing sigils, where the bulk traffic flowed. The East Cargo Lift, squared-off and industrial, stacked with cranes and container racks, Company colors on everything. The South Rail Spine, a mag-rail line running arrow-straight to a smaller, sharper gate, fast and quiet and never pedestrian.

Everything else: solid wall.

Sixteen highways spidered out from the city like spokes from a wheel, each one eventually joining a gate or a cargo corridor or the rail. The girls stood above one of the feeder roads that led to the North Gate Causeway — old-world highway, patched and warded until it almost believed it was new again. Floodlights on long poles washed the approach in white and sodium yellow. Lines of vehicles crawled along the asphalt: battered trucks with welded plates, Church caravans marked in holy sigils, Company transports with matte-black sides.

From the hills, it looked like veins returning blood to a heart that refused to let anything in without a blood test and a background check.

Amara stood at the edge of the hill and looked at it.


M102 — AMARA'S UNEASE

She wasn't afraid. That was the thing she had learned over the last several days, somewhere between watching Sera catch a rifle round and watching Soliera raise a hand and expand a city lot: fear, for herself, had stopped applying. Whatever happened to this city — whatever happened to anyone near the girls — it would not happen to her, and she had made her peace with that.

What she was, instead, was quietly, precisely uneasy.

The danger in this situation flowed outward, not inward. Somewhere in that wall, behind those gun emplacements, inside those watchtowers, were people whose entire professional lives had been organized around the project of keeping things out. Dangerous things. Unknown things. Things that came from outside the wall and did not have appointments. Those people had protocols. They had weapons. They had the institutional certainty that their job was correct and their methods were sufficient.

They were about to meet four women on bare feet who had walked in from the badlands at night.

Amara thought about all of this and kept it behind her eyes.


M103 — HIGHWAY QUESTIONS

Beside her, Ember squinted down at the road, then up at Amara. The glare from the lamps made her eyes narrow into sharp gold slits.

"Mara, what's the wall around that road up there? Why are they so bright?"

"That's a highway," Amara said, leaning down just enough that her words were meant for Ember first, the world second. "The wall is just to keep the cars from smashing into each other. The lights tell them when to stop and go."

Ember wiggled an obsidian toe into the soft, dry ground at the edge of the hill. "Why don't they just walk? We walked."

"Because," Sera said, already grinning, "they're even slower than the things on the path."

She tilted her head back to look at Soliera, grin spreading wider. "Do you think we should walk down the path with the lights, or just make our own?"

For a heartbeat, nothing moved.

Then a breeze rolled up the hill from the highway, bending the grass around their ankles. On the road below, the lights at the next intersection flicked to red a fraction too early, holding the cars in a neat, obedient line — as if the city were pausing to hear the answer.

"Let's go down!" Ember announced, looking at the steep hill face as though it were a gentle suggestion.

It was basically a cliff. Loose dirt, jagged scrub, patches where rock showed through. Amara looked at the drop, then at Ember, then at Sera.

"Ember... Sera... how would I get down?"

Sera peered over the edge, then turned back to Amara with a broad, toothy grin. "I'll carry you! We can just jump~"

Amara went a shade paler.

Ember laughed, delighted. "Oh, so we can jump? I was going to roll!"

Soliera watched them, eyes on Her girls rather than the city. Her lips curved — not a mortal grin, just the soft, private shift of someone who already knows the outcome and enjoys seeing it anyway.


M104 — THE ROLL

Ember gave a sharp, happy yip and took off toward the edge. Not a sprint of desperation — just a happy, eager run, bare feet kicking up little ghosts of dust. She dropped into a roll at the lip, arms tucked, obsidian skin shrugging off rocks and dry grass like it was nothing. Dust kicked up in her wake as she pinballed between scrub and stone, laughing the whole way down.

Amara stood there watching until Sera touched her shoulder.

"Alright," Sera said, eyes glittering, "how high do you want to go?"

Amara chuckled, thin and nervous. "How — how high?"

"Yeah, yeah, yeah. Watch this."


M105 — STEEL BRACES / THE LEAP

She set her staff down against the hillside without being asked, stepped over, and climbed onto Sera's back. Sera hooked one arm under her legs and another across her back, and to Amara it didn't feel like arms at all — it felt like steel braces locking into place. No give. No slip. Just held.

"Well, at—"

She never finished the sentence.

Sera bounded off the edge, legs coiling and releasing in one impossible motion. The ground where they'd been standing coughed out a small dust crater, grass tearing under the force, and then they were airborne — two silhouettes thrown up against the neon crown of the city and the pale, washed-out moon.

For a breath, Amara screamed — pure, reflexive, mortal fear as the world dropped away.

Halfway through, it bent into something else. The scream turned ragged, then broke into a wild, breathless laugh as she realized she wasn't falling, she was being carried through the air by something that did not recognize gravity as a rule.

They came down hard. Or rather: the world came up hard to meet them. Sera hit the base of the hill like a meteor and simply ate the impact, all the shock vanishing into her compact, unbothered frame. Dirt cracked, pebbles jumped, but her knees didn't bend so much as a token centimeter.

She slid Amara off her back as if she weighed nothing.

Amara staggered one step, caught herself, bowing forward slightly with her hands on her knees, heart pounding. She straightened, cheeks flushed, and grinned despite herself.

"Thanks, Sera. That was… awesome."

Ember had completed her very serious roll all the way down, tumbling and bouncing and coming to lazy spins. She pushed herself upright, patting at a fresh tear in her outfit and frowning at it.

"These rocks are very annoying when they hit me," she declared, as if the terrain had personally insulted her.

Then the air beside them seemed to collect.

Soliera's last steps of descent carried Her down on nothing: each bare footfall landing on invisible solidity, the space beneath Her thickened just long enough to hold Her weight before relaxing back into air. She stepped off the final invisible stair and came to a calm halt beside the others.

All four of them stood at the base of the hill, dust settling around their ankles, looking out at the wall and the wash of neon that crowned the city.


M108: THE SEVENTEEN HOURS

The wall filled half the main screen in Oak City command — neon halo, gun emplacements, traffic streams reduced to data lines and pulsing nodes. Auxiliary feeds showed heat signatures on the badlands, threat icons ticking up and down in the margins.

Niv sat in the center of it, draconic eyes not quite focused on any one display. Half his work happened in the spaces between.

Footsteps approached. Cassian didn't bother with a polite cough. He just came up beside the chair and watched the scrolling data for a few breaths. Veyren was already in the room, leaning against the far wall with his arms folded, watching the same feeds in silence the way he watched most things — like a man cataloguing outcomes he's already decided he can live with.

"Do you always track them?" he asked. "Do you always keep an eye on them?"

Niv's jaw worked once, a tiny sideways motion. He didn't look away from the wall.

"No," he said. "I lost them once."

Cassian's brows lifted. Executive F, leaning against a nearby console with arms folded, turned his head a fraction, suddenly more interested.

"For about seventeen hours," Niv continued. "When I found them, they were here."

Cassian snorted softly. "Oh. Well. That tracks."

Executive F actually laughed — short, incredulous, the kind that comes out when you're too tired to scream.

"So let me get this straight," F said. "You followed them across universes, and it took you seventeen hours?"

Niv turned then, just his head, eyes settling on F's face with the patient weight of an ancient predator tolerating a clever mammal.

"Yes," he said. "I thought we were moving fairly slow."

Executive F held the look for a moment, then turned back to his screen without a word. The advance warnings were already out — had been since the girls left Oak City's northern edge. He'd sent them up through every official channel he could access: cease orders, incident briefings, flagged priority lines to Neo-Moscow's command infrastructure. Whatever the city's response protocols were, someone in that chain needed to know: do not fire.

He had not expected it to be enough. He had sent it anyway.

Up close, the city looked worse.

The asphalt of the feeder road was scarred and oily, guardrails tagged and bent, neon strips along the edge flickering unevenly. The whole thing stank of exhaust, hot metal, and old fear — years of things trying to get in and things trying to get out compressed into the surface of the road.

Sera wrinkled her nose. "Soli, look at this place. It's nasty."

Soliera looked where Sera was looking, then looked at Sera instead, as if the road weren't worth much attention. Sera took the non-reaction as full permission and turned back to the wall.

"Well," she said, "I guess we'll just have to make our own path."

Ember brightened immediately. "Yeah! I love having my own path." She squinted toward the city, scanning the wall. Near the crown, a section of reinforced plating glowed brighter than the rest — a cluster of glyphs and hard neon arranged around a thicker segment of barrier, like a plugged wound in the city's skin. Around it, vertical pylons and tower struts bristled: antennae, ward-spikes, gun mounts.

"Well, that's a really, really glowy thing," Ember said, pointing. "It almost looks like a rune I recognize. And those sticks look kind of fun too."

Sera followed her finger, saw the cluster, and grinned. "Yeah. Those sticks do look kind of fun."

Soliera's mouth twitched — the faintest smile — as She glanced between the wall and Her girls, almost shaking Her head without doing it.

Amara stood there taking it all in. She already knew. Of course they weren't going to queue at a gate. They were going to walk straight into a two-million-person city through the wall.

Ember took off toward it — not a sprint of desperation, just a happy, eager run, bare feet kicking up little ghosts of dust.

The wall responded.

One by one, the long horizontal strips of light snapped from standby amber to hard white, rippling in clean rows around the curve of the city. Floodlights the size of wagons hummed and warmed, lenses rotating, shutters opening. Sera moved to walk beside Soliera, both hands laced behind her head, idly kicking at small stones. Her dark blue silk swung around her thighs, catching the neon in deep, oil-slick highlights.


M110 — FLOODLIGHTS WAKE

The floodlights fully woke.

Beams swept out from the wall in overlapping fans, carving the badlands into stark blacks and whites. Ember slowed, then stopped altogether as one beam passed over her, then back.

"Eww!" She threw an arm up in front of her face, eyes narrowing to slits, gold irises catching the light. "That's way too bright. What are they looking for?"
