### LOCATION 03: MIRABAR
**[MAP POSITION]** Southeast of the Sanctum, due south along the primary construct road - approximately 4 days from Bryn Shander by road, faster from the Sanctum directly.
**[STATUS]** FIRST VISIT - the engineer already here (parallel thread)
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** Paved Sanctum road arrives at Mirabar's north gate. No teleport node - Mirabar's Council of Sparkling Stones refused the embassy treaty three times, citing "jurisdictional sovereignty." A Sanctum waystation (not a full embassy) is positioned two miles outside the city walls.
**[WORD OF MOUTH]** Regional, approaching direct. Mirabar is commercially savvy - it has profited enormously from Sanctum road traffic passing through its territory and maintains an informed version of the trio. Its merchant council is simultaneously grateful, resentful, and calculating.

**Setting Changes (20+)**
1. The Sanctum road that arrives at Mirabar's north gate was built without Mirabar's permission. The Council of Sparkling Stones protested. A Sanctum Steward responded that the road runs on the mountain range, not in Mirabar's jurisdiction, and the gate end of the road sits on undeveloped land that Mirabar has no legal claim to. The Council accepted this. They privately understood they had been outmaneuvered.
2. The road has increased Mirabar's trade volume by approximately 40%. Goods that used to require six days overland now arrive in two. The merchants who initially protested are now the road's loudest supporters within the council.
3. A Sanctum waystation sits two miles north of Mirabar. It has a construct garrison, a supply depot, and a visiting Steward on rotation. The waystation's teleport circle is not officially tied to the Mirabar node (because there is no Mirabar node), but for practical purposes, anyone arriving via waystation is in Mirabar within an hour's walk.
4. THE ENGINEER IS HERE. Operating under a Sanctum logistics contract, the unnamed engineer has spent three months engineering a mass-nutrition production facility in Mirabar's industrial undercity. She is working with the dwarven mining council to repurpose a decommissioned ore-processing chamber into a large-scale food production operation. The project is technically voluntary collaboration. In practice, the engineer showed up, identified a space, explained what she was going to do in it, and started. The dwarves have been watching with a mixture of fascination and professional respect.
5. The engineer's food production system (which she calls "the kitchen" in a tone that suggests she knows this is a significant understatement) is designed to process raw bulk ingredients - grain, mountain herbs, dried protein, imported fish - into standardized nutritional blocks at industrial scale. The blocks are approximately the size of a brick, mildly flavored, and contain sufficient nutrition for one adult human for a full day. They are not delicious. They are extraordinarily efficient.
6. The dwarves who have worked alongside the engineer have started calling her "the little engineer." She has not corrected them. She considers "engineer" a correct title and "little" not worth the energy to dispute.
7. Mirabar's official position on the Sanctum is "respectful non-membership." They trade with Sanctum-adjacent merchants, use the roads, and tolerate the waystation. They do not display the blackbird symbol, do not accept Sanctum administrative oversight, and have their own guards patrol within city limits. This is the most politically complex relationship the Sanctum has with any city it has not transformed.
8. Mirabar's underground city (the true dwarven settlement, beneath the surface human quarter) is older and larger than most outsiders realize. The surface city is the commercial face. The undercity is where the actual power resides - in the Council of Sparkling Stones' chambers, several hundred feet below street level.
9. Mirabar is currently experiencing an internal political debate about whether to formally request Sanctum embassy status. The "for" faction (primarily younger merchants and human administrators) argues that Sanctum alignment would accelerate their trade position significantly. The "against" faction (primarily the dwarven old guard and two of the five High Captains equivalent - the Marchion's council) argues that alignment is dependency and dependency is weakness.
10. A rumor has spread in Mirabar that the Sanctum has found a gem vein in the Spine of the World that rivals anything Mirabar has ever extracted. This is true. The Sanctum has not publicized this, but the information leaked through trade networks. The Council of Sparkling Stones is monitoring the situation carefully.
11. Mirabar's primary industry (deep-ore mining: iron, gold, gems) has been indirectly affected by Sanctum construct mining. Construct operations do not directly compete - they mine in different areas - but the sheer efficiency of construct labor has introduced a "what if" pressure that makes Mirabar's traditional labor-intensive methods feel increasingly outdated to the younger generation.
12. The Arcane Brotherhood (based in Luskan's Host Tower) has historically had trade and intelligence interests in Mirabar. Since the Sanctum road arrived, Brotherhood agents in Mirabar have become more active - specifically monitoring what goods move between Mirabar and Sanctum-affiliated merchants. Velin is aware. The engineer does not care and has said so.
13. Mirabar produces the finest cold-forge ironwork in the North. The technique involves metalworking in artificially cooled chambers (natural mountain cold, no magic) that produce a specific grain structure in the metal. Sanctum smiths have studied the technique with permission. The collaboration has been productive and created a small but genuine cultural bridge.
14. The city's human quarter has a street market that runs six days a week. It is loud, crowded, and commercially ruthless - price negotiation is expected and refusing to negotiate is considered insulting. Sanctum-standard goods (recognizable by their uniform quality) have appeared in this market and command premium prices.
15. Mirabar's garrison - the Axe of Mirabar - is one of the most professional military forces in the North. They have observed Sanctum constructs on the road and have quietly updated their tactical assessments to "do not engage under any circumstances."
16. A dwarven engineer named Brunndar Copperlock has been the unnamed engineer's primary liaison in the undercity project. He started the collaboration as a skeptic and is now so invested in the kitchen project that he has reorganized his entire work schedule around it. He has not told anyone he finds the whole thing remarkable. His wife has told everyone.
17. Mirabar's food culture is specific: heavily preserved meats, dense breads, root vegetables, strong ale. The concept of mass-produced nutritional blocks is culturally alien to them. The engineer's kitchen is not changing Mirabar's food culture - it is creating a parallel export product that will leave Mirabar through Sanctum distribution channels. This distinction has been carefully explained to the Council of Sparkling Stones.
18. The city maintains a dedicated messenger service using trained raven handlers (called the Raven Post). The post has been quietly replaced by Sanctum construct messenger relays on the inter-city routes, but the local intra-city ravens remain. The raven handlers resent the constructs. The ravens are indifferent.
19. Mirabar's wall - a series of concentric stone fortifications designed for siege resilience - was built to withstand the kind of orc armies that used to sweep down from the Spine. No such army has attempted Mirabar since the Sanctum's presence became known in the region. The walls are maintained anyway because Mirabar is the kind of city that maintains its walls.
20. Three of Mirabar's prominent merchant families have quietly relocated extended family members to Sanctum embassies in Waterdeep and Bryn Shander - not as immigrants, but as "commercial liaisons." This is the Council's hedge: keep official distance, maintain private connection.
21. The Marchion (ruler of Mirabar, political appointee) is privately favorable to Sanctum relations but publicly neutral. He is managing a coalition government and cannot afford to alienate the dwarven old guard. He has met with Velin twice, both times in locations that are not on any official record.
22. The engineer's kitchen, when complete, will produce enough nutritional output to supplement the food supply of a city three times Mirabar's size. This was in the design spec she submitted to Brunndar. He did not ask why she designed it at that scale. She would have told him: because eventually the project moves.

**City Flavor (Street Level - Surface Quarter)**
The surface city of Mirabar sits on a stone plateau above the mines. The streets are wide and practical - built for ore wagons, not people. The buildings are heavy stone, built to last centuries, not to impress. The air smells of coal smoke, hot metal, and something almost savory from the deep ore refinery vents. Sound travels strangely - the deep hum of mining operations below fills the background constantly, a subsonic vibration you feel more than hear. Market stalls are tightly packed and the vendors are the best negotiators in the North. Children running errands between merchants move with practiced efficiency - nobody in Mirabar has time to be charming.

**Embassy Status**
WAYSTATION ONLY - 2 miles north of city. One Steward, four constructs. No teleport circle. Velin considers this the most frustrating outstanding diplomatic situation in her portfolio and brings it up in quarterly reviews.

**Hooks & Quests (`TR-04` through `TR-07`)**
- **TR-04:** The "for embassy" faction in Mirabar's council has sent an unofficial message to the waystation Steward: if the girls visit Mirabar in person, and if their visit is publicly visible and demonstrably peaceful, the faction believes they can flip two of the council votes. They are not asking the Sanctum to stage anything. They are informing them of the political math.
- **TR-05:** The engineer has found something in the undercity chamber she is working in. She has not told the dwarves yet. She sent a one-line message to Niv: "There is something in the sub-basement that I need Deashi to look at before I continue. It is very old and it is not ore." Niv has not passed this to the girls yet.
- **TR-06:** A Zhentarim agent posing as a merchant has been photographing (via Arcane Eye spells) every construct that passes through the Mirabar waystation. They are building an intelligence file on construct capabilities. The Steward has noticed but lacks authority to act. Velin needs to make a call.
- **TR-07:** Brunndar Copperlock quietly requests a meeting with whoever is in charge, specifically about a question of ethics: the kitchen the engineer is building will eventually replace the jobs of approximately four hundred Mirabar citizens who currently work in manual food processing. He does not want to stop the project. He wants to know what the Sanctum's plan is for those people.

**Parallel Threads**
- **The engineer:** In the undercity. Focused. Not available for casual conversation. Will surface for the girls' visit and will give Soliera a full engineering briefing that lasts forty minutes and involves blueprints.
- **Velin:** Has briefed the Marchion via back-channel. Her note to the Steward: "Make the visit look spontaneous. It is not. That's fine. Just don't make it look planned."
- **Niv:** Is managing the logistics of routing three supply convoys through the Mirabar waystation while also fielding the engineer's message about the sub-basement. He has not slept in thirty hours. He is a dragon. This is fine.

**DM Notes**
Mirabar is a friction point - not hostile, but not comfortable the way Ten-Towns is. The girls are respected here but not beloved. Their presence is complicated by politics rather than welcomed by it. This is good tension for the arc: the first city where the girls encounter "we appreciate you, but this is complicated." The engineer's sub-basement discovery can be a session hook or a slow-burn background thread. The ethics question from Brunndar is the most interesting NPC beat here - it is the kind of question that has no clean answer and where Ember's contributions will make everyone uncomfortable in productive ways.
