## BADLANDS TO BREACH — PART 3: EMBER ENTERS GRID 7

The northeast feeder narrowed quickly.

The main drag had been wide and bright and corporate — clean storefronts and managed signage and the particular aesthetic of a city district that has been told to look orderly and has complied. The feeder street going northeast was none of those things. The buildings closed in. The signage got personal. Handmade shop fronts, wiring that ran in improvised loops between windows, the smell of cooking smoke and close-packed life. Children appeared briefly in upper windows and disappeared again.

The city's slum grid, Grid 7 and beyond, began about four hundred meters from the corner.

Ember was already ahead.

M123 — EMBER AND THE ELECTRICAL BOX

She had found, at approximately the first junction, an electrical box mounted to a wall — gray metal, slightly dented, covered in stickers, humming faintly. She stopped in front of it with the focused attention she usually reserved for things that were genuinely puzzling.

"What is this?" she asked no one in particular.

"An electrical junction box," Amara said, catching up to her.

Ember touched the side of it gently with two fingers. Felt the faint vibration of current. Her eyes lit up. "It hums," she said, delighted.

"Yes."

"It hums in a pattern."

"It does."

Ember pressed her whole palm against it, feeling the vibration change against different parts of her hand. "What is it for?"

Amara explained, briefly, the concept of managed electrical distribution. Ember listened to the entire explanation without moving her hand. Then she removed it, nodded once, and walked to the next junction, where she immediately found a different electrical box and repeated the examination.

Soliera walked through all of this with an even pace and an expression of mild, observational interest — not in the boxes, not in the wiring, not in the city infrastructure, but in Ember discovering it. The slums, when they properly began, registered to Her as continuous with everything else. Another texture of the world. She observed it the way She observed the highway, the way She observed the badlands — with the attention of someone who has seen all kinds of places and finds the variety genuinely pleasant.

Risa walked slightly ahead, guiding without performing, leading them along the path she knew: through the first junction, past the corner market, into the first long alley of Grid 7. She could hear, behind her, the sounds of a neighborhood realizing something had happened to the street it was living above.

On the balconies above the alley, faces appeared.

On the walls, small watchers went very still.

In the deeper alleys, message runners who had been moving for other reasons found new purposes and adjusted their routes.

The city was learning that something had turned off the main drag, and the news was moving faster than they were.

Ember paused at a junction where a string of handmade lights had been hung between two buildings — cheap incandescent bulbs in colored glass, strung on improvised wire, casting circles of yellow and red on the alley walls.

She stood under them and looked up.

"Amara," she said, very softly. "These are nice."

Amara looked up at the lights. They were, in the particular way of things made by hand out of insufficient materials by people who wanted their street to look like something, genuinely nice.

"Yeah," she said. "They are."

Soliera paused beside them, looked up at the lights, and then looked further down the alley where the lights continued, strung between buildings for another two blocks in the same improvised, determined way.

She didn't say anything.

She walked on, and the alley kept going, and the slums opened up ahead.
