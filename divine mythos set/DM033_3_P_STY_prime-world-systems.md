## XII. WORLD SYSTEMS

### The Tier System (PROM Variant)

The Tier System is the dominant framework used by the Company, the Church, and most independent military orders to classify operative and entity threat levels. It is pragmatic, not metaphysical — a tool for logistics and engagement planning rather than a cosmological truth.

| Tier | Classification | Examples |
|------|----------------|---------|
| 1 | Baseline civilians | Evan, general population |
| 2 | Elite soldiers, minor casters | Standard Company operatives |
| 3 | Unique operatives, high-tier casters | Veris, early-arc Cassian |
| 4 | Narrative battlefield dominators | Varn, Veyren, Cassian (late arc) |
| 5 | Singular, time-free entity | Arden, divine-level entities |
| — | Outside classification | Soliera Prime |

Soliera Prime exists outside this system entirely. This is not a polite fiction — it is a practical acknowledgment that the Tier System models *combat* and *threat*, and Soliera does not operate through those vectors. Arden overrides the system as a function, not a Tier. Applying a Tier number to him has been attempted. The results were not useful.


### Intent-Based Reality

The world shapes itself subtly around Soliera's presence. This is not commanded magic. She does not cast. She does not invoke. Reality *cooperates* with her the way a well-worn path cooperates with someone walking it. Doors open when her attention moves toward them. Animals settle when she watches them. The best possible version of a space she occupies begins to assert itself alongside the actual space.

This effect is cumulative and quiet. It does not announce itself. The people around her simply notice, over time, that things work out — that the soil near Cradle was unusually fertile, that weather events seemed to detour, that equipment malfunctions resolved themselves. They attribute it to luck, or her competence, or the general improvement that comes from someone pleasant being present.

**The Room Rule:** She walks through a place and the best possible version of that place becomes real beside the actual version. It doesn't replace what's there. It *insists* alongside it.

**The Closet:** Infinite, context-aware. Appropriate clothes appear because belonging is a function of her reality field, not wardrobe planning. She extended this function to Veris's room. It now works there too.

**The Rule of Three / Third Attempt Rule:** On the third attempt at something, reality adjusts to make it work. This functions even when she doesn't know she's attempting something. "Third Attempt Rule" is recovered archaeology terminology; "Rule of Three" remains the live-canon label.


### The Modular Magic and Item System

Magic in this world is modular — tagged, catalogued, and applied through a framework that resembles enchantment as database logic.

**Item Tagging:** Every artifact, weapon, or enchanted object carries a modifier string. Example:

> `Cursed Steel Dagger [+Poisoned Edge][x2 Bleed Mod][Unstable Wound Roll]`
> `Iron Fang [+Bleed][+1 Shock Resistance]`
> `Warded Cloak [Echo Tag][Minor Phase Resistance][Company Seal]`

Tags are not just mechanical — they are metaphysical classifications. A `[Cursed]` tag means something specific about the item's history. A `[Radiance]` tag implies Church contact. `[Echo]` tags mean the item retains impressions of prior use.

**Spell Architecture:** Magic is cast through conduits or *runlines* — pre-established channels either burned into objects or prepared in terrain as Spell Anchors. Freeform casting exists at the high end, but it is expensive and imprecise. Most combat casters work within their prepared infrastructure.

**Miracle Classification:**
- *Minor Miracle* — Observable but technically plausible. Healing acceleration, enhancement, localized weather shift.
- *Major Miracle* — Reality-breaking in scope. Soliera rewriting a disease out of a species at a conceptual level. Arden eliminating something that happened.
- *Relic-Tier* — Artifacts witnessed by multiple factions, confirmed to have survived divine contact. Typically singular, often self-evolving relative to their bearer's metaphysical alignment.

**Divine Null:** At the divine level, spell cancellation is instinctual rather than prepared. Soliera passively rejects conceptual attacks through her intent-reality field. Arden simply does not permit magic directed at her to function in his presence.


### Combat Logic (D&D Flow State)

The world runs on D&D mechanical logic expressed as realistic flow. Buffs, initiative, and advantage states exist — they're just not announced. A character who gets the drop on another has narrative initiative. Someone with reach advantage and high strength doesn't announce it; it manifests as the description favoring them. Status effects like bleed, burn, or stagger exist as physiological realities, not game tokens.

High-level combat in this world is brutal in direct proportion to Tier. Tier 1 characters die to bad luck. Tier 3+ characters are structured encounters. Tier 4 combat is short and absolute. Nobody at Tier 4 fights for long — either it ends fast or someone retreats because continuing is stupid.

Arden does not fight. He concludes.


## XIII. LOCATIONS

### Cradle (Village / Growing City)

Soliera's origin village. Small when she lived there, quietly impossible in its prosperity even then — the soil yielded more than it should, illness was rare, livestock didn't panic, the community was not utopian (there were arguments, politics, petty grievances) but catastrophe had not touched it in living memory.

**State at departure:** Soliera Prime left Cradle fortified and thriving. Before she traveled to Outpost Seven, Arden dug a moat around the entire settlement — not instructed to, not asked, simply done. The walls that now ring Cradle stand fifteen feet high and five feet thick, built from a super-dense dark wood that does not appear in any regional lumber catalog. Soliera's blessing on the walls is passive and permanent: they are not enchanted in any formal tag-system sense; they simply benefit from being built in proximity to her and being intended for the protection of people she loves.

Crops inside Cradle perform at a level that agricultural surveyors who visit tend not to write up, because the reports don't publish well. Illness remains rare. The community continues to grow. Outsiders who settle there tend to stay.

Cradle is not famous yet. It is becoming famous slowly, in the way that Outpost Seven became famous — by being inexplicably better at existing than anywhere nearby. It is one of the most prosperous settlements on this side of Northern America, and the people who live there attribute it to good land and good luck, which is, in the most practical sense, accurate.


### The Road (Cradle to Outpost Seven)

Two mid-ranking Company officers came to Cradle on routine Company business — not a recruitment mission, not a directive. They spent time in the village. Long enough for Cradle to absorb them in its usual way: the food was good, the people were easy, nothing went wrong. Soliera decided she liked them well enough to go back with them when they left.

She was not recruited. She was not compelled. She chose to go. Evan was not part of this leg of the journey — no one from Cradle was. Just Arden and two soldiers who would later file reports they were not entirely sure how to classify.

The road produced miracles in the way roads near Soliera tend to: unannounced, unrepeatable, impossible to file accurately. The full P-beats for this stretch are unwritten. What is known: things happened. The two officers arrived at Outpost Seven having experienced events that recalibrated their understanding of what she was. They did not discuss the reports with each other.

**Arrival:** When the group reached Outpost Seven, the commanding officer moved to dismiss the two mid-rankers — the tone was administrative contempt, not a request. Soliera noticed. She said something. The dismissal did not happen. The two officers remained. Nobody at the Outpost reviewed the sequence of events closely enough to produce a record worth citing. The commanding officer's position on Soliera adjusted accordingly.


### Outpost Seven

The Company installation Soliera reached after departing Cradle — described on paper as a minor Company-Church outpost, actually a megafortress disguised through administrative classification as something smaller. By the time Soliera had been present long enough for the Outpost's ambient reality to begin adjusting, it had effectively become a divine embassy operating under Company letterhead.

It has stabilized now. The field zone around it extends approximately twenty miles in every direction with layered checkpoints and global monitoring attention. It is considered one of the safest locations on the continent, which is interesting because almost no defensive action has been taken there — things threatening it have simply stopped arriving.

Evan lives here. His garden in the outer compound is performing better than it has any climatic right to. Arden's presence is assumed by all monitoring bodies at all times. Veris has a room across the hall from Soliera's. Cassian, Veyren, and Varn maintain their own sections of the compound and train in the outer yard.

The Outpost's teaching staff is a mix of races — most are human, a few are not. All are world-class caliber; paragons in their disciplines, not ordinary professionals. One staff member, the librarian, is not what they appear: The Many operates here in human disguise, posing as an Outpost librarian. The Many is the only non-human presence at the Outpost in the sense that matters — everyone else is what they say they are. The Many is something older, something that emerged from folklore or myth in a way that has never been cleanly classified, and whose interest in Soliera Prime specifically is the reason they are here. They observe. They do not interfere. They are deeply, quietly fascinated.


### The Compound

Beneath Outpost Seven. Multi-floor installation, partially warped by mimetic influence from Company dead that didn't stay dead, corrupted spatial logic in the lower levels, and whatever the Church buried there before the outpost was repurposed. It is the dungeon beneath the safe place — proof that the world's damage predates Soliera's arrival and doesn't simply dissolve because she's near.

It is the location where the narrative gets honest about what corporate divine-decay looks like in the basement.


### The Field Zone

The twenty-mile defense radius surrounding Outpost Seven. Not a wall. Not fortification. Just the area within which Arden's probability-of-threat-manifesting approaches zero. It is monitored internationally because it is the most anomalous peaceable zone in the known world, and every faction wants to understand why, and none of them have been able to get close enough to investigate with the right instruments.
