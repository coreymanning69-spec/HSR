## Mortal Perceptions and Functional Hierarchy

Together, these individuals form an integrated system: Soliera provides the guiding purpose and stabilizing presence; Sera enforces the boundaries and carries out willful change; Deashi guarantees ultimate security and consequence; Niv-Saiffar orchestrates resources and plans; Velin handles communication and translation of will; Pell ensures the system cares for the least and learns from the people; Ember keeps the system’s heart compassionate and its miracles potent through belief. It’s like a pantheon operating as one deity, or a government without bureaucracy where each member is an entire department unto themselves (meta: notably, classic empire roles – general, spymaster, diplomat, etc. – are here filled by quasi-divine figures, but all in service to a singular vision, not personal ambition). The Triad’s interfacing with the Sanctum is so complete that one can hardly separate the “leader” from the “infrastructure” – they are symbiotic. As one concise rule stated, “presence replaces mortal thrones” – indeed the Triad are the throne, the law, and the engine of this living city.

Mortal Perceptions and Misreadings

The ordinary people of Faerûn and beyond struggle to comprehend the Sanctum for what it truly is. Instead, they frequently interpret it through familiar lenses – often as an empire, a religious movement, or an occupying force. These tensions and misreadings appear in numerous scenes, highlighting the gap between the Sanctum’s nature and mortal understanding:

Imperial Misconception: The sweeping territorial influence and civic transformations of the Sanctum lead many to
call it an “empire”, even though it lacks a typical imperial administration. We see officials in Waterdeep and
other cities essentially capitulate to Soliera’s rule as if she were an empress or conquering monarch. For
instance, when Sera spared three Primeverse soldiers but ordered “Bring me your king”, the men understood it in
the old paradigm: “the gods had arrived, and they demanded an audience”. They reported up their chain of command
like one would after encountering an invader issuing ultimatums. Similarly, in Oak City, the local Church and
Company authorities misread the Sanctum’s arrival as a possible territorial takeover – prompting the armed
convoy and snipers. The Church leadership splits – one faction (Malachar) insists on asserting jurisdiction
(implying they view the Sanctum as a rival power to challenge), while another (Hale) urges seeing it as a matter
of survival and adaptation. This debate within the local leadership shows how they oscillate between treating
Soliera as a foreign sovereign to resist or as a higher power to appease. Ultimately, when Sera and Soliera
establish a peaceful embassy (literally building a house and sharing food), Hale’s humane approach wins out and
the locals adopt new etiquette to coexist. But the very need for new etiquette underscores prior
misunderstandings: initially, townsfolk try using their normal protocols – armed greetings, titles like “ma’am,”
etc. – only to find these can backfire. In one telling aside, “what’s polite to them may be offensive to Sera.
Every word/title is a potential landmine”. A militiaman calling Soliera “lady” in the wrong tone could be seen
as insolence. This fragility in manners is something one might experience under an occupying tyrant as well, so
villagers conflate Soliera’s unpredictable divinity with the capriciousness of a conqueror. The difference, of
course, is that the Sanctum truly cares for its people, whereas empires mostly extract resources. Mortals slowly
realize this – e.g. Oak City’s folk see that instead of taxation or demands, the Sanctum brought them a
self-replenishing feast and improved infrastructure. That realization (that this “occupier” gives more than it
takes) is mind-bending, and some respond with quasi-religious gratitude while others remain cautious, suspecting
a hidden cost or future domination (after all, who just gifts a house with silk couches to a whole town?). This
ambivalence illustrates the domestic horror vs. divine horror tension: Is Soliera a benevolent queen or a
beautiful monster? To many, she might be both.

Religious/Church Misidentification: Others attempt to frame the Sanctum as a new religion or cult – a church
with Soliera as deity and Sera as her prophet/enforcer. Mortals in Faerûn are used to clerical hierarchies and
worship rituals, so the casual, intimate way the Triad interact can be deeply confusing. For example, outsiders
witness Sera’s public displays of affection with Soliera – “Sera constantly claims Soliera physically… a public
declaration of belonging” – and don’t know whether to read it as sacred ritual or blasphemous irreverence. Some
devout observers (like paladins of established faiths or a character like Althea mentioned in the notes)
experience “faith tension, prayers and doubts against the Trio’s overwhelming presence”. They pray to their gods
for guidance: are these beings to be worshipped, feared, or opposed? One character, Old Mara in Oak City,
initially reacts in a ritual manner – invoking guest-right and laying down white cloth as if dealing with
powerful fey or angels. She frames the encounter in religious hospitality terms, performing what her Church
taught for dealings with higher powers. This is a cultural misreading born of caution and piety; luckily, it
meshes decently with Sanctum etiquette (respect and consent are indeed themes Soliera likes). However, more
rigid clergymen (like Brother Haim, the timid local priest) are simply paralyzed – their cosmology didn’t
account for living goddesses arriving by portal. Some might denounce the Sanctum as heretical or demonic until
shown otherwise. Indeed, in the Thay arc, the Red Wizards initially see the Sanctum as a threat to their godlike
power, leading to fatal misjudgments (they assassinated a dragon under Soliera’s protection, which provoked
divine retribution they couldn’t fathom). Even after the Sanctum’s works tilt benign – healing plagues, feeding
villages – pockets of resistance likely cast Soliera as a false god or anti-god. The narrative doesn’t deeply
dive into organized religion’s response (aside from negotiating with deities like Auril or Lolth at Soliera’s
level), but one can infer many common folk start worshipping Soliera/Sera in some way, which is itself a
misinterpretation: the Triad doesn’t ask for worship, only alignment and respect. A scene illustrating this is
the festival summit: Soliera speaks to her 300,000+ residents not to demand praise, but to apologize for not
seeing them sooner and to explain the truth of Deashi. This humility shocks people – it inverts what a church
would do (where a goddess would demand fealty, Soliera instead empathizes). Mortals prepared for a sermon of
domination instead got an explanation and gentle reordering of infrastructure. While the crowd was “utterly at
ease” and likely adoring, it’s notable that Soliera’s actions “made her entire reality simply what she says” – a
power that, if seen cynically, could be interpreted as brainwashing or cult-like reality bending. Thus even her
kindness can be eerie to a skeptic.

Occupier/Oppressor Fears: Especially in the Primeverse context, where modern sensibilities exist, some mortals
simply fear the Sanctum as an unknown occupying force. The campaign uses the term “Divine Horror vs. Domestic
Horror” – one way to view this is that to ordinary people, the Sanctum embodies Divine Horror (cosmic,
otherworldly threat) intruding into their domestic sphere (homes, daily life). Scenes from Oak City after the
gate appears show residents peeking from windows in terror, arming themselves, or even one man wetting himself
after witnessing Sera’s power. An illuminating moment: after Sera’s violent warning in the tavern, the locals
“had seen it in their bones – everyone was going to die… And yet – life lingered.” They are left trembling not
just at what did happen (a boot sliced off, soldiers humbled) but at the realization of what could have happened
(total massacre) and that their continued life is at the whim of these beings. This is classic horror framing:
the relief of survival tinged with dread that it was only by the invader’s grace. We also see a tavern keeper
and a mage, Amara, reacting with “disbelief” and physical shaking even as Sera cheerfully returns to eating lamb
like nothing happened. Amara’s internal question “How can that be real?” and observation “this girl kills
without hesitation… but speaks of love like prayer” exemplify a fundamental misalignment of perspectives: what
is normal and loving to the Sanctum is terrifying to mortals. The Sanctum tries to mitigate this through optics
– for example, Hale (the compassionate church leader) emphasizes “humane optics”, instructing Sanctum agents to
appear merciful and avoid unnecessary scare tactics. The result is a kind of choreographed etiquette (white
flags, soft words, Sera modulating her Velvet Spiral charm aura) to ritualize terror into etiquette. But despite
these efforts, many mortals simply cannot shake the feeling of walking on a knife’s edge. One document notes:
“Outsiders: perpetual terror; one wrong word = annihilation. Inner Circle/children: opposite; protected, adored…
Sanctum: ritualizes terror into etiquette and optics.” This succinctly captures how misunderstandings arise:
outsiders only see the horror (and assume an oppressor), while those within the Sanctum’s embrace experience
only comfort (perhaps assuming everyone else must feel this too, which is not the case). The Sanctum’s very
attempts to be gentle can be misread – e.g., Sera enforcing a “titles only” rule and shushing someone is an
attempt to avoid killing them (giving them a gentle warning), but the person shushed might experience it as
arbitrary tyranny.

In summary, the narrative frequently flags these misunderstandings through dialogue and POV shifts. It contrasts
the domestic normalcy the Triad feels (for Sera, dragging a 500-pound couch to redecorate is no big deal, for
mortals it’s a shocking display of strength that blurs the line of reality) with the divine dread mortals feel.
Many mortals initially frame the Sanctum as an extremely powerful empire or a new pantheon to fit it into their
worldview. Over time, some perceptions evolve – cities like Waterdeep go from fear to fawning acceptance, their
citizens enjoying the golden age the Sanctum brings (even as high society trembles privately at Soliera’s
shadow). But in every new encounter, the cycle repeats: panic, awe, adaptation. The campaign explicitly has
mechanics for “Rumor Radius” and “Panic” gauges, indicating that whenever Sanctum influence appears, rumors
(often exaggerated or wrong) spread faster than truth, and fear spikes until the Sanctum normalizes itself in
local culture. Even at the end, when Soliera nearly conquers the whole world (Primeverse endgame), there’s
mention that a countering force arises – a young man of opposite nature leading opposition, because inevitably
some will always see the Sanctum as an oppression to throw off. This underscores that no matter how much comfort
and plenty the Sanctum provides, the horror of lost autonomy and the inscrutability of living gods ensure that
mortals will continue to have conflicted, imperfect understandings of what the Sanctum really is.

THE SANCTUM: OPERATIONAL REFERENCE BIBLE

Status: Post-Scarcity Utopia / Opt-In Theocracy Population: ~2.1 Million (Expanding) Governance Model: Divine Corporation (Executive Board + Hyper-Competent Administration)
I. THE POWER PLANT (The Executive Board)

The Sanctum does not run on taxes or elections; it runs on the physical presence of the Triad. They provide the "Capital" (Infinite Power/Resources) and the "Security" (Absolute Defense).

    SOLIERA (The Chairman / The Battery):

        Function: She does not rule; she is the infrastructure. Her passive existence stabilizes reality, softens floors, purifies water, and cures chronic illness within her radius.

Operational Role: "The Hush." She sets the cultural baseline. Her lounging and relaxation are required for the empire’s stability.

SERA (Quality Control / The Immune System):

    Function: She does not patrol; she reacts. Her Knife-Thought Edge detects hostile intent before action occurs.

Operational Role: "The Filter." She removes bad actors instantly. She creates the "Velvet Spiral" (social gravity) that makes bureaucracy run smoothly through sheer awe.

DEASHI (Legal / The Nuclear Option):

    Function: He does not move; he waits. His presence in the Antechamber is the ultimate deterrent.

Operational Role: "The Boundary." He creates the only "No-Go Zone" in the Sanctum, ensuring the populace respects the lethality of the Gods.

EMBER (The Variable):

    Function: The innocent paradox. She amplifies belief and, in the Primeverse, creates conditions where Soliera reduces her voluntary presence dampening. [Attenuation = Soliera's self-imposed shield for mortal protection, not a power ceiling.]

Operational Role: "The Stabilizer." She must be protected to prevent reality glitches.

II. THE ADMINISTRATION (The C-Suite)

The Gods provide the power; these individuals provide the distribution grid. They effectively run the Sanctum as a business.

    NIV-SAIFFAR (ADMINISTRATIVE HEAD):

        Role: Administrative command, logistics, and macro-economy. He has always been in charge of Sanctum administration and does not make mistakes.

        Impact: An Ancient Red Dragon running a "red-tape proof" supply chain. He manages the infinite stockpiles of ore/grain and distributes them instantly.

VELIN (Chief of Staff / External Relations):

    Role: Diplomacy & Human Interface.

    Impact: Manages the "Melting Pot" integration. She is the face kings and commoners speak to, translating Divine Will into actionable policy.

T535 IRONSLAG ASSIGNMENT:

    allied Ironslag: the status of Duke Zalto and fifty fire giants, the rights
    and support of four hundred freed workers, and the limits and consequences
    governing the exposed yakfolk. The goddesses and trainees explicitly do not

III. THE DIRECTORATE (Department Heads)

Each Steward directs unnamed functional teams whose size and internal layers
expand or contract with need. The stable canon is department function and
reporting relationship, not a symmetrical staffing formula or fixed roster.
1. Infrastructure & HR (Director: PELL)

    Mission: "Therapeutic Care."

    Operations:

        Orphan Care: Priority One. Managing the "Third Rail" safety net.

Public Works: Bathing houses, mess halls, housing assignments.

Health: Processing refugees from "survival mode" to "living mode".

2. Intelligence & Reach (Director: KESSLA)

    Mission: "The Hush Agents."

    Operations:

        The Network: Managing spies in Waterdeep, Luskan, etc..

The Underdeal: Waterdeep's sewer district housing Xanathar's Guild, the local
thieves' guild, and the Sanctum orphanage/sanctuary and Embassy operations.

Doctrine Spread: Agents act as missionaries/social workers, not just spies.

SOUND CURTAIN: The Sanctum house operates under a passive silence enchantment (~80ft perimeter, self-enforcing, localized to the estate). Named and confirmed canon at M182. Town goes about its business outside; the household has its own audio space inside.

3. Defense & Culture (Directors: KORIN & LETHA)

    Mission: "Normalization of Legends."

    Operations:

        Defense (Korin): Ranger Corps, Scouting, Threat Assessment.

Culture (Letha): Festivals, Arts, Morale. Keeping the population "interesting" for Soliera.


## V8 Open Register: Field-Agent Layer

The locked Agent doctrine is surgical deployment, never punitive torture, and
missionary/social-work presence alongside intelligence work. The current
corpus has the directorate layer but no named field Agent below director level.

Do not invent a field operative merely to fill a scene. The next authoring pass
may define a recruitment pipeline, cover-legend standard, field procedure, and
the rule used when an Agent and a city Steward disagree. Those are open design
surfaces, not implied permissions.

Dialogue texture is available without resolving the machinery: a mortal may
say "Hush Agents," "the white cloaks," or "Agents" according to proximity and
age, while Sanctum internal language remains "Agents" / "Intelligence and
Reach."
