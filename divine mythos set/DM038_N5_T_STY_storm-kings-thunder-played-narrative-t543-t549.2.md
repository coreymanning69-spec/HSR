## T543 — Goldenfields: First Contact

### T543.0 — The Standing Grain

The network put them down on the hardpack a quarter mile short of the wall, in
the lane between two fields, and the first thing either of them noticed was that
it was beautiful.

Late in the season, and Goldenfields was doing what Goldenfields does. Grain to
the horizon in three directions, gold going brown at the tips, heavy enough that
the heads bowed. Cut rows laid in windrows to dry. The abbey wall to the north
with the granaries stacked behind it like a second, squarer town. Somewhere out
of sight a mill was running, and the sound of it came across the flat as a low
steady knock, patient as a heartbeat.

There were people in the field to their left. Eleven or twelve, working a row
with sickles and a two-wheeled cart, close enough that Wren lifted a hand.

Nobody lifted one back.

They did not stop working, either. That was the part Doran filed. Two strangers
walk out of nothing onto the lane at the edge of a harvest field — a man in
mirror-white plate with a sword on his back that is not a sword so much as an
argument, and a woman in white and gold who is very obviously not from here — and
the crew kept cutting. Heads down. Sickles going. One of them, a boy of maybe
fourteen, started to look up, and the woman beside him put a hand flat on his
shoulder blade without breaking rhythm, and he did not look up.

They walked on. Goldenfields is enormous and nothing about walking it is fast,
and for a while the wrongness stayed exactly that size: nobody hailed them,
nobody sold them anything, nobody asked their business. In a town that feeds the
whole North and lives on the road traffic that comes to buy, that is not shyness.
That is a rule.

Half a mile on, in a field that had not been cut yet, there were three boulders.

Wren saw them first and did not immediately understand why she had stopped. Then
she did, and it was a farmer's logic, not a cleric's: field stone does not stay
in a field. You pull it. Every spring you pull it and stack it at the margin, and
the margins here were stacked — long low walls of it running the boundaries,
generations of pulled rock. So a field worked for four hundred years does not
have three boulders standing in the middle of it, chest-high on a horse, spaced
forty feet apart in a line that is not quite a line.

They left the lane and came up through the barley.

At sixty feet it was still stone. Gray, weathered, pitted, one of them furred
green on the north face like it had sat there since the abbey was founded.

At forty feet the near one blinked.

Not the boulder. The boulder was a back. Seventeen feet of stone giant folded
down onto its heels with its arms crossed over its shins and its head down
between them, folded that way long enough for moss to take on its shoulder, and
it was watching them come up the row with one eye out of a face that had not
otherwise moved.

The other two had not moved either. Not the way sleeping things don't move. The
way a held breath doesn't.

And the third — the far one, still mostly hidden in the standing grain — had a
hand open on the dirt beside its foot, and the fingers were working. Slow. Curl
and flatten. Curl and flatten. Wrong for stone, wrong for a stalk, wrong for
anything in a field in the afternoon, and out of time with everything else in the
world including the mill.

### T543.1 — The Ground Stands Up

They came up the last thirty feet and the ground stood up.

Not fast. That was the worst part. Three seventeen-foot bodies unfolding out of a
crouch they had held long enough to grow moss, and they did it in the same
second, in the same way, like three doors opening off one hinge. Dust and dry
chaff sheeted off their shoulders. The moss on the near one's back tore loose in
a mat and fell.

Wren had her hand up before she had decided to. Palm out, fingers open, the
oldest gesture there is.

The far one took a fistful of Goldenfields out of the ground. Not a rock — a
slab: dirt and root and four hundred years of pulled-stone bedding coming up in
one wet tearing sound, held one-handed, dripping soil.

Nothing said anything. Nothing was ever going to.

### T543.2 — The First One

Doran did not run at it. He arrived at it. Fifty feet of standing barley went
down in a single flattened line and the Cleaver was already moving when he got
there.

Five swings. The first opened the leg. The second and third went into the same
place because that was what the first was for. The fourth took the hip apart. The
fifth came up under the ribs on the backhand and something inside the giant's
chest made a sound like a wall coming down.

It did not fall.

Two hundred and forty-four points of divine steel through a body, and the stone
giant was still upright, still holding the slab, and — this was the thing Doran
noticed, and filed, and had no time to think about — its face had not changed.
Not before the first swing. Not during. Not now, with its own weight coming out
of it in sheets. Nothing in the eyes moved.

So he made himself the loudest thing in the field. He stepped square into its
shadow, planted the Cleaver point-down in Goldenfields soil, and said one word up
into that unchanging face in a register that carries — and whatever the word was,
the giant heard it. The slab dropped. Four hundred years of pulled field-stone
hit the dirt and stayed there, and the giant reached instead for the club at its
back, and came for him.

One swing went wide. The mirror-white plate took the second across the shoulder
and turned most of it into a shove. The dagger was in Doran's open hand before
the club had finished travelling; he put it in under the arm on the follow-
through and it was gone again.

And forty yards behind him the second one was already moving, because Doran was
now fifty feet away from Wren, and she was standing alone in chest-high barley
with a seventeen-foot silhouette closing and her hand still, ridiculously,
half-raised.

She got the shield up. The club came down through it and skidded off nothing.

A quarter mile north, the harvest crew had not stopped cutting.

### T543.3 — The Fire

The wings came out of her back the way they always do, which is to say all at
once and without a sound, and the barley flattened in a ring under the first
downbeat.

She went up fifty feet and put fire on both of them.

Two blooms, one on each, close enough together that the pressure fronts met in
the middle and the flame went flat and horizontal for half a second across thirty
feet of ripe grain.

It was not enough. That is the honest shape of it. The near one turned its
shoulder into the blast the way a man turns into rain and came out the other side
with its arms scorched black and its grip on the club exactly where it had been.
The far one took the whole thing full in the chest, walked two steps out of the
fireball still burning, and did not adjust anything about how it was standing.

The field, however, took.

Late-season barley, dry to the root, standing thick, and a light wind out of the
southwest. It did not smoulder.

Wren was fifty feet up watching that happen — watching the exact moment where
Goldenfields stopped being a place with a problem in it and became a place on
fire — when the second giant tore a wagon-load of dirt and stone out of the
ground and threw it.

It caught her across the ribs and shoulder. The ward ate it and the ward was not
what it had been. She was turned wrong, wing folded, and she has no strength to
speak of and never has, and the sky stopped holding her.

She came down fifty feet into her own fire.

### T543.4 — Grand Cleave

Grand Cleave is not a bigger swing. It is a different animal, and Doran had to
stop being one thing to become the other: the Cleaver came off the ground into
both hands, his feet reset, and he gave up his movement and everything else he
could have done with the next six seconds to buy one commitment.

The arc went through a hundred and eighty degrees of burning barley and took the
giant across the middle.

It did not come out the other side. Huge bodies stop it — that is the whole cost
of the mode, and the reason the swing is worth what it is against everything
smaller. The blade went in and stayed in and the giant came apart around it
without coming apart.

It was already dead when it started to fall. And in the half second between the
two facts — between the thing that was holding it letting go, and the body
understanding — something arrived in its eyes.

Doran saw it.

Then it was on the ground and the fire was on it and he was pulling the Cleaver
free with his boot on its ribs, and there was no time at all to do anything with
what he had just seen.

Fifty feet east, the second one took a single step and brought a club down on a
woman lying on her back in burning grain.

The ward went. It went the way it is built to go — the blow was bigger than what
was left of it, so the whole blow stopped, all of it, the impact and the follow-
through and the weight, seventy-five points of Soliera's arithmetic spending
itself in one instant to make one swing not have happened. Wren felt the club
stop eight inches above her sternum and felt the ward stop existing in the same
moment.

The second swing came down into a plate of hard blue air she had already put
there. It skidded wide and took a two-foot trench out of the field.

She was completely unhurt and she had nothing left between her skin and the next
thing.

### T543.5 — Free Their Mind

He could not get there. That was the first thing, and he knew it before he had
finished the swing — the Cleaver was still in the giant's ribs and he had spent
every second of the last six on putting it there.

So he used the only thing he had left, which was his voice, across fifty feet of
burning field, in the register he uses when a room has to hear him the first
time.

"FREE THEIR MIND!"

She heard him and she was already reaching before she had her feet under her, and
she did not reach small. Sixth level, straight out of the domain slot, because
Doran does not shout twice and there is no version of this where she goes cheap
and asks a second time.

Dispel magic wants something to take hold of.

There was nothing there.

Not resistance — resistance she would have felt; resistance is a hand closing on
a hand. This was a woman reaching into a locked room and finding the room. The
giant's mind was occupied, she could see that much through the Weave, it was the
loudest thing in the field, and the occupation was not sitting on the giant where
a spell would sit. It was not laid on. Whatever holds these things is not in
them, and it is not in that field, and a sixth-level dispel went all the way
through and closed on air.

She had time for exactly one thought about what that meant, and then a club came
down at her.

She was already gone. Feet, wings half-flared for the turn, and thirty feet of
the world folded and she was standing in the flattened lane Doran had cut on his
way in, twenty feet from him, close enough that everything she carries covered
him again.

The one that had been standing over her came after her across the burning ground
and swung twice into the smoke and hit nothing either time.

### T543.6 — The Knee, and the Rain

He crossed the distance before the giant had finished its backswing and he did
not go for the head or the chest. He went for the knee.

The Cleaver took it across the joint, and the shove that turns a cut into a fall
was in the swing, and the giant's whole leg stopped being load-bearing. Three
tons of stone giant went down into burning barley on its side with a sound like a
quarry collapsing, and Doran was already stepping inside its guard while it was
still falling, because a thing on the ground cannot use its reach and he intended
to spend the next four seconds proving that.

Behind him the second one came across the field at a dead run for Wren and put a
club through the space she was standing in, twice.

And Wren, who had not moved and had not defended herself and had spent the whole
of the last six seconds looking north at four hundred acres of somebody's winter,
put her hand up and it rained.

Not a cloudburst. A hundred-foot cube of grey vertical water dropped out of
nothing onto a burning field, hard and cold and absolutely without drama, and
every exposed flame under it went out at once. Steam came off the ground in a
wall. The standing grain at the edge of the burn stopped being a fuse. The stink
of wet ash rolled north.

Ninety feet of Goldenfields was a black scar now, and it was going to stay ninety
feet.

The giant on the ground got its elbow under itself and started to come up.

### T543.7 — He Does Not Look

It stood, and Doran let it, because there is nothing to be gained from hitting a
thing while it is down when you are going to hit it anyway.

The club came at him twice. The first went over his shoulder. The second he did
not even move for.

And on the second miss the Cleaver came back the other way along a line the giant
had just opened for him, and it went in under the arm and up, and Doran turned
his hips into it the way he turns his hips into everything, and the swing did not
stop until it had been all the way through the chest cavity and out.

He did not look at its face on the way down.

He was already turning for the last one before the body landed.

### T543.8 — Twenty-Four Seconds

Thirty-five feet, and then seven swings in six seconds.

The last one was not a fight. Doran arrived inside its reach before it had
registered that the field was a wet black scar and both of its brothers were
lying in it, and after that it was only a question of how long three hundred and
twenty-six hit points takes to come apart under a two-handed weapon in the hands
of someone who has never once in his life swung it badly. The answer is under six
seconds. The giant was still standing at the fourth swing. It was not standing at
the seventh.

It went down into the ash and the steam closed over it.

Twenty-four seconds, start to finish.

### T543.9 — The Row

Wren had not moved. She was standing where the misty step had put her, in a
hundred feet of cold wet ruin she had made on purpose to stop the fire she had
made by accident, and there were three stone giants lying in it, and every one of
them was whole. She had made sure of the fire and Doran had made sure of the
bodies and neither of them said a word about either.

Somebody's whole granary was a black rectangle now. It would be visible from the
abbey wall. It would be visible from a long way further than that.

A quarter mile north, at the edge of the burn line, twelve people with sickles
were still working the row. They had not stopped. They had not stopped for the
fireballs or the fire or the rain or three giants dying, and they were not
stopping now, and the nearest of them was close enough that Doran could see the
boy's shoulders and the woman's hand still flat between them.


## T547–T549.2 — The Abbey Yard and Ithreva's Arrival

At the south gatehouse, Goldenfields was working under a total lock: five
thousand people moved grain without speech or recognition; the cellar ramp under
the watch posts did not bottom out. Doran and Wren cleared six Red Wizards and
three porter stone giants. Two agents were disintegrated, leaving no bodies for
the Ledger. The alarm gong changed nothing. A girl on a rick line looked up for
one moment after the agents died, then returned to the work: a fact, not yet an
explanation.

Miros Xelbrin, innkeeper and elder of the Fellowship of Innkeepers, was able to
speak but barely stand. He confirmed a gradual purchase of the town, that people
who resisted were sent below, and that the great temple of Chauntea has been
sealed and untouched for eleven days. The temple is protected; Ithreva has not
attempted its steps. Wren arranged a ten-minute Ember contingency to keep
civilians standing if the field pair did not report. It cost no resource and did
not invoke Divine Intervention.

The north-road response was five stone golems, five v2 stone giants, five
knight-tier defenders with Myrkul-marked weapons, and three Red Wizards. Wren
created G2, then G1 held a Careful *reverse gravity* across the column. Doran
cleared the three grounded enemies; G1 and G2 destroyed the suspended force.
All five golems, five giants, five knight defenders, and three wizards died.
The golems counted as creatures for Harvest, but Ithreva was not within range.

At T549 the mill stopped and every enthralled person turned north. Ithreva
teleported in on a spectral dragon, with the Ledger of Attendance open and two
Named Dead attending. Doran charged, leaving the dragon at 5 HP and his own AC at 23 until his
next turn. The real Wren remained invisible roughly 320 feet away; G1 and G2
were the public "Wren."

Wren's Weave-sight established that the dragon was a dispellable bound structure,
the Ledger carries the Named Dead's two threads, the town's hold is not a spell,
and a second line from Ithreva runs off-field. That line confirmed a phylactery
connection but not its location at this beat. G2 then cast *time stop* for four turns. At
T549.2, G2 has completed three self-only setup turns and is paused on the fourth
with its action undeclared. Affecting the carried Ledger ends *time stop* after
the breaking action resolves.
