## HARD CANON NOTES

T327: The Whitefire Hearth #LOC-WATERDEEP-EMBASSY #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-EMBER #ATMOSPHERE-DOMESTIC-HORROR
Waterdeep Embassy, post-Well of Dragons

— The fire was too big. It always had been.
A twenty-foot-wide maw of white brick and divine mortar dominated the north wall of the embassy’s central lounge—open, square-toothed, and softly growling with gold-orange light. The flames didn’t crackle or smoke; they curled like silk in slow motion, folding around invisible currents, casting no soot. No one questioned why the marble tiles in front of it didn’t blacken. No one stepped close.
Except her.
Soliera stood too close—just short of touching. The heat, if it existed, bent around her. She didn’t lean on the mantle, didn’t crouch, didn’t speak. She simply stood there, eyes on the flame... then slowly turned, gaze traveling without urgency or reaction, toward the couch nearby.
Sera was crouched on her knees across the ottoman, face alight with feral concentration. She held a giant blue ribbon like a sword, twisting it midair with both hands. The bow was too large, the tails too bouncy, and every time she pinned it against Ember’s head—floof—the glowing red strands rejected it with elemental dignity.
“Stop that,” Ember said flatly, not moving.
But she didn’t stop her.
She sat very still, elbows on her knees, spine straight, eyes slightly narrowed. Her hair shimmered with living heat—embers flickering like candlelight in a furnace’s breath. It wasn't angry. It was just... not participating.
Sera pouted, then squinted harder. “Ohhh you think you can resist me,” she teased, eyes narrowing with wild delight. “We’ll see about that, you little lava ferret.”
The ribbon twisted again. Ember sighed audibly. Somewhere across the room, a vase cracked from the passive heat. No one moved to fix it.
And Soliera… watched. Still and unreadable.
She had not said a word since breakfast.
She had simply walked to the fire—stood inches from it—and now observed the only two beings in the world made by her will but not governed by it. One moved too much. One barely dared to move at all.
And the flame behind her flared—just once—without sound or ash.

T328: The Ribbon Rebellion #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #INTERACTION-PLAY #THEME-FAMILY
— Ember’s first morning was not marked by war drums or divine proclamations, but by Sera trying to pin an oversized blue ribbon to her head. Ember sat straight, her body still new to rest, her instincts resisting play even as her hair passively singed a distant vase.
Soliera watched. Silent. Sera grinned. The game was one-sided.
“Stop that,” Ember said.
But Sera didn’t stop.

T329: The Second Offering #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-EMBER #RITUAL-DRESSING #THEME-DEVOTION
— Later, in the evening stillness, Soliera rose. The weight of her divine presence altered the room’s gravity, and Sera moved. Off the bed, eager, trembling—not with fear, but devotion.
She undressed Soliera with practiced reverence. Ember lingered near the doorway, unsure. Should she help? Should she look? Her fingers twitched at her side, half-reaching, then still.
“You can help,” Sera said gently. “You’re a good sister. Soliera chose you too.”
Sera held out her hand. Ember took it.

T330: The Sleep of Fire #CHAR-EMBER #THEME-REST #RELATIONSHIP-BONDING #SLEEP-MECHANICS
— That night, three bodies lay entangled in silk and sovereignty. Sera curled contentedly. Soliera rested at the center, eyes closed. Ember lay beside her—head tucked under Soliera’s chin, her first real sleep descending like ash over cooling stone.
Her dreams were not dreams. They were silence. Peace. Stillness.

They matched today. Soliera’s gown long, Sera’s dress short and scandalous, Ember’s outfit awkward but clean. None wore shoes.

T341: The Morning After (Embassy, Bedroom) #DOMESTIC-ROUTINE #CHAR-EMBER-GROWTH #SCENE-WAKEUP
— Silk sheets rustled. Morning sunlight—dim and magically filtered—crept through the side panels of the divine chamber. Soliera stood first. As always, no alarm, no hesitation. Ember, watching, copied them clumsily. Her blouse went on sideways. Her socks were misaligned. But no one corrected her—not harshly.
Soliera was already dressed. Sera handed her a comb. Ember reached for a belt. The three were silent, but whole.

T342: Breakfast with the Staff #CHAR-VELIN #CHAR-KESSLA #LOGISTICS-MAPS #CHAR-EMBER
— Velin entered first, her heels striking the tile like polite punctuation. Kessla followed—barefoot as always—setting down trays with practiced grace. No one bowed, but everyone stood when Soliera rose. Sera, sprawled again by now, waved.
“Thay, huh?” Kessla mused over eggs and fruit. “They’ve been… less than quiet.”
Velin updated logistical maps—troop patterns, Ark supplies, communications. Kessla handled Waterdeep politics and spymaster duties. Ember blinked, half-awake still, standing just behind Soliera with one hand loosely curled around the back of her skirt—not pulling, just present. Grounded. She held her bread without eating it. Her role wasn’t clear.
Not yet.

T343: The Divine Routine #RITUAL-DRESSING #THEME-MATCHING-OUTFITS #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-KESSLA
— Soliera allowed Ember to assist with dressing her for the second time. Sera beamed the whole time, spinning slowly in a half-sheer sea-green wrap. “You look like us now,” she whispered to Ember.
The three were dressed in coordinated tones: pale seafoam and navy. No shoes. None needed.
Kessla gave Sera a kiss on the cheek and said, “Good luck.” Not for fear. But reverence.
The Divine Triad left through the upper hall. The Embassy stirred behind them—papers shifted, voices whispered, water boiled. It was the calm before judgment.

T344: A World Prepared #LOC-WATERDEEP-DOCKS #SOCIAL-REVERENCE #CHAR-SERA #INFRASTRUCTURE-SIGIL
— The walk to the dock wasn’t solemn. It was surreal. Fishermen glanced, bowed, then quickly looked away. Parents clutched children—not in fear, but in reverence so deep it paralyzed.
The new flags flew high: the black bird on white—Sera’s sigil. The piers were cleared, three of them entirely reconstructed under Sanctum design. Staff moved like dancers in a temple.
Sera giggled at Ember’s curiosity. “You wanna go to sea?”
“We have a ship,” she bragged. “You haven’t even seen it!”
Soliera walked behind them, silent. Smiling slightly. Watching.

T345: The Ark #VEHICLE-THE-ARK #CHAR-MALHARON #CHAR-DAIRE #INFRASTRUCTURE-DISPLAY
— The Ark—massive, radiant, and utterly divine—gleamed with white stone and golden inlay. It towered three stories above the dock. Children played at the base. Vendors offered food to the workers. The Ark stays in the water. the Ark is NOT an airship.
On the top deck, the captain waited. Tall, dark-skinned, elegant. His name was Malharon. His vice-captain, a younger elven woman named Daire, stood just behind.
They straightened as Sera bounded aboard.
“Hellooo! Where even is that place we’re going?”
Ember bowed slightly. The crew flinched.
Soliera arrived last. The gangplank lifted without command. Divine presence had returned to the sea.

Behind her, Sera winked at a sailor. He blushed. She kept walking.
Soliera stood on the bridge, hands folded behind her back.

T348: Ember’s Watch #CHAR-EMBER #CHARACTER-GROWTH #OBSERVATION #THEME-PERMANENCE

The divine walked the sea again.
Thay had sent no ships to stop them.
That was their final mistake.

T353: The Judging Sea #CHAR-SOLIERA #ATMOSPHERE-WRATH #THEME-JUSTICE #COURSE-CORRECTION

Sera mentioned Thay to Kessla, who explained happily. Soliera listened intently. Ember had slipped away through the lower halls of the Underdeal Embassy — the Waterdeep sewer district housing Xanathar's Guild and the local thieves' guild — not wandering, but visiting. Xanathar's people. The rogues and crews who had been blessed and folded into Sanctum operations back when the beholder himself had been corrected into compliance. This was her first time meeting them properly. They loved her immediately. Several of the older hands had heard of "the fire child" from Waterdeep accounts; a few had quietly decided she was their patron saint of staying out of trouble. The embassy's lower floors were warmer than usual when she came back up. Kessla noticed. Said nothing. The Sanctum's presence in the Underdeal was working exactly as it was supposed to.

Thay had red towers. Red ships. Red wizards.
But they had no red that could match the sea when it judged them.
Soliera didn’t rage. She didn’t plan.


A silence passed through the Embassy’s war room and settled into the ocean’s current.
Even Ember felt it.
