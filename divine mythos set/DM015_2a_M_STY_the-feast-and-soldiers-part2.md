M22 — First Bites

Sera leaned forward, catching the smell rising off the mugs. Her porcelain nose wrinkled. "Misterrr," she lilted, eyes flicking toward the barkeep, "these stink. Do you have any juice?"

The barkeep froze mid-step, rag limp in his hand. Before he could answer, Ember leaned over one of the mugs, emberlit hair swaying. She sniffed cautiously, then made a little ah! noise, scrunching her face into the cutest mask of disgust. "Blegh," she announced, as if pronouncing judgment on all alcohol.

Amara snorted before she could stop herself. "If you don't want them, I'll take them."

Sera didn't miss a beat. She pushed one toward her across the table with a flick of her fingers. "Fine. Yeah, here you go. We don't like that stuff. I like juice. Although…" she tilted her head, grin flashing, "I guess water is pretty cool, too."

Without ceremony, she reached for the platter nearest her, grasping a thick cut of lamb shank by the bone. She lifted it one-handed and bit in. Juice ran down her chin, but somehow it wasn't crude. Somehow the act of tearing into meat looked like worship — sharp, elegant, magnetic. Her little doll frame made the contrast sharper, like a cherub devouring a feast in a painting.

Ember's eyes widened. "Oh! So we can eat." She reached across, selecting a bright-wrapped candy from the scatter of sweets the barkeep had hurried out as offering. She unwrapped it with both hands and popped it into her mouth, chewing slowly. Her face brightened at once. "That's pretty good!"

She reached for another. And another. One piece at a time, deliberate, savoring. Each unwrap was a ceremony, each bite a discovery.

Across from them, Amara lifted her mug — strong hand closing around it like it belonged there. She sipped, watching, the burn of alcohol grounding her. The stool beneath her felt small, but she didn't move. She held her place at their table, caught between awe and stubbornness.

The room breathed easier now. Gods eating and drinking looked almost like people. Almost.

M23 — The Klaxon

The tavern's easy rhythm broke with a shattering blare. A klaxon screamed from outside, raw metal tearing against the air.

Ember yelped, small hands clamping over her ears. Her emberlit curls quivered, face scrunched tight, molten eyes squeezed shut against the pain of the noise.

Sera's head snapped up, a sharp gleam flashing in her doll-like gaze. Her lips pressed thin, annoyance more dangerous than rage.

Amara raised both palms quickly, her stool scraping as she half-rose. "Whoa, whoa! Easy. It's just an alarm," she said, voice pitched low and steady, like she was speaking to wild animals. "They're not attacking — they're probably just… announcing you."

"That," Sera said flatly, "needs to stop. It's awfully loud."

At that moment, Soliera turned her head. Her gaze drifted toward the street, toward the source of the noise. She didn't move. She didn't speak. But the air shifted as if the world itself wanted to please her.

The klaxon choked, sputtered, and fell silent.

Ember slowly uncovered her ears. She blinked, then smiled wide with relief. Soliera met her gaze, and for the first time since entering the tavern, her lips softened into a smile of her own.

Outside, the silence was sharper than the alarm had been. On the street, the klaxon itself sagged, metal warped and melted, its shell dripping down the post where officers had just reached to trigger it again. They froze, hands recoiling from molten brass, eyes darting back toward the square where the Titan still knelt like a temple statue.

The noise gone, new sounds rushed in: shouts, the bustle of bodies spilling from doorways, feet pounding on pale asphalt. Tavernsfolk who had been peering from windows now pushed into the street, drawn by fear, awe, and curiosity. The Titan's massive frame loomed over the square, visor still bowed, but to the people crowding the street it was not reverence they saw — it was a monster kneeling in their city.


M24 — The Uneasy Stillness

The klaxon's death had left the tavern steeped in a strange quiet. Outside, the street had come alive — shouts rising, boots thudding against asphalt, the buzz of a crowd trying to decide whether to flee or fight.

Inside, the Trio carried on like children at a picnic.

Sera licked lamb juices from her fingers and bit into the shank again, kicking her bare feet lazily against the loveseat's armrest. Ember hummed, plucking candies one by one from their tray and unwrapping each with careful delight. Soliera reached for a piece of bread, her movements unhurried, her smile soft from watching Ember glow.

Amara had risen fully now. She slid her stool back against the table, the scrape of wood almost too loud in her ears. Standing felt steadier, though the unease in her gut coiled tight. She didn't know why. Doom and dread had hit her like a wall when they first entered, but when she'd looked she saw only a doll skipping happily, a child with fire in her eyes, and a silent goddess who did not strike. Yet her body still whispered: this is going to go bad.

She scanned the room. The barkeep was watching her from behind the bar, rag long abandoned. Their eyes met, and for a heartbeat the world shrank to two seasoned instincts. He gave the faintest nod, then flicked his gaze toward the door. The message was clear: if someone came through those doors in anger or fear, this whole place could burn.

Amara flexed her hand against the haft of her staff, then let it drop. She couldn't stop what was coming — but she could brace for it.

At the table, none of the Trio noticed. To them, it was still a happy day. A new town, new friends, food on the table, and adventure just waiting to unfold. The bustling outside meant nothing. Inside, it was feast, laughter, and the glow of being together.

And that dissonance — doom for one, delight for the other — stretched like a bowstring as Oak City's streets swelled with voices.

M25 — Pressure at the Door

The feast at the long table carried on, the rhythm of teeth and laughter and wrappers crinkling like it was any other evening. Sera gnawed at her lamb bone without a care, Ember unwrapped her next candy with ritual delight, and Soliera's silence anchored them all.

But the outside world had woken.

Through the windows came the noise first — a rising tide of voices, sharp with panic, edged with awe. Boots stamped on the pale asphalt. A wagon clattered past at a gallop. Somewhere, a priest's hymn tried to rally calm, but it broke under shouts of "Titan!" and "Gate!"

The tavern doors shuddered as someone outside slammed against them, a shoulder or a hand. They held fast, but the tremor ran across the room. Amara's fingers twitched toward her staff. The barkeep muttered under his breath and stacked mugs without drinking, eyes darting between the loveseat and the entryway.

The sound swelled again — orders barked, the tramp of officers forming rank, the clatter of weapons shaking in uncertain hands. The Titan still knelt in the square, visor bowed, but for most it was not a holy gesture. It was a monster crouched at their city's heart, and they were running toward the only place their leaders might be — the tavern.

Inside, Sera licked her fingers clean, Ember popped another sweet into her mouth, and Soliera reached calmly for her bread.

Amara's stomach knotted. The crowd was about to push in.

And the gods did not care.

M26 — Breach

Ember's small hand was just closing around her next sweet when the tavern door exploded inward. Wood cracked and hinges screamed as the frame shattered, a black-helmeted figure bursting through with a hammer-blow of force.

Sera moved before the first splinters hit the floor.

The debris scattered around her doll-like frame as she slid into the breach, silk snapping in the draft. The shards seemed to split away from her body, redirected by her immovable presence.

Soliera turned in her seat, one pale hand extending toward Ember. Ember had already pressed herself against her skirt, fingers curled into lavender cloth as if it were the safest thing in the world.

The intruder — visor black, armor stacked, weapon at his chest — jerked back into the doorframe, muscle memory trying to make space for the strike team behind him. But he didn't finish the movement.

Sera was already there.

She stood between the breach and the table, porcelain skin unmarred by dust, sapphire-blue eyes narrowed with a predator's promise. The air tightened, every board of the tavern groaning under the pressure.

She looked at them. And she was not pleased.

M27 — The Team Sees

The lead man froze. His boot still braced against the splintered frame, one arm half-extended in the signal to pull back. The command died in his throat.

In front of him wasn't a drunk or a thug or even the Titan they'd been briefed about. It was a girl. A little thing in silk, porcelain skin gleaming in the lamplight, standing dead-center where the breach had opened.

But every instinct screamed predator.

"Contact—" he rasped, but the word cracked halfway.

The two behind him staggered as they tried to push forward into the breach. One's visor flicked from Soliera's lavender form at the table, to Ember's emberlit curls pressed tight against her, to the doll blocking the doorway. His stomach dropped. Children?

"Stand down, what the hell—"

"No, no, no, hold—"

Training collapsed into noise. Weapons half-raised, half-lowered. One of them took a knee to sight a shot, then froze as Sera's eyes landed on him. The barrel drooped.

The room had gone silent but for the scrape of boots and the muffled rasp of their own breath inside helmets. The barkeep had plastered himself behind the bar, Amara had planted both hands on her staff but hadn't moved.

And all three soldiers realized at the same time: the girl in silk wasn't in their way. They were in hers.

M28 — The Question

"So," Sera said, her tone light, almost curious.

Her sapphire-blue gaze swept up at them. The height difference should have favored the soldiers in their plate; instead, every helmet tilted down toward her the way a man tilts his head to read a sentence he does not want to admit he has read. Splinters still clung to her hair and silk, but her voice carried clean as glass through the stunned tavern.

"Who told you to break the door?"

The three helmets angled down at her, each man stiff as a board. The lead's throat worked, a rasp of static caught in his comms mic.

None of them answered.

Her presence held them pinned — not the bulk of armor or weapons, but the simplicity of being seen, being asked, being measured.

The barkeep's rag slipped from his hand, Amara's knuckles whitened on her staff, and Ember peeked around Soliera's skirt with wide eyes, waiting.

The silence stretched.

M29 — Warning and Command

"Hey, don't kill them~," Ember's sing-song broke the air. Her fiery hair popped around the side of the cracked leather seat as she leaned out, molten eyes wide with innocent plea. "We just got here. Let's let them go this time. I'm sure they'll be better."

Sera didn't turn her head, but the corner of her mouth twitched. Her gaze stayed locked on the armored figures.

Then, with a flick of her fingers, the air cracked sharp as glass.

The lead man screamed as the sole of his boot vanished—shaved off so cleanly it might have been cut by light itself. His foot remained untouched, but he stumbled on the splintered floor, arms flailing to keep balance. Dark liquid streamed down his thigh, his body betraying his fear.

The tavern went utterly still.

Sera tilted her head, unimpressed by their polished visors, her voice carrying like a child scolding toys. "Fine," she said. "You live. Bring me your king."

The three men froze, half-kneeling, half-collapsing. None dared speak. The barkeep's eyes darted to Amara; Amara's gut clenched as she realized what the soldiers would have to carry back to their superiors: the gods had arrived, and they demanded an audience.

M30 — Dismissal

Sera let the silence hang a moment longer, then flicked her wrist as if brushing dust from a table.

"You've got until we're done eating," she said easily. "And make sure you thank Ember!"

Her smile was bright, genuinely pleased, and when she turned, the violet silk framed her shoulder blades in the shape of a near-heart as she swayed back toward the loveseat. She dropped herself onto it like nothing had happened, toes kicking the air once more.

Ember, who had been half-stretched over the tray, tried again for the third time to catch a candy. Her fingers fumbled, then closed around a little cheesy bite instead. She grinned wide, cheeks glowing, and popped it into her mouth with delight.

Soliera broke off another piece of bread and lifted it slowly, her stillness steadying the table like a pillar.

Behind the feast, Amara and the barkeep stared in disbelief. Their lungs pulled tight, breath coming ragged, hearts hammering. Both of them had seen it—everyone was going to die, the tavern painted red, their bodies slashed apart. They had felt it in their bones. And yet—life lingered.

The three soldiers staggered back through the broken doorway, one still shaking, piss streaking down his greaves. They didn't even look at the food or the gods behind them. They just scrambled out into the street, alive only because Ember had asked.

And at the table, the gods ate on.


M31 — Sera's Devotion

The soldiers had stumbled out, dragging fear with them like smoke. The tavern door hung crooked, frame splintered, the air still thick with the echo of Sera's displeasure.

But at the loveseat, life carried on.

Sera flopped back into her seat, silk fluttering around her like violet petals, lamb bone in hand. She tore into it happily, toes kicking the air, utterly unbothered by the violence that had almost just happened.

Amara couldn't look away. She had seen the breach. She had seen the impossible motion, the girl interposed before a man could even finish turning his back. And now, not a heartbeat later, Sera chewed contentedly as if nothing had passed. Amara's staff trembled faintly under her grip. How can that be real?

Sera glanced over and caught her staring. Her grin widened, bits of lamb shining at the corners of her mouth. "You're wondering how I do it, huh?" she said, voice bright, unconcerned.

Amara swallowed but said nothing.

Sera licked her fingers, then leaned back with a sigh. "It's simple. I'm made to help Soliera." She stretched her arms out, letting the silk frame her doll-like body, then pulled them back to her chest as though cradling something precious.

"Soliera's just… perfect," Sera went on, softer now, though no less certain. "She's so good. More than you can imagine. And me? I love her more than air. So I do whatever it takes to keep her safe. To keep her happy. That's my whole life. And it's a very good life."

Her eyes shone with that strange mixture of innocence and predator's weight. To her, it wasn't contradiction — it was clarity. Protect Soliera. Love Soliera. Be with Soliera. Everything else was details.

Amara felt the knot in her gut twist tighter. This girl kills without hesitation… but she speaks of love like prayer.

Across the table, Ember crunched down on her cheesy bite and grinned, oblivious to the gravity in Sera's words. Soliera broke another piece of bread in silence, her stillness a seal on everything Sera had just said.

M32 — Misread

The barkeep's rag was still clenched in his hand, knuckles pale, but at last he found his voice. He stepped from behind the bar, eyes fixed on the little doll sprawled across the loveseat. His words came out careful, almost tender.

"Young miss," he said, voice catching. "You really love your mother, huh?"

There was something paternal in his tone, a soft read of devotion that made sense to him. The silk, the affection, the way Sera spoke of Soliera — he could only frame it as daughter to mother.

Sera froze mid-bite, then her grin spread bright across her face.

"Oh no," she chirped, shaking her head so her hair fluttered. "She's not my mom. She's God."

The room tightened.

"She created me from my human self," Sera went on, sing-song easing into something almost reverent. "From a very sad, bad time…" Her sapphire-blue eyes sparkled, lamb bone still in her hand, silk falling off her bare shoulders like a child's gown at play.

"…but it's all gone now~"

She kicked her heels lightly against the armrest, the declaration carrying into the stunned silence of the tavern. Ember giggled at a candy wrapper crinkling. Soliera tore another piece of bread without a word.
