## HARD CANON NOTES (continued 2)

A couple of locals occupied tables closer to the bar. One older man nursed a chipped mug, pretending to look at his drink but sneaking glances at the newcomers. Two women further down whispered to each other, the kind of murmurs that turned into stories before the night was out. The barkeep, a barrel-chested man with thinning hair, froze mid-polish when Sera and Ember came through the door, rag hanging limp in his hand.

The room wasn't loud before, but now it was quieter still—every cough, every scrape of chair, swallowed by the fact that gods had just walked across the welcome mat.

M17 — Introductions in the Tavern

"Hello~!" Sera chirped, silk flashing as she spun in a half-circle to face the room. "I'm hungry. This is Ember. That's Soliera!"

Her words carried like bells through the tavern, bouncing off old wood and glass bottles.

Ember's cheeks glowed faintly brighter, emberlit eyes widening. "Your name isn't hungry, Sera!" she protested, tugging at the sleeve of lavender cloth. "It's Sera!"

Soliera's smile flickered small, a glint of warmth for her girls, even as she remained still and silent. Ember clung close to her side, a dark flame against pale silk. Sera, by contrast, strode right into the middle of the tavern as though she had always belonged there, her doll-like feet padding softly over the cold floorboards.

In the back corner, Amara Windbreaker shifted. She had been still, facing the wall in her corner table, but now something knifed through her chest — an evil pressure, absolute and unyielding, like doom pressed close to her spine. Her dark eyes turned, first falling on Sera with her singsong voice, then on Ember glowing like a lantern, and finally toward Soliera.

She could not look away.

The goddess stood quiet, lavender hem brushing ankles, gaze unreadable. Amara's throat tightened. It was one thing to hear whispers of monsters and gods, another to feel their weight.

Sera noticed. Of course she did. She grinned, bright and careless. "Hey! You're pretty. Can we eat with you?"

A hush tumbled across the room. Amara didn't answer at once. Ember tilted her head toward the small table, voice earnest: "But how would we fit?"

Sera just shrugged, turning on her heel to point toward the center. "Oh, I don't know. We'll move to that really long table in the middle!"

The barkeep, who had frozen with rag in hand, started wiping the bar again. His knuckles whitened as he rubbed at old nicks and scars in the wood, suddenly aware of every chip and groove. Around him, the other locals kept their breaths small, afraid even the scrape of a chair leg might carry the wrong way.

And in the corner, Amara Windbreaker sat, staff leaned against her table, caught in a line of sight she could not sever.


M18 — The Focus on Amara

The scrape of wood cut the air like a blade. Amara had pushed her chair back, and in the hush of the tavern, the sound rang louder than any voice. Every head turned.

She stood, one palm brushing the table's edge, eyes cutting toward the trio. "Well," she said, voice low but steady, "how was I supposed to ignore this? And why—why here? Who exactly are you?"

Sera took a few light steps toward her, porcelain frame gleaming in the tavern's yellow light. Her voice was sing-song, but her eyes were not. "Don't pick that up. It won't do you any good anyway."

Amara's fingers had already begun to close around the length of her bo-staff. She froze, the warning landing like a weight on her chest. "What?" she breathed, more instinct than argument.

Before she could decide, Ember padded closer, gaze wide and warm. "That's a cute staff," she said brightly, reaching out without hesitation. Her small hand closed around the haft, lifting it with ease. She swung it once—too carelessly—and knocked a pewter mug clean off the table behind her.

"Eek!" Ember squeaked, nearly dropping the weapon as the mug clattered to the floor. She fumbled it back onto Amara's table, hands flaring emberlight as she scurried back, pressing herself against Soliera's lavender side, one hand finding the fabric of her skirt without looking.

Now all three of them were upon her.

Soliera advanced last, the steady tide that followed Sera's hop and Ember's stumble. Her silence filled the space between words, her gaze locking on Amara with an inevitability that rooted her in place.

Amara's throat worked. The weight of three gazes—playful, curious, and crushing—pinned her more surely than any chain. For the first time in years, her staff felt like a stick. For the first time in years, she was not the most dangerous woman in the room.

From her corner vantage, Amara Windbreaker understood with bone-deep clarity: she was the subject now, not the observer.

M19 — Amara Under Gaze

Amara's throat was dry. She had stood to confront them, but now the confrontation had reversed; three sets of eyes pinned her as surely as shackles.

Sera's eyes danced first, playful and sharp. The doll-like girl folded her shoulders inward, arms pressing against her chest. It wasn't much, but she leaned into it anyway, presenting herself in a mockery of seduction.

Amara blinked. What is she—

Ember's laugh cut the thought in half. "Sera, why are you trying to embarrass this woman?"

"I'm not trying to embarrass her," Sera replied without missing a beat, voice bright as bells. "I'm trying to sleep with her."

The words landed like stones in Amara's gut. Heat crawled into her face despite herself. "What… what are you talking about?" she stammered, dark eyes flicking between them. "Why would we—why would I—" Her pulse hammered against her ribs. "What has happened while I've been sitting here taking a nap?"

The barkeep didn't answer. He moved like a man condemned, rag thrown aside, arms full of bottles and plates. He laid them out across the long table in a frantic rhythm: mugs thumping down, bread loaves sliding across polished wood, platters of cheese and smoked meat following. Every dish he could reach went flat across the table until it resembled a feast. His face gleamed with sweat, hands trembling, but he didn't stop.

Amara glanced at him once, then back to the trio. The air was tight with two truths at once: gods had walked into the tavern, and one of them had just announced her desire with the bluntness of a knife.

She swallowed hard. This is madness. This is doom. And yet…

Soliera stepped forward behind her girls, still silent, still unreadable. That weight—cool, unrelenting—pressed into Amara's spine. She forced her chin up, but her palms felt hot against the table's scarred wood.

She had asked who they were. Now she feared she already knew.

M20 — The Leather Seat

Ember's face tightened with effort, her little black-and-gold tongue poking from the corner of her lips as she concentrated. She was staring at the long bar table, now covered edge-to-edge with platters and mugs, and her eyes brightened suddenly.

"Oh! Sera, the food is ready!" she exclaimed, clapping her hands together in delight. Then, turning to the corner, her emberlit gaze found Amara. "Are you coming to eat with us?"

Amara stiffened. Her first instinct was to protest — to stake distance, to assert control. But before she could frame words, Sera's porcelain hand darted out and clasped her own. The grip was small but unyielding, warm as stone pulled from the sun.

Sera tilted her head, violet silk swaying. "Oh, you've got strong hands," she said, tugging lightly. Her voice made it sound like a compliment, but the pressure in that grip reminded Amara she had no choice but to follow.

Her jaw tightened. She was no child, no villager trembling in the pews. Her calloused palms were the record of years with staff and blade. Yet here she was, being led across the floor like a dancer plucked from her seat. Strong hands, yes. But not strong enough to break free.

At the bar, Soliera moved last, lavender hem brushing past stools lined in a row. Narrow-topped, three-legged, wobbly — unworthy. She passed them without pause, her silence heavier than any demand.

Sera glanced back at her, then chirped toward the barkeep, "Hey! Misterrr…" Her voice dipped into a playful purr. "Do you have a nicer chair?"

The barkeep froze halfway through setting another mug, throat bobbing. "Y-yeah," he stammered. "But it's over in the corner. Awfully large, though. Not… not for someone your size."

Sera's grin widened. She skipped across the tavern floor, skirts fluttering, and found it where he'd said: a wide, cracked leather loveseat, more bench than chair, big enough for two broad men.

She put one delicate hand to its arm and lifted. Wood shrieked against wood as she dragged it across the floor. The scrape carved a pale line into the boards, but she moved as though hauling nothing heavier than a stool, humming to herself as she brought it across the room.

By the time she set it down before the laden table, the tavern was so quiet that the final thunk of leather against floor echoed like a drum.

Amara's dark eyes tracked every step. What are you? she thought. What could you possibly want from me?

And yet, her hand still tingled where Sera had touched it.

M21 — Seating the Feast

The tavern smelled of oil, old wood, and chilled air from humming vents overhead. Plates and bottles crowded the long table until it looked like a feast day had been declared without warning. Locals whispered from the edges, too afraid to leave but too afraid to draw near, while outside the Titan still knelt sentinel at the gate.

At the center of it all sat the divine trio.

Sera had hauled the cracked leather loveseat across the tavern floor and claimed it like a throne. She lounged against the armrest, bare feet kicking lazily in the air near the right-hand side. Her silk shifted with each movement, violet fabric catching the yellow lamplight, her grin half-playful, half-predatory.

Ember had claimed the seat beside Soliera. She sat cross-legged — adult-bodied, obsidian and gold-veined, kintsugi lines warming a steady amber along her forearm where it brushed Soliera's lavender sleeve. Every now and then her emberlit eyes flicked toward the food and the lines along her collar brightened — half a question already forming about the bread, the jars, the steam rising in coiled patterns she wanted to read. She did not lean against Soliera so much as occupy the same gravitational well, one hand of the source resting beside the other.

Soliera herself was still, lavender hem falling neat over leather. Her silence anchored the loveseat more firmly than nails to the floorboards. When she turned her head, the barkeep's rag slipped from his hand.

And there, at the table with them, sat Amara Windbreaker.

She had tried to hold the wall, tried to keep her corner, but Sera's porcelain hand had closed around her calloused palm with that bright sing-song: "You're pretty. Want to hang out with me?" Her first answer had been confusion, almost protest. Then Ember's teasing voice, Sera's blunt declaration — "I'm not trying to embarrass her. I'm trying to sleep with her."

Amara had flushed. No man had ever spoken want for her with such brazen clarity. Warrior's hands and hardened will had kept her distant from desire, and most men lacked the courage to look past her staff or her scars. But here was a doll of silk and teeth, stating want like it was weather.

So Amara sat now, not on the loveseat but on one of the plain stools Soliera had passed by. Her staff leaned against the table within easy reach, her back straight, her jaw tight. Her dark eyes shifted between the three: Soliera's unreadable calm, Ember's innocent glow, and Sera's lazy sprawl with that smirk that still made heat creep into her cheeks.

The barkeep, desperate to keep motion between them, laid the last mugs down with shaking hands and stepped back.

The tableau was set:

Soliera, silent throne.

Ember, tucked flame.

Sera, apex lounging predator.

Amara, warrior pulled unwillingly into their orbit, perched on a stool that already felt too small beneath her.

And the feast waited.
