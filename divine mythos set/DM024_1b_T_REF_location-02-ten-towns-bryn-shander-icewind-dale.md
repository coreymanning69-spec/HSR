### LOCATION 02: TEN-TOWNS / BRYN SHANDER (Icewind Dale)
**[MAP POSITION]** Far north, southeast of the Sanctum along the new paved road - roughly 3 days travel by horse on the old roads, 1 day on the new Sanctum road.
**[STATUS]** Established / Ally - return itinerary stop and `TR3-SKT` play entry
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** Paved road from Sanctum (fastest overland route in the North). Teleport circle in Bryn Shander embassy.
**[WORD OF MOUTH]** Intimate/direct. These people have seen the girls repeatedly. They were the first communities outside the Sanctum to receive construct labor, road infrastructure, and food support. Many children in Ten-Towns were born into a world where the Sanctum's presence is simply normal.

**[TR3-SKT HISTORICAL PLAY-START]** Duvessa's northern relay sent the crisis alert through the established Bryn Shander channel. The return and southwest-gate emergency are completed history preserved in played narrative and the hashed Chapters 1–9 archive. Do not restart this bridge; current continuation belongs in `DM038_L`.

**Setting Changes (20+)**
1. Auril's shrines across all seven towns have been cleansed by Deashi. The process was not violent - he simply stood near each shrine until the divine thread connecting it to Auril's domain frayed. Locals who witnessed this describe it as "the cold leaving the stones." The shrines still stand but are inert. Some have been repurposed as community gathering points.
2. The weather in Icewind Dale has moderated. It is still Icewind Dale - it is still brutally cold by most standards - but the supernatural killing cold that Auril enforced (the "endless winter" that threatened starvation) is gone. Seasons now behave as seasons. This is understood by the locals to be a consequence of the divine intervention, though they debate whether to credit Soliera, Deashi, or some negotiation between the two.
3. Bryn Shander now has a full Sanctum embassy: a permanent building in the city center, staffed by two Stewards and a rotating construct garrison. The embassy also serves as the local administrative point for all construct road maintenance crews.
4. The Sanctum road between the Sanctum and Ten-Towns is the most-used road in the entire North. Merchant traffic has tripled since its completion. Goods that previously required dangerous overland travel in winter can now move year-round.
5. The fishing trade has transformed. With construct vessels supplementing mortal fishing crews (and following strict catch-and-release protocols for rare species), the daily fish quota that used to take a full day now takes two hours. The surplus goes to Sanctum distribution networks and to cities further south along trade routes.
6. Targos, Lonelywood, Easthaven, Caer-Dineval, and the other towns have each developed small Sanctum liaison posts - not full embassies, but staffed rest points where travelers can access basic Sanctum services (healing constructs, food distribution, road information).
7. The glacier tribe populations (nomadic groups living north of the towns) were offered Sanctum civic terms and largely declined - they preferred their own way. The Sanctum's response was to build them nothing they did not ask for but to leave an open invitation standing. The relationship is respectful and distant.
8. Bryn Shander has become the commercial hub of the entire dale. Its market district has tripled in size. There is now a proper inn quarter, a dedicated construct maintenance yard, and a Steward-mediated trade arbitration office where local merchants can resolve disputes without violence.
9. The Ten-Towns Speaker council still exists and still governs. The Sanctum's presence has not replaced it. What has changed is that the council now consults with Velin's Stewards before making decisions that affect Sanctum road use or embassy activity. This consultation is technically voluntary. No one has tested what happens if they skip it.
10. There is a small community of former Auril cultists who remain in the dale. They have renounced active worship of Frostmaiden (pragmatically - her power is gone from their shrines) but privately resent the Sanctum's presence. Velin has flagged them. No action has been taken.
11. The dock infrastructure at Targos (primary fishing port) was upgraded by construct labor three years ago. What was a collection of rotting piers is now a proper working harbor with stone quays, stone warehouses, and a maintained seawall. The cost to the town: zero. The obligation: Sanctum ships have priority berthing when they arrive.
12. Icewind Dale has a new export that did not exist before: deep-mountain gemstones that the Sanctum's mining constructs discovered while building road tunnels. They share the revenue with Ten-Towns' council on a documented split. The council was initially suspicious. The split has never varied by a single coin.
13. The old dangers of Icewind Dale (roaming monsters, dangerous weather, bandit packs targeting isolated caravans) have been significantly reduced. Construct road patrols operate continuously. The result is that the dale, once a lawless frontier, now feels almost orderly. Some long-time residents find this unsettling.
14. The ice sheet north of the towns - which Sera once ran across, breaking a twenty-mile channel through the Sea of Ice - is now a permanent landmark. Locals call the channel "Sera's Cut." Ships use it as a summer navigation reference. Children dare each other to swim it in summer (it is still extremely cold).
15. The Reghed barbarian clans who operate in the broader Icewind Dale region have had mixed responses to Sanctum presence. Clan Elk (historically the most diplomatically engaged) has a semi-formal relationship with the Bryn Shander embassy. Clan Tiger has not engaged. Clan Bear is currently negotiating access to construct fishing tools in exchange for allowing a waystation on their territory.
16. There is a persistent rumor among Ten-Towns locals that Deashi walks the high ice north of the dale at night. No one has confirmed this. No one is going to go check. The rumor has significantly reduced bandit activity in the northern approaches.
17. Construct-maintained street lighting now operates in Bryn Shander's main market district. It runs on a stored magical charge that the constructs refresh every three days. The town has requested expansion. The request is in Niv's queue.
18. The local inn culture has been permanently altered. There is now a Sanctum-standard accommodation: a clean, warm room with reliable food, available at fixed (low) price, at any Sanctum liaison post. Private inns compete by offering things the Sanctum will not (more elaborate food, privacy, entertainment). The market has stratified interestingly.
19. The Dale's primary annual festival (Midwinter Feast) now incorporates a moment of acknowledgment for Sanctum presence. It is not religious. It is more like the way a town might acknowledge a wealthy patron. The girls were invited to the first one after the Auril cleansing. They did not attend. Velin sent food and a politely worded regret note.
20. The children of Bryn Shander who have grown up entirely within the Sanctum era have no experiential reference for the before-times. To them, the blackbird symbol on the embassy wall is as normal as a temple spire. Several of these children have applied to become Stewards when they come of age.
21. A small population of dwarves who were formerly Auril worshippers (a rare sect) relocated to the Sanctum after the shrine-cleansing rather than stay in a community where their faith was effectively ended. They have integrated well. Three of them now work in construct maintenance.
22. The Mere of Dead Men (a swamp south of the dale, along the road toward Neverwinter) was a Cult of the Dragon staging area. After the cult's collapse, Sanctum constructs were sent to clear the dead and salvage usable material. A Black Dragon that was coerced into cult service was re-encountered here and offered Sanctum terms. The terms were accepted. The dragon, Varixanthus, now resides at the Sanctum alongside Cloudgazer and the unnamed young blue dragon from the convoy interception.

**City Flavor (Street Level - Bryn Shander)**
The city smells of fish oil, pine smoke, and something almost mineral - the deep cold of the mountains, which even the moderated weather cannot entirely remove. The streets are wide and mud-free now (construct maintenance), lined with low stone buildings that were built to endure rather than impress. The market is loud and specific: you can buy ice-fisher gear, yeti fur (illegal in Sanctum territory but sold here openly), mountain herbs, deep-mountain crystals that glow faintly, and Sanctum-standard preserved food rations packed for travel. The people are direct. They shake hands hard and mean it. They watch strangers carefully. When the blackbird symbol appears on someone's clothing or gear, the watching stops and the respect starts - not fear, specifically, but the kind of careful courtesy you extend to people who have genuinely helped you and whose continued goodwill you value.

**Embassy Status**
ACTIVE - Regional Hub. Two permanent Stewards. Rotating construct garrison of eight. Teleport circle. Velin visits personally four times per year. The embassy hosts town council meetings twice a month, which the Stewards attend as observers, not participants.

**Optional Atlas Hooks (legacy labels `TR-01` through `TR-03`; not the SKT opener)**

This is notable because they have never asked for anything before.
- **TR-03:** The former Auril cultist community has begun meeting in private at an unusual regularity. A Steward flagged it. Velin has not acted. The meetings are occurring near one of the cleansed shrines. Nothing has changed at the shrine. Yet.

**Parallel Threads**
- **Velin:** Has been coordinating a trade dispute between Targos fishing operations and a Waterdeep merchant consortium who claims the Sanctum's presence has "artificially depressed" the northern fish market. This is technically true. Velin is managing the optics.
- **Niv:** Has three supply runs scheduled through Bryn Shander this month, one of which involves a shipment of deep-mountain crystal ore that requires a new packaging technique the engineer developed. The engineer is not in Ten-Towns. Her notes are in Ten-Towns. This is a problem.
