### TR1-07: The Tower Tour — Interior
**Tags:** #LOC-HOST-TOWER #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-CORVEL #CHAR-CASHAAN #CHAR-TEYVA

They walk the tower. Sera drifts through corridors, picks things up, asks questions. Tells the Brotherhood they should catalogue the storage room. Teyva agrees. Cashaan follows at a respectful distance running every calculation available to him.

The oldest texts in cases — not locked, because the locks were less reliable than the wards, and the wards less reliable than the texts' own reluctance to be read by people they've decided aren't ready.

Ember puts her hand on the case glass and asks nicely. The text inside — pre-calendar Faerun script — turns its own pages to something specific. Teyva stares. Cashaan makes a sound that is almost nothing.

Ember tells Corvel the books will open for him. Factual delivery. Not a prediction. He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to. This is going to change the course of his career.

Sera does a quick assessment:

> "The place is a sad place. There's a man in the walls who used to be a person and now he's a very big building. Something sealed in a room that might be sad. I feel like this place needs more help than you think it does."
>
> "And the flooding," Ember adds immediately.
>
> "Right. We gotta do something about the flooding situation. The whole city's built wrong for it."

Druette, from behind: "The flooding has been an issue for—"

"Centuries," Sera says.

"So we'll sort that."

She says it the way Velin might say "I'll send a follow-up letter." Administrative. Casual. Actionable.


### TR1-08: The Books Conversation
**Tags:** #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-CASHAAN #CHAR-TEYVA #CHAR-DRUETTE #CHAR-CORVEL #THEME-DOMESTIC-DIVINITY

**[CANONICAL DIALOGUE — Preserved verbatim. One of the better Sera/Ember philosophical exchanges.]**

> Sera: "Books are okay. But they're no fun. They're just words sitting there. What's really fun is somebody telling you what the book says."
>
> Ember: "Then it's a story. Not a book."
>
> Sera: "It can be both."
>
> Ember: "If it's being told out loud it's a story. The book is just where the story lives when nobody's telling it."
>
> Sera: "So what's the book then."
>
> Ember: "The house. The story lives in the book the way people live in houses."
>
> Sera: "That's actually pretty good."
>
> Ember: "I know."

Cashaan, Teyva, and Druette — three of the most accomplished arcane scholars in the North — watch this exchange in silence. Corvel takes mental notes.


### TR1-09: The Sealed Room — Fourth Floor
**Tags:** #LOC-HOST-TOWER #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-DRUETTE #CHAR-CORVEL #CONCEPT-TRUTH-SURFACING

The sealed room. Fourth floor. Door unmarked except for sixty years of accumulated institutional avoidance — the slight extra distance people unconsciously maintain passing it, the way nobody looks directly at it.

Druette explains: a fragment from the original Cabal. A residual consciousness. Not a full lich. Something that didn't fully complete whatever it was trying to become when Laeral Silverhand sealed the tower. It's stuck.

> "For sixty years," Ember says.
>
> "For considerably longer than sixty years."

Ember looks at the door. The door, in the way that very old things sometimes respond to attention, seems to look back.

> "That's not okay," Ember says quietly.

She puts her hand on it. Not pushing. Just contact.

The room on the other side goes very still in a way that is audible as absence of sound rather than silence. Then something happens that nobody in the corridor has language for — a quality of presence behind the door that has been coiled and fixed for decades simply releases. Not violently. More like a held breath finally let out.

Then nothing.

> "It was just scared," she says. "It's okay now."

Druette's sixty-year institutional problem resolved in four seconds. She has no framework for filing that.

Corvel is writing something down on a small piece of parchment. Nobody told him to. He's doing it anyway.


### TR1-10: The Window — Afternoon
**Tags:** #LOC-HOST-TOWER #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-TEYVA #RULE-SOLIERA-SILENCE-UNLESS-DM

Soliera finds the upper window. Stands at it. Looks at the city. The tower goes quiet around her in the way it does.

Sera wanders back to her natural orbit. Ember follows. They stand and look at Luskan from the height of the Host Tower. The city visible as a whole system from up here. The Ark in the turning basin, pale and enormous and patient.

They stay there a while. Even Sera doesn't talk. Teyva, watching from the corridor doorway, notes it.

> "It's pretty," Ember says.
>
> "Yeah," Sera says.

Soliera says nothing. Her posture says the same thing.

Sera looks at the city. The rope bridges, the walkways, the islands. The geography of a place that has been compensating for its own flooding problem for centuries by building upward instead of addressing the water.

She looks at Soliera. Soliera looks at the city.

That's the whole conversation.

Sera turns back to Harker.

> "Alright. We're gonna fix the flooding. How long has that been a problem?"
>
> "Since the city existed," Harker says.
>
> "Great. So there's nobody who's going to be upset about it changing."

Cashaan opens his mouth.

> "The harbor infrastructure—" he starts.
>
> "Will be better," Sera says pleasantly.

Cashaan closes his mouth. Teyva, beside him, is doing an extremely good job of not smiling.

Soliera looks at the tree. The tower itself — the dead stone tree that used to be a man who gave himself to protect people. Sera chats. They wander the halls.

Sera tells them they should head down. They told Letha they'd be back before dark.

> "Good tower," she says to Cashaan. "We'll come back."
>
> "You're welcome here," he says. And means it, which surprises him slightly.

Ember slides off the window sill and lands lightly. Looks up at Corvel.

> "Come on. We're going to fix the flooding now."

Corvel looks at his Archmage. Cashaan makes a very small gesture that means *go.* Corvel goes.


### TR1-11: The Night — They Stay
**Tags:** #LOC-LUSKAN #CHAR-HARKER #CHAR-TEYVA #CHAR-CASHAAN #CHAR-JARLAXLE

The girls stay the night. Luskan does what Luskan does when something remarkable has docked in its harbor and word has gotten out — capitalizes on it. The dockside taverns run their best evening in recent memory. Sanctum crew who crossed from the Ark spend freely and talk to everyone. The mood in the city shifts from its usual scheming undercurrent. Something pleasant has moved through it.

The Brotherhood hosts. Teyva is an excellent host. Cashaan is present and composed and will spend the next several weeks thinking about the afternoon. Harker attends, understands that his position relative to the other four Ships has just shifted in ways that will take time to fully calculate, and enjoys himself anyway.

Jarlaxle is in the city. He watches the changes from the background and does
not make himself known. The Girls remain surrounded by high-profile people,
and he has no reason to walk Luskan's streets in daylight when he already runs
his part of the city invisibly. He and Velin correspond; the message he sends
her is a joke, not a request waiting to be delivered.


### TR1-12: The Departure Blessing — Before Dawn
**Tags:** #CHAR-SOLIERA #CONCEPT-DEPARTURE-BLESSING #THEME-INFRASTRUCTURE-AS-LOVE #RULE-SOLIERA-SILENCE-UNLESS-DM

**[CANONICAL BEAT — Soliera's passive blessing. Nobody asks her to do this. Sera mentioned the flooding the day before. Ember confirmed it was a problem. Soliera absorbed the information. That's the whole process.]**

Before the ship leaves, while the city sleeps:

**The flooding stops.** Drainage channels that have never existed run themselves into the stone of Cutlass Island and the others. The River Mirar's flow through the city normalizes. The tidal surge points that have driven the flooding for as long as Luskan has existed simply stop being surge points. The geometry of the water changes. The city, which has spent centuries compensating for something, no longer needs to compensate for it.

**The walls are straightened.** Not dramatically — not overnight reconstruction, not theatrical divine intervention. The oldest load-bearing walls in the city, the ones that have been settling and shifting for generations, are simply correct now. True. The masons who check them in the following days will find plumb lines they've been chasing for decades suddenly accurate.

**The harbor itself.** The currents that have made the eastern approach treacherous since the wreck field accumulated are gentler. The salvage divers will notice within the week. The harbor master will spend a month confirming it before telling anyone.

She doesn't announce it. She doesn't tell anyone it's happening. It's just done before the ship leaves.

**[CANON NOTE:]** The Brotherhood will spend years studying what she did. They will not fully understand it. The records they keep will be the most detailed magical analysis produced in the North in a generation and they will still be missing something fundamental. Teyva will note in her private journal that it felt less like spellwork and more like a correction — as though the city had always been supposed to work this way and something had finally noticed the error.


### TR1-13: The Sendoff — Morning
**Tags:** #LOC-HARBOR #LOC-THE-ARK #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-HARKER #CHAR-CASHAAN #CHAR-TEYVA #CHAR-DRUETTE #CHAR-CORVEL #THEME-WITNESS-ECONOMY

They leave in the morning with the full attention of the city.

The harbor front is lined. Not organized — Luskan doesn't do organized civic ceremony without a political reason, and this isn't political, it's something else. People came because something remarkable was leaving and the instinct to witness it was stronger than the usual Luskan calculus of whether witnessing things is worth your time.

The five Ships are represented. The Brotherhood is on the Host Tower's second-floor landing — Cashaan, Teyva, Druette, and Corvel, who is technically not senior enough to be there and is there anyway. Harker is at the dock, because he was the one who walked with them and he's going to be there when they leave.

Sera waves from the Ark's deck. Elbow and arm, the same easy wave she gave when the Brotherhood came through the tower door. Ember waves from beside her, smaller, fingers up, already turning to say something to Letha.

Soliera is at the rail. She looks at the city.

The Ark moves.

By the time it clears the harbor mouth, the first of the city's masons has found a wall that is inexplicably, perfectly plumb. He checks it three times. Gets his foreman. They stand there for a while looking at it.

The harbor turns back to its work. The rope bridges sway. The gulls work the waterfront. Luskan does what Luskan does.

But the water isn't going to flood anymore.


## FOOTNOTES & CANON NOTES

### On Jarlaxle
He deliberately remained in his normal background role while too many
high-profile people surrounded the Girls. He watched the changes to Luskan and
corresponded with Velin. His message was a joke. No request, missed meeting, or
owed reveal remains from this visit.

### On Corvel
An eighteen-year-old Brotherhood apprentice got adopted by Ember for an afternoon and was told the oldest texts in the library will open for him. He's going to be a different kind of wizard than he would have been. The Sanctum effect at street level — no announcement, no ceremony, just a direction pointed and the path cleared. **Trails to: TR-46 (what Ember opened in the sealed building)**

### On Harker
He came in third. He ends the visit with Sanctum diplomatic access, ship repair support from the most resource-rich organization on the Sword Coast, and a story that none of the other four High Captains have. The reason this happened is that Ember liked a fish on a flag. He knows this. He finds it, privately, very funny.

### On the Sealed Room
Sixty years of institutional Brotherhood anxiety resolved in four seconds. Ember said it was just scared. The Brotherhood has no framework for that answer and cannot disprove it. Druette is going to be thinking about it for a long time.

### On the City Itself
Luskan is now a city that doesn't flood. The architectural culture built around compensating for flooding — the elevated walkways, the rope bridges, the stilted construction — will persist for a generation because cities don't rebuild overnight. But the new construction will be different. Lower. More direct. Fifty years from now the city will look different than it does today and the historians will trace the shift back to a morning in the harbor when three women arrived on a very large ship and stayed for one night.

Nobody will quite agree on what they looked like.

The fried dough vendor near the market square will tell the story of the small pretty woman who bought two portions and ate them walking for the rest of his life. His grandchildren will repeat it. It will lose details over time and gain others.

The crab will not be mentioned in any official account. But someone who was at the Salted Mast that morning will tell their friends, and their friends will tell theirs, and somewhere in the third or fourth generation of the telling it will become a story about a god who liked crabs and blessed the harbor on a Tuesday.

Which is not entirely wrong.
