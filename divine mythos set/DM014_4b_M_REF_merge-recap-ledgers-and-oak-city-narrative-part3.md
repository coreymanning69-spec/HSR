Oak City was still catching its breath when the Trio arrived.

They began at the hospital. Sera read the uniforms as a unit, Ember mapped the world by color and shine, and Head Nurse Rhea Collins—competent, unsentimental—granted the only permission that mattered: "Please help us." Soliera did, silently. The building exhaled. Hard anchors snapped belief into place: pediatrics sats rising (89→97), a fever breaking without meds (102.8→99.1), facility water dropping from brackish to clear (TDS 380→20), alarms settling, even a man who'd lost a leg walking out on two. Floors softened; carts rolled true; staff pain eased. Sera praised Soliera openly and kneeled to the healed man; Ember's honest questions set the lanes; Bucky coordinated without fuss; Rhea kept the room sovereign and counted outcomes, not prayers. With that, Oak City's panic slid into awe.

The camera shifted to the Gate.

The titan construct stood watch—arms at its sides, head bowed in peacetime posture—while the Company kept a polite cordon and townsfolk watched the seam. The air changed when Niv-Saiffar stepped through: white-and-red boot, then the man himself, tall and unafraid, followed by ordered ranks of constructs and stewards, and—between them—humans and elves and stranger folk moving with polished grace. Velin's standard was already claimed in the plaza; the Sanctum's mesh unrolled like it had rehearsed with this city all its life. Sera detonated into joy and hit Niv like a cannon; he took the impact, skidded lines into the lightened asphalt, and hugged her like a brother who'd been counting the hours. "Seventeen was too long," he admitted. Ember tugged Amara by the hand—"That's Niv and Velin. They're my friends."—and the plaza's temperature rose to human-warm.

From there, the choreography widened: tents bloomed, line crews leveled seams, traffic patterns were chalked, and the sanctum's competence made proof more than posture. Bucky shifted the Company from contain to deconflict. Veyren—big, ground-sure, newly "claimed"—fell into the Trio's orbit with Tank-with-Manners energy.

Then the Church convoy appeared: white trucks under sheeted canvases, SUVs with dark glass, a doctrine looking for a foothold. Sanctum knights met them on the road in a clean line. A lead truck demanded passage; a single draw cut silenced its engine block without touching flesh. "Walk or not at all," the knight told them, sword tip resting in dirt more as a statement than a threat. That set the etiquette.

In the plaza, the hierarchy clarified itself without speeches. Soliera's stillness set the baseline. Ember touched a tattooed forearm and called his ink "pretty paintings"; the man did not yank away. Sera's Velvet Spiral pressed crowds gently to respect and made posture honest. Malachar and Ysbet carried gatekeeper energy; Hale, reading the moment, moved among his own like a shepherd who knew the difference between order and harm. Bucky handled optics and lanes; Niv handled perimeter math.

Then someone fired at Soliera.

Sera moved before most people had finished hearing the shot. A blur, a soft shockwave that knocked two troopers to knees, a midair pinch as she caught a hot .50-cal bullet between her fingers and flicked it to Niv—"Here, what's this?"—and she was gone, laughing and fast, southeast toward the source. The Church yelped denials; the knights' visors slid down. In the time it took for anyone to say the word "sniper," Sera reached a peeling shed with a stuck half-window, brushed away incoming rounds like rain, and called into the rooms—bright, polite: "These toys don't work. You'd have better luck with your bare hands."

A claymore went off. It hurled her across the porch; she popped up, dusted her torn dress, and said, perfectly pleasant, "That was rude."

"Somebody duck," she offered, one breath of mercy. Then she cut horizontally, four feet off the foundation. The roof and upper walls parted with a single clean scream. Out back, the line studded bark in a straight, unforgiving row. One man hadn't moved; one was too slow halfway down. Sera stepped through the open box, lifted the long black rifle between two fingers like a baguette, tucked a helmet under her arm, and tried one more "hello?" into the silence in case anyone had obeyed. No answer. She walked back to town.

Thirty seconds is a long time when you've seen a bullet stop in a dragon's hand. The plaza adjusted itself: muzzles dipped to honest low-ready, radios fell to murmurs, civilians mirrored knight-hands-visible etiquette. Hale did the quiet work of keeping his people gentle. Bucky widened a corridor and kept his thumb near his mic without pressing it. Niv watched a thin heat seam tighten toward home and told Ember, simply, "Yes—now."

Sera returned small and bright, rifle almost as tall as she was. "I found what did the thing I caught," she announced, wiggling it once. "Also I have a hat." She handed the helmet to Bucky—"Evidence." Veyren arrived at a lope, blushed when he admitted he'd meant "I like you with it," and earned a sunny "Good boy." For a beat, the air itself said you did not interrupt unless you belonged at the center.

De-escalation followed. "Convoy's welcome—walk it in," Bucky told the Church. Hale answered in the register that calms humans, not cameras: "We got off wrong. Let's restart." Malachar saved his pronouncements. Ysbet's ledger hovered, hungry for a sentence that didn't come. The convoy crept. The titan, seventy feet across the shoulders, hands the size of buses, stayed bowed and off. Someone—maybe Hale—prayed under his breath not to ruin it.

The merge kept accelerating. Four or five thousand Sanctum folk had already crossed, and they worked like they'd never stopped: canvases, cables, curbs, kitchens, field mapping with locals shoulder-to-shoulder. The Church had no doctrinal drawer to put this into. Outcomes kept happening anyway, and the safest way through was to be polite.

The Trio steered the tone back to domestic. Ember and Sera argued about the correct order of butter and syrup; Soliera's mouth made a small curve; Niv, a pace behind, said he'd never been lost and decided Sera hadn't either. Soldiers smiled without quite letting themselves clap. The city began learning the etiquette of living near divinity: hands visible, voices low, walk—don't bark—around the still point.

Then Sera proposed what would anchor the chaos: "We should probably make ourselves a home base." She pointed, delighted, at a broken shed. "That one."

Niv knew better than to say no. Soliera lifted her hand a fraction and the ground made room. The shed folded
like careful paper and rose again as a two-story house whose bones were divine and posture domestic: inlaid gold
and warm wood, birds and flowers engraved into stone as if the pictures arrived before the walls, a porch that
breathed once, a hedge trellis that knit itself into bloom. Inside, street noise fell away into crickets and
small wings. An oval main room held red silk, pillows like fruit; to the right, a food bar for griddles and
honey, not bottles; to the left, closets and mirrors and the quiet tools of being pretty; back storage aligned
in a straight sightline; a spiral stair lifted to a one-way glass loft to watch the town kindly. Sera spun on
the silk and parked the captured rifle at the door "for later." Ember brushed a petal when Amara's touch said
yes and beamed at the reflection a mirror gave her. Niv tested a stair newel—"Good bones," which counts as
praise. Veyren used the "leave boots here" hook like a man happy to fit. Soliera stepped in last, and the house
found its exact shape—light choosing where to fall, a draft deciding to be nothing. The city, busy a moment ago,
remembered to breathe.

Where we stand now

Civic situation: Oak City is becoming a Sanctum settlement wrapped around a town—tents and crews working alongside locals; lanes open; no panic. The titan remains bowed and off.

The Trio: Soliera sets baseline without a word; Sera is cheerful and lethal when needed, back to domestic the second the room is safe; Ember's innocence keeps everything small and kind; Amara is the human hand on Ember's sleeve that makes "proper" land.

Sanctum: Niv is the empire's brain in a body—polite, preemptive, and learning this verse by doing; knights enforce lanes without cruelty; Veyren is a tank with manners at Sera's outside shoulder.

Company: Bucky's posture is deconflict/escort; he keeps proof clean and optics calm; local support is aligned.

Church: Malachar (oldest) is recalculating; Ysbet (procedural blade) is taut; Hale is the survivable path and is quietly aligning his people to "do no harm." They've entered under escort, on foot, and are seeing a world that doesn't need their permission.

Open threads set by the scene

The Church's internal split (Hale's humane optics vs. gatekeeper reflex) will decide whether "coexist" or "correction" happens next.

Bucky and Niv have an effective liaison rhythm; Veyren is fully in the orbit and useful.

The Sanctum will keep scaling domestic miracles (infrastructure, water, comfort) as proof, not propaganda.

The new house—divine core, human heartbeat—gives you a quiet lens to keep the scope small when the world outside wants to get huge.

That's the run so far: awe proved by outcomes, a convoy walked instead of fought, a sniper lesson delivered with one horizontal line, and a gold-and-wood home blooming where a ruin stood—three women setting a city's tempo without raising their voices.
