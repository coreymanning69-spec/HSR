Story semi-prose version
Sera was the first one to step off the bank.

Not because she needed to prove anything. Because Lulu was hovering tight and Ember was hugging her like a life preserver and the river itself looked like a living bruise. Black. Thick. Slow. Wrong.

Sera walked straight into it anyway.

Barefoot.

The ichor swallowed her ankles. Little tendrils rose up like curious hands and tried to cling. Tried to slow her. Tried to make a point.

Nothing stuck.

She kept going with her arms swinging lightly. Casual. Almost bored. She waded deeper until it reached her knees, then her thighs, then her waist. The river pressed at her like a mouth that had eaten names for so long it forgot what water was for.

Sera looked down and squished her toes into it.

“Ah, well,” she called out, voice bright, “where’s the thing everyone was worried about?!”

On the shore, Soliera stood relaxed. Still. Watching the river the way a builder watches a bad foundation. Ember stayed near her skirt with Lulu hovering close, trunk curled tight, eyes wide and glassy with old fear.

Under the surface, deeper into the dark, shapes shifted.

Hydrodaemons moved like thoughts you did not want. Quiet. Patient. Closing distance.

Sera turned in a slow circle, letting the muck lap at her waist like it could do something about it. She smiled toward the shore like she’d solved the whole problem by existing in it.

“See,” her posture said. “It’s fine.”

That was when the horizon changed.

Not the sky first. The sound.

A deep groan of metal far away. The kind of noise that makes devils look up even when they pretend they do not look up. The port along the riverline began to stir. Dock crews. Chain gangs. Horned overseers. Infernal labor that usually did not get surprised by anything.

This time they did.

People started backing away from their own workstations. Staring. Pointing. Forgetting to act like Hell was normal.

Something huge slid into view through the smoke.

Zariel’s Flying Fortress.

It did not arrive like a ship. It arrived like a moving city made of punishment. All iron ribs and siege geometry. It drifted toward the docks like the plane itself was making room for it out of habit. Chains clanked. Infernal engines rumbled. The air around it tightened into military order.

Sera looked up and waved.

“Hellooo~” she called happily, as if greeting a neighbor pulling into a driveway.

She swashed back toward the shore. The black ichor dragged at her legs, tried to stain, tried to mark. She came out unblemished. Dry. Not even a smear.

That detail landed harder on the watching devils than the fortress did.

Ember hugged Lulu tighter and whispered into her ear, “I told you it’d be fine.”

Lulu trembled anyway. Her wings beat too fast. Her eyes kept flicking to the water like it might reach up and touch her memories.

The fortress continued to dock. Massive clamps. Iron pylons. The whole port shifting its posture to accommodate authority.

Then something broke in the authority.

Zariel was aware.

Suddenly.

Not in the way a commander is aware of an enemy. In the way a body becomes aware of a pressure it cannot breathe through. The air around the dock did not change. It was not “harder to breathe” like smoke. It was worse than that.

It was presence.

Zariel’s chest tightened under her armor. A shuttered breath escaped her. One hand rose to her plate ornaments as if she could hold herself together by gripping metal.

She swayed.

Then she fainted.

No speech. No confrontation. No battle.

Just a collapse.

Someone dragged her away. Quick. Quiet. The fortress kept docking because machines continue even when pride fails.

Above the river, a different gaze opened.

A portal manifested like a precise cut in the layer. Not dramatic. Not blazing. A clean aperture.

Asmodeus looked down through it.

Not an avatar smile. Not a public mask. A measured stare that felt like a thousand contracts turning their pages at once.

Sera didn’t flinch.

She looked up at him like he was late to dinner.

“We haven’t claimed anything,” she called out, voice carrying without effort. “There’s just no reason to use these people who might not have even wanted to be in it.”

Her eyes sharpened.

“And then you’re getting rid of them. If you’re getting rid of them then how can Soli use them to get entertained later?”

She paused. Tilted her head like she was truly curious.

“Besides,” Sera said, “who are you anyway? Come down here and let’s talk.”

A beat.

“I’m actually pretty hungry if you have someplace for us to talk.”

Ember looked up too, eyes bright even in the weirdness. “Yeah,” she said, simple, “snacks sound pretty cool.”

Asmodeus did not answer with thunder. He answered with logistics.

A place appeared at the edge of the dock. A table. Correct. Technical. Functional. Clean enough to be a negotiation surface. Plates. Cups. Food arranged like a demonstration of competence.

Sera stared at it for one heartbeat, then grinned.

“Well,” she said, “you’re missing the chairs.”

She glanced back at Soliera.

“Soli. Can you show them how it’s done?”

Soliera’s mouth lifted slightly. A private amusement. A tiny crack in the stone of her quiet.

Her hand lifted.

Mesh rose from the ground. Lattice. Folding geometry. The iron itself answering as if it had been waiting its whole life to be useful for comfort. Chairs formed. Large. White. Abundant. Not stolen from Hell. Recompiled out of Hell like Hell was raw material and nothing more.

The table reacted. It did not fight. It shifted. Grew. Smoothed. Plates upgraded into lavishness without becoming gaudy. The whole scene moved from “legal venue” into “dinner.”

Sera clapped at the delight of it.

She pulled out Asmodeus’s chair like she was hosting.

“Here you go,” she said brightly. “You can sit here.”

She placed her own chair beside Soliera’s. Deliberate. Correct. The rules of closeness were not up for debate.

Ember sat in the middle without ceremony. Lulu hovered near her shoulder, still wound tight, still watching the river.

Asmodeus approached. Ruby Rod in hand. He sat because refusing would be its own posture mistake. He watched the transformation without comment because comment would admit surprise.

Sera leaned in like a girl about to teach a grown man manners.

“Well sir,” she said, “if I can call you sir. From what I’m understanding this river keeps making problems for you.”

Her tone stayed light. The words did not.

“If we solve these problems you don’t have any reason to still be around right?”

She waved a hand like the question was purely practical.

“You can’t use these souls anymore. Because that’s what we’re claiming. That doesn’t mean we can’t help you out with some other power source.”

She put her food down. Looked at him plainly.

“We have a city here. Floating around in this layer. It has to go back to where it belongs. If we can work that out then I can give you all the energy you need to power all your people here forever.”
