# Sera's Itinerary (Part 2: Coast and Midlands)

### LOCATION 04: LUSKAN (The City of Sails)
**[MAP POSITION]** Western coast, south of Mirabar along the partially-improved road. Coastal city on the Sea of Swords.
**[STATUS]** FIRST VISIT — No Sanctum presence inside city limits
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** Road from Mirabar (partial construction, transitions to old road south of the Mirabar waystation). The Ark can dock here — technically. The harbor master will have feelings about this.
**[WORD OF MOUTH]** Regional/institutional. Luskan’s High Captains have gathered intelligence on the Sanctum and know the broad shape of the trio. They know that every city the trio significantly engaged with was fundamentally altered. They are performing nonchalance.

**Setting Changes (20+)**
1. Luskan is ruled by five High Captains — each controls a Ship (a political-criminal faction that operates like a hybrid between a merchant house and a gang). The current power balance among the five Ships is volatile: Ship Rethnor and Ship Kurth have been in a cold conflict over dockside territory for eight months.
2. The Arcane Brotherhood — a mercenary wizard society operating out of the Host Tower of the Arcane — maintains a parallel power structure in Luskan. The Captains rule the docks and the streets. The Brotherhood rules what the Captains can’t: the arcane, the intelligence networks, and the sea routes that require magical navigation.
3. Jarlaxle (the famous drow mercenary, leader of Bregan D’aerthe) has a semi-permanent presence in Luskan. He is simultaneously aligned with the Brotherhood, several of the Ships, and at least two foreign powers. His actual loyalty is to Jarlaxle. He has been briefed on the Sanctum and considers the girls interesting. He is not concerned. Jarlaxle is never concerned.
4. The Host Tower of the Arcane is the most visually distinctive structure in Luskan: a massive, alien-looking tower that predates most of the city, built during an era of magical experimentation that modern wizards don’t fully understand. It contains more wards than the Brotherhood knows how to map.
5. Luskan’s harbor is the largest in the North. It handles pirate ships, legitimate merchant vessels, Brotherhood transport, and occasional naval craft that are technically from no recognized navy. The harbor master is the most politically experienced bureaucrat in the city.
6. The city has no formal law enforcement in the way Waterdeep or Neverwinter does. The Ships provide their own security within their territory. The Brotherhood handles magical incidents. The gaps between Ship territories are effectively lawless. This is understood and largely accepted.
7. Since the Sanctum road network began bypassing Luskan (the road from Mirabar cuts inland before reaching Luskan, continuing south toward Neverwinter without a Luskan branch), the city’s overland trade position has been weakening. The High Captains are aware that this is deliberate. They are unsure whether it is a punitive decision or simply a routing choice. Velin has not clarified.
8. Ship Kurth controls the primary deep-harbor docking facilities. If the Ark arrives, Kurth’s representatives will be on the dock within minutes. The encounter will be polite, transactional, and very carefully measured on both sides.
9. A persistent population of drow has been filtering into Luskan’s undercity for the past year — refugees from Lolth-disruption events in the Underdark (the goddess’s “silence” period from the City of the Spider Queen era). They are not organized as a faction yet. Jarlaxle is watching. So is the Brotherhood.
10. Luskan maintains a population of approximately 16,000 — smaller than it looks, because the city’s geography (spread across five islands connected by bridges) creates an impression of greater size. The “mainland” district (Closeguard Island and the connected shore) is the densest.
11. The Brotherhood’s intelligence file on the Sanctum is more complete than any other organization’s outside of Waterdeep’s Lords’ Alliance. They have tracked construct road-building, embassy establishments, and the Ark’s movements for three years. The file concludes with: “Do not provoke. Observe only.”
12. Luskan’s street culture is confident to the point of aggression. Violence is not common in the main market district during daytime, because the Ships all want the market to function — but the implication of potential violence is always present. Everyone is armed. Everyone notices who else is armed.
13. The Luskan orphan network — a self-organizing survival system for children with no family — is one of the city’s most functional underground institutions. The Ships occasionally recruit from it. The Brotherhood occasionally recruits from it. A Sanctum Steward who visited as part of a trade delegation three years ago made a note that this network exists and flagged it to Velin.
14. The city’s signature architectural feature: every significant building is connected to every other by a system of rope bridges and elevated wooden walkways. The original streets at ground level are cramped and dark. Life in Luskan happens at ten feet of elevation.
15. There is a functioning black market for Sanctum goods: items produced in Sanctum territory carry a quality premium that commands high prices in Luskan. Ironic that the city avoiding official Sanctum contact profits from the Sanctum’s output anyway.
16. The harbor at low tide exposes a wreck field along the eastern approach — three dozen sunken ships from various eras, the graveyard of vessels that misjudged Luskan’s currents or fell to her privateer fleets. The wrecks are dived regularly for salvage. The salvage operations are licensed by Ship Rethnor as a revenue stream.
17. Luskan has no official temples to any deity — worship is private and unorganized by design, because organized religion implies organized power that the Ships don’t control. This makes the city spiritually hollow in a way that outsiders find eerie.
18. The one exception: a small, unacknowledged shrine to Tymora (goddess of luck) that has existed in the sailors’ district for two centuries. Every captain in Luskan has touched it at least once, secretly. No Ship controls it. No Ship has tried.
19. The Arcane Brotherhood has been monitoring construct activity at the Mirabar waystation via the same Arcane Eye agent mentioned earlier. Their analysis: the constructs are more sophisticated than current Faerûn magical technology should allow. The Brotherhood wants one. They are devising a plan that Velin will find entertaining and Sera will find irritating.
20. Luskan has a tradition: when something or someone powerful arrives in the city, the High Captains each send a different, conflicting welcoming party to the dock. The goal is to determine which Ship gets first relationship with the new power. This is called “the Luskan handshake.” It is a controlled form of immediate political competition.
21. Jarlaxle already knows the girls are coming before they dock. He sent a message to Velin (somehow — no one knows how he got the embassy contact) that reads: “I look forward to the visit. Please tell Sera I have no plans to be interesting. This is, of course, a lie.” Velin has not passed this message to Sera.
22. There is a building in Luskan that no Ship claims and no Brotherhood member enters: a four-story structure in the oldest part of the city, the door sealed with a ward that predates the Brotherhood’s founding. Local legend says it belonged to the original mage who first settled Luskan. The Brotherhood has spent eighty years trying to open it. They have not succeeded.

**City Flavor (Street Level)**
Luskan smells like the sea, tar, fish, and the specific smoke of a hundred fires that no one fully controls. The walkways above the streets are the social tier — the elevated city where business happens, where the better taverns are, where you can see from one island to another and judge the distance of a ship on the horizon. Below, in the street-level dark, the city has a different rhythm: quieter, faster, more dangerous. The rope bridges sway even when there’s no wind. The harbor noise is constant — creaking hulls, shouted orders, the crack of rigging. The people of Luskan are the most observant of any city in the North: everyone here has survived by knowing who’s coming before they arrive.

**Embassy Status**
NO EMBASSY. NO WAYSTATION. The Sanctum has never formally approached Luskan for diplomatic establishment. Velin’s assessment: “The moment we try to establish there, we become one more Ship. That is not a position we want.” Current approach: invisible commercial presence (Sanctum goods in the black market), intelligence monitoring (one construct embedded as a dockworker), and observed diplomatic distance.

**Hooks & Quests (TR-08 through TR-11)**
* **TR-09:** The sealed building. Ember wants to know what’s inside. “What if something is sad in there?” The Brotherhood has tried everything. Ember’s approach will be to simply ask it if she can come in. This is either going to work immediately or reveal something the Brotherhood never accounted for.
* **TR-10:** The five High Captains perform the Luskan handshake simultaneously. Five conflicting welcoming parties at the dock. Velin has to manage which one the girls actually speak to while maintaining the other four in a holding pattern of polite delay. This is her nightmare scenario and she is absolutely prepared for it.
* **TR-11:** A drow refugee has been found in Luskan’s undercity in a condition that suggests they fled something catastrophically bad in the Underdark — not just the Lolth situation, but something newer. The refugee is not speaking. Jarlaxle is not speaking about the refugee. The Brotherhood has made a quarantine request. Someone needs to go down there.

**Parallel Threads**
* **Velin:** Running point on all five Ship contacts simultaneously, plus managing Jarlaxle’s message, plus coordinating harbor access for the Ark. She will look perfectly composed. She will not sleep for thirty-six hours after this stop.
* **Niv:** Has been asked to evaluate the tactical implications of the Brotherhood’s construct-intelligence operation. His report to Velin: “They want one. They cannot have one. The interesting question is what they will try.” He found this professionally stimulating.

**DM Notes**
Luskan is the friction point that has actual teeth. Unlike Mirabar’s political complexity, Luskan has actors who are genuinely sophisticated enough to attempt things. Jarlaxle is the key NPC — he’s the first character in the tour who is not performing fear or respect and is instead performing genuine peer-level interest. He should feel different. The Luskan handshake is a comedy beat if you want it (five competing parties, Velin’s barely-controlled chaos) or a tension beat if you play it straight. The sealed building is Ember’s beat. Let her lead it.
