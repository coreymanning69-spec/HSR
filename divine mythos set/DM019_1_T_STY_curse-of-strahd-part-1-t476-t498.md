HARD CANON RULES FOR THIS ARC:
1) The module is SETTING, not SCRIPT. Published encounters provide scaffolding; divine forces rewrite outcomes without breaking character integrity.
2) This is not a hero's journey. This is "Deus ex Machina made literal" — Capital-G Gods discover someone else's hell and decide whether it's worth noticing.
3) The horror is INVERTED. Module horror comes from helplessness; Divine Mythos horror comes from what happens when absolute power decides something matters.
4) Soliera only speaks when Corey directly provides her dialogue. She spoke ONCE in this entire arc: "Of course, Ember." — THIS OCCURS IN DM019_2, NOT THIS FILE.
5) Sera drives the narrative — she moves, asks, notices. Soliera approves. Ember complicates with innocence.
6) The power differential is absurd and intentional. Three nuclear weapons visiting a regional bully's tree fort. The tension is never "can they win?" but "what does winning look like when you ask the victims?"
7) Barovia's module mechanics (mist barriers, teleportation locks, resurrection madness, false sunlight) are CONSTRAINTS, not LAWS. Soliera operates outside these restrictions entirely. Sera and Ember can be sensed by the domain; Soliera registers as nothing.
8) Stanimir functions as the mortal lens and witness. His journal/report to Lady Voda is the historical record of what happened.
9) Corey played blind on module content — no spoilers about plot beyond what was directly encountered through play.


ARC OVERVIEW: CURSE OF STRAHD (T476–T515)
The Curse of Strahd arc represents the divine trio's intervention in a Domain of Dread — a pocket dimension in the Shadowfell ruled by Count Strahd von Zarovich, a vampire lord trapped in a four-century optimization loop of cruelty maintained by abstract cosmic entities called the Dark Powers. The arc resolves the module's entire premise in approximately two in-world days through overwhelming divine authority applied with consent-first methodology, culminating in mass resurrection, domain curse dissolution, kaiju-scale deicide, and the establishment of a permanent Sanctum portal to Barovia.

Structurally, the arc transitions from conversation-heavy early beats (feast, briefing, village encounters, Strahd dialogue) to the execution phase (resurrections, curse-breaking) and climaxes with Sera's first appropriate-scale combat since the Thayan Arc — destroying a Dark Powers manifestation the size of a mountain range in what amounts to deicide by fistfight.

The arc's core question: "What does a god do when she decides a person is worth noticing?"
The arc's answer: She asks that person what they want. Then she does it.

THIS FILE (DM019_1) covers T476–T498. See DM019_2 for T499–T515.


PRE-BAROVIA: SANCTUM PREPARATION (T476–T479)

T476: Vistani Arrival at the Sanctum #LOC-SANCTUM #CHAR-STANIMIR #CHAR-NIV-SAIFFAR #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-SOLIERA #THEME-DIVINE-DOMESTICITY
— The Vistani caravan — led by Stanimir, a court functionary disguised as merchant-storyteller — arrives at the Sanctum's northern gates claiming to carry trinkets and tales from distant lands.
— Niv-Saiffar reports their presence to the divine trio during a moment of domestic tranquility: Soliera in meditation, Sera draped across her lap in contentment, Ember absorbed in a self-reassembling puzzle.
— Sera's immediate reaction: "Soo, that means we've got some people with stuff we've never seen before?!" Her excitement is kinetic — air pressure shifts, small objects tremble.
— Soliera shifts and glances toward Sera. The communication in that glance is complete: Yes. Interesting. Go look. Don't break them.
— Ember: "Does that mean we get dressed?"
— The trio prepares to receive the Vistani in the garden courtyard rather than the throne room (reserved for threats) — a choice signaling curiosity over caution.


T477: The Garden Courtyard Meeting #LOC-SANCTUM-GARDEN-COURTYARD #CHAR-VELIN #CHAR-STANIMIR #CHAR-SERA #THEME-THEOLOGICAL-TOURISM
— Three divine figures make their entrance: Soliera in silent observation, Sera as primary interlocutor, Ember as innocent explorer.
— Velin arrives to function as theological interpreter, immediately establishing protocol: speaking freely to Soliera is a privilege most don't survive, and Stanimir should feel honored by his continued existence.
— Stanimir abandons his merchant performance when confronted by beings who read truth in clothing wear-patterns.
— He presents the actual offer: Barovia, a cursed demiplane ruled by Count Strahd von Zarovich — a vampire prince locked in four-century optimization of cruelty. The land is a prison, the people are trapped, and the Vistani alone can cross the mists.
— His hook: not the villain, but nineteen-year-old Ireena Kolyana, twice-bitten and awaiting transformation into undead consort.
— Sera's engagement shifts from predatory curiosity to genuine interest when "woman in peril" intersects her reclaimed-body autonomy axis.
— Ember's contribution: killing Strahd would be "elder abuse," establishing arbitrary care protocols that constrain divine violence through innocence rather than morality.
— Soliera turns away — smiling. Executive approval. The scenario achieves adequate entertainment value.
— The Vistani's apocalypse is greenlit as divine field trip. Salvation operates on attention economy, not moral imperative.


T478: Vistani Settled and Feast Preparation #LOC-SANCTUM #CHAR-VELIN #CHAR-NIV-SAIFFAR #CHAR-STANIMIR #CONCEPT-WITNESS-ECONOMY
— Vistani granted guest quarters, hot baths, and safety protocols (don't touch glowing objects, ignore screaming, avoid red-sigiled doors).
— Velin schedules the feast for sunset (a calendared event rather than astronomical phenomenon).
— Velin's warning to Stanimir: "When you describe Count Strahd von Zarovich to them, be accurate. Don't embellish his power to make him sound more threatening, and don't downplay it to make yourselves sound braver. They'll know. And lying to Sera is... messy."
— Intelligence briefing follows: Stanimir must explain Barovia's mechanics to beings who can probably ignore them, providing complete data rather than heroic embellishment.


T479: The Feast and Intelligence Briefing #LOC-SANCTUM #CHAR-STANIMIR #CHAR-KATJA #CHAR-PETROV #CHAR-ISMARK-THE-ELDER #CONCEPT-ABUNDANCE-NOT-SCARCITY #THEME-DIVINE-DOMESTICITY
— Evening feast in the Sanctum garden courtyard. Infrastructure focus: not gods being powerful, but what divine comfort feels like to mortals who've only known scarcity.
— Embodied experience: stone that warms instead of hurts, chairs designed for spines that don't ache, food that doesn't decay or require rationing, light that cares about being present, silence underneath noise (safety, not suppression).
— Sera sat on Soliera's lap; laughed at Ember; interrogated Stanimir about Ireena; requested juice instead of wine.
— Ember arrived first (yelling about feast through hallway); ate jelly that exploded across table; fell asleep against Soliera by evening.
— Stanimir transitioned from strategic calculation to witness mode by evening's end. Admitted Ireena has never been asked what she wants in a way that meant anything.
— Named Vistani/Barovian reactions documented: Katja (older Vistani, merchant's mind, cataloging cosmic shift), Petrov (younger, nervous, learning not to fear), Ismark the Elder (grandfather, explained Ireena's situation), gravedigger man (hands shaking from paralysis of abundance), young girl (asked if all children receive such food).
— Sera expressed confusion why Ireena "wouldn't want to come back" to the Sanctum — "She needs a life and a chance."
— Collective Vistani experience: transitioned from fear-based observation to embodied understanding of abundance; nervous systems recalibrated from scarcity-thinking to safety-baseline.
— The realization established: Power requiring scarcity is fundamentally smaller than power existing in abundance.
— Departure scheduled for morning. Most handlers remain in Sanctum (safe, fed, comfortable). Stanimir and emissaries to accompany.


DEPARTURE AND TRANSIT (T480–T485)

T480: The Knock of Adventure #LOC-SANCTUM #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-VELIN #THEME-DIVINE-DOMESTICITY
— Morning. Ember pounces — a mighty attack that Soliera simply allows to crash into her. Not catches, not braces for. Allows. The distinction is theological.
— Ember hugged Soliera tight, which ensured Sera was close behind on the dog pile. The trio laid in bed for a moment, relaxing and giggling. Ember was very pleased with herself.
— A single knock before the door opened. Velin entered right away — she knew she was welcome, otherwise she wouldn't have knocked.
— Velin stands over the trio, inlaid in a huge circular bed. The silk and cotton blended with thousands of different fibers — each prayed over by a worker, honestly thankful for the privilege to create for their Goddess — made a beautiful red sea.
— Velin grins: "Sera, I'm surprised you're not already dressed!"
— Ember interjects with laughter: "Yeah, I jumped on Soli! Totally ruined Sera's routine! Hahah!"
— Sera rises with purpose — dressing the group and starting the morning proper.
— Velin provides briefing on Barovia's mist mechanics: mortals can't leave once they enter, those who try circle back. Vistani have a pact — they cross freely. Domain approximately thirty miles across, mountain-ringed, perpetually overcast. Self-contained. Strahd's the centerpiece.
— Ember: "So we just... walk into fog and end up in a prison?"
— Velin: "The mists may not register you properly. Mortals experience disorientation, memory displacement, temporal distortion. You three... unknown variables."
— Sera, already moving toward wardrobe: "Sounds fun."


T481–T483: Departure from the Sanctum #LOC-SANCTUM #CHAR-NIV-SAIFFAR #CHAR-STANIMIR #CONCEPT-LOGISTICS-TRAVEL
Beat Ledger:
T481: Departure logistics lock; Niv-Saiffar and the handlers see the party out from the Sanctum gate.
T482: The party crosses from Sanctum abundance into Barovia-bound travel, with Vistani wagons as the mortal frame.
T483: Escort roles settle; the mists become visible ahead and the domain threshold becomes the next problem.

— Niv-Saiffar walked with them to the northern gate. "Safe travels. We'll maintain operations in your absence."
— Soliera's smile — faint but present. Permission and trust made visible.
— Vistani wagons waited beyond the gate: three covered carriages, horses harnessed, drivers checking supplies.
— Sera immediately claimed the second wagon, pulling Ember up with her. "Road trip!"
— Soliera settled onto the same wagon with fluid grace — didn't need to check if seats existed; they always did. Sera immediately sprawled half across her lap.
— Handlers lined the gate: Katja still writing in her journal, Petrov waving, Anya holding her mother's hand. Watching until wagons turned the corner.
— Tunnel sloped upward, torchlight giving way to natural dawn as they emerged onto the mountain path.
— Ahead — visible on the horizon — a wall of gray fog that didn't move with the wind. The mists of Barovia, waiting.


T484: The Road to the Mists #LOC-BAROVIA-MISTS #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-STANIMIR #THEME-DIVINE-DOMESTICITY
— Two hours into the journey. Sera achieved architectural sprawl — started sitting upright, progressed through every possible configuration of limbs across the wagon bench, currently lying on her back with arms spread wide, head resting against Soliera's hip, white-gold fingers threading through dark hair.
— Ember sat opposite, asking questions about the landscape, the road, the history of the Vistani. Stanimir provided context from the lead wagon.
— Conversation pieces along the journey: Ember curious about the fog, asking Stanimir about what it feels like. Sera making observations about the geography. Soliera silent, observing.
— The atmosphere changed as they approached. Trees thinned. Color drained from the landscape. The air got colder, heavier. Something in the environment shifted from "natural terrain" to "maintained environment."
— Sera sat up. Not alarmed — interested. The kind of attention she paid to things that were about to become her problem.
— Ahead: the wall of fog, now enormous, stretching across the entire road and into the treeline on both sides. Dense, gray, opaque. Not natural mist — structural. The boundary of a domain.


T485: The Mists Bow #LOC-BAROVIA-MISTS #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-EMBER #CONCEPT-JURISDICTION-TRUMPS-DOMAIN
— Wagons approached the mist wall. Stanimir explained the Vistani passage rights — their pact allows free crossing.
— Soliera walked forward. The mists... hesitated. Then parted. Not because they chose to — because Soliera walked here, and the domain barrier recognized higher authority.
— A bubble of clarity formed around the caravan. Within it, the fog was transparent, breathable, unthreatening. Outside the bubble, dense gray wall continued.
— A shape formed in the mist — tall, vaguely humanoid, composed of fog itself. It raised one hand, palm outward — the exact same gesture Soliera had made. Reflected. Recognized. Returned.
— The figure bowed — deep, formal, absolute deference — and dissolved back into undifferentiated fog.
— Sera stared: "Did you just get saluted by a domain barrier?"
— Soliera's mouth curved. Something more complex than a smile. The universe recognizing its own.
— Ember: "That's so cool. Can I learn to do that?"
— Sera: "Probably not. That wasn't magic. That was jurisdiction. The mist recognized Soli as higher authority and adjusted accordingly."
— She shook her head, grinning: "Strahd is going to hate us."
— The road became clearer. The mist parted slightly ahead, almost deferential, offering directions. And ahead — wooden buildings, stone walls, the outline of a village.
— Barovia.


THE VILLAGE OF BAROVIA (T486–T494)

T486: Arrival at the Village of Barovia #LOC-BAROVIA-VILLAGE #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-STANIMIR #CHAR-EMBER #THEME-GOTHIC-HORROR-GENRE-INVERSION
— Sera launched herself toward the lead wagon, grabbing the side and hopping onto the running board. "Is that it? That's Barovia? The village?"
— Stanimir: "Yes. The village of Barovia. Maybe a hundred residents. Less, now, since..."
— Sera, bluntly: "Since the vampire's been snacking on people. Yeah."
— The village was a study in Gothic exhaustion: gray stone buildings, shuttered windows, empty streets. Everything designed to endure suffering, not resist it.
— The fountain in the center of the village square — long dry per module canon, environmental storytelling for "this place is dying" — began to flow with clean water as Soliera's wagon entered the square. Passive reality restructuring. No gesture, no command. Her presence is infrastructure.
— Villagers appeared in doorways. Staring. Not at the Vistani (familiar) but at the three figures who radiated wrongness on every axis: Sera's beauty that made your teeth ache, Ember's literal incandescence, and Soliera's terrifying absence of detectable anything.
