# ITHREVA SAZHER — LICH OF MYRKUL

#CHAR-ITHREVA-SAZHER #CHAR-MYRKUL #CHAR-TARNETH-SAZHER #ITEM-THE-ADJOURNMENT #ITEM-LEDGER-OF-ATTENDANCE #THREAD-THAYAN-REMNANT #THREAD-PHYLACTERY

## WHY MYRKUL CARES

Myrkul holds the dead and he holds dread. He does not hold the act of killing —
that belongs to Bhaal, and the distinction matters, because Ithreva is not a
murderer by vocation. She is a clerk. What Myrkul lost is not corpses. It is the
*weight* of them.

The Sanctum made loss recoverable. Resurrection is available, the Open Door is
open, and the ordinary catastrophic fact of a family losing someone has been
converted into a scheduling problem. Simultaneously, Sanctum protection removed
the things people were afraid of — the raiders, the dragons, the sea, the giants
where the Sanctum chose to intervene. Between the two, fear stopped being
load-bearing in the world. Nobody propitiates what cannot reach them and nobody
mourns what can be returned.

That is a portfolio collapse, and it is the second one Myrkul has suffered. He
lost the office of the dead once already and has never gotten it back. He is not
angry in the way Bane is angry. He is a creditor watching an account go to zero
and looking for someone still solvent.

He does not act directly. He funds.

## THE DISPLACEMENT CHAIN

The chain runs clean and any high-INT character can derive it without being
told:

Sanctum resurrection and the Open Door make death recoverable. Recoverable death
is not dreadful. Sanctum field protection removes ambient predation. A world
without ambient predation does not generate dread as a byproduct. Dread is
Myrkul's product. Product demand collapses. The god cannot compel demand back
into existence at scale, because the Sanctum's coverage is structural rather
than local — so the only path left is to manufacture scarcity by hand, in a
place the Sanctum has not yet reached, at a scale large enough to be *heard
about*.

That last clause is the whole design. Ithreva does not need to win. She needs
the story to travel. A stone giant clan turned into a walking atrocity within
reach of the most productive farmland in the North is not a military objective.
It is an advertisement.

## WHY ITHREVA

Because the Sanctum already took everything from her, and she is the only one
left who can do the arithmetic.

At Eltabbar the triad erased the temples of Shar, Myrkul, and Bane, broke the
Red Wizard command, and killed Tarneth Sazher in his own hall — slowly, and on
purpose, as a lesson. Ithreva was abroad. She came back to a city under new
management, an order that no longer existed, a god's house reduced to absence,
and a kinsman who had spent his last breath begging Shar for help that never
came.

That last detail is load-bearing to her. Tarneth called the wrong name. He had
served power his whole life and at the end he reached for the goddess of
oblivion instead of the god who actually keeps the dead, and he got nothing,
because the Sanctum had already closed the sky over that city. Ithreva's
lichdom is a correction of her cousin's error as much as it is ambition. She
will not be forgotten, she will not be recoverable, and she will not be reaching
for anyone at the end.

She is, by the terms of DM038_1d, exactly what that file describes: a lich of
the Szass Tam remnant structure, a former holder of a title that no longer
exists, reaching out to destabilized giant lords and raising infrastructure out
of the ordning's collapse.

## HOW SHE READS ON THE GROUND

Not red. She left Red Wizard crimson with the order that failed to survive one
afternoon. Bone-ash grey, wrapped close and layered like a records-keeper's
coat, cut for movement rather than ceremony. The tattoos are still there
underneath and she deliberately does not cover them — the pedigree is the point,
and she wants anyone educated enough to read them to know exactly what house is
standing in the room.

Myrkul's mark is small, precise, and worn at the collar rather than displayed.
She is not a preacher. Skulls do not hang off her.

The horror of her is that she is *tidy*. She does not rave, she does not gloat
at length, and she does not enjoy the screaming in the way Bhaal's people do.
She takes attendance. She writes names down. When a giant dies in front of her
she notes it, and the note is the offensive part, because it means the death was
expected and accounted for before it happened. Everyone in the canyon is a line
item and she has already balanced the page.

Her voice register is dry, unhurried, faintly courteous. She will answer a
direct question honestly. She will not stop working while she does it.

## THE KIT

### The Adjournment — carried, right hand

A staff of grey bone banded in iron, unadorned. Myrkul's grant, and the reason
she survives contact with a Sanctum field pair.

**Harvest.** Whenever a creature dies within 60 feet of Ithreva, she gains 10
temporary hit points. These stack and persist for the duration of the encounter,
to a maximum of 200. In an engagement where a clan is being culled around her,
this is not incidental — it is her second health bar, and the pair fills it
themselves.

**Not Yet (reaction, once per round).** When Ithreva would take damage from a
single source, she may reduce that instance to 0. Doing so costs her 30
temporary hit points from Harvest; if she does not have 30 temporary hit points,
she cannot use it. She pays for her survival out of the harvest, which means
burst damage early — before the room has generated corpses — is the correct
counterplay and the pair will not know that on the first round.

Stripping or sundering the staff ends both functions and any temporary hit
points already accumulated remain but stop growing.

### The Ledger of Attendance — carried, left hand, never set down

A thick codex bound in something that is politely not identified. It is a
register of the dead: every name Ithreva has taken, written in her own hand, in
Myrkul's ledger discipline. Naming is what holds a raised thing together.

**Attendance.** While the Ledger is intact and in her possession, each of her
Named Dead gains +2 to attack rolls and deals an additional 2d6 necrotic damage
on a hit.

**Re-entry.** At the start of Ithreva's turn, any Named Dead destroyed since her
last turn re-forms at full hit points in an unoccupied space within 30 feet of
her. This is the summons cycle and it does not stop while the book exists.

**The book as an objective.** AC 18, 80 hit points, immunity to poison and
psychic damage, vulnerability to fire. Destroying it ends Re-entry permanently
and strips the Attendance bonus. Ithreva will defend it before she defends
herself, and she can be baited by that. This is deliberate: it gives Wren a job
in the fight that is not a damage race, and it gives the pair a reason to split
that the encounter itself creates.

**Turn Resistance.** Advantage on saves against any effect that turns undead.
**Rejuvenation.** If destroyed, her body reforms in 1d10 days near her

**Current state.** The rejuvenation interval has passed. Ithreva is physically

### Fixed encounter suite

Distance-covering, area-denying, and built to hurt rather than finesse.

**Blood Darts (action).** Five darts, each a ranged spell attack at +15 against
targets within 120 feet, 4d6 necrotic on a hit. Ithreva gains temporary hit
points equal to half the total damage dealt, counting against the Harvest cap.
This is her default turn when nothing better is available and it never runs out.

**Poison Air (action).** A 40-foot-radius cloud centered on a point within 120
feet, heavily obscuring the area. Each creature that starts its turn inside
makes a DC 23 Constitution save, taking 8d6 poison damage on a failure and half
on a success. The cloud persists for 1 minute and drifts 10 feet toward the
nearest living creature at the start of each of her turns. It does not require
concentration.

**Heatwave (action).** A 90-foot cone. DC 23 Dexterity save, 12d6 fire damage on
a failure and half on a success. Flammable terrain ignites. Intended to punish
the pair for standing anywhere near the thralls or each other.

**Called to Account (action, recharge 5-6).** Each creature of Ithreva's choice
within 60 feet makes a DC 23 Charisma save. On a failure, the creature spends
its next turn standing before the Ledger — it loses its action and bonus action,
though it may still move. *Wren is immune; her categorical immunity to
mind-altering effects applies without exception.* Against Doran this is the
action-denial that makes a caster survivable, and against Wren it is nothing at
all, and Ithreva will register that fact visibly the first time it fails.

**Standard 20th-level access.** Counterspell, wall of force, finger of death,
circle of death, cloudkill, contingency, and the rest of the necromancer's
catalog are available. Use them; the suite above is her signature, not her
ceiling.

### The Named Dead — summons

Two are active at all times. They act on Ithreva's initiative, attack the
nearest living non-thrall creature without instruction, and require no
concentration, no command, and no upkeep. They do not stop.

**AC** 16 · **HP** 90 · **Speed** 30 ft
**Immunities** poison, necrotic; charmed, exhaustion, frightened, poisoned
Two claw attacks, +11 to hit, 14 (2d8+5) slashing, plus 2d6 necrotic while the
Ledger is intact (+2 to hit from Attendance included in the listed bonus when
the book is in play).

They re-form at the start of Ithreva's turn while the Ledger stands. Killing
them is not progress. Killing the book is progress.

### Legendary and reaction package

**Legendary Resistance (3/day).** If Ithreva fails a saving throw, she may
choose to succeed instead. Three uses, per day, matching the champion band.

**Legendary actions (3 points).** She regains all points at the start of her
turn and may spend them at the end of another creature's turn.

- **Dart (1 point).** One Blood Dart attack.
- **Corpse-step (1 point).** She teleports up to 60 feet to an unoccupied space
  she can see that is within 5 feet of a corpse. A caster who cannot leave melee
  is a dead caster; this is how she stays a problem.
- **Call a Name (2 points).** One destroyed Named Dead re-forms immediately
  rather than waiting for her turn. This is what keeps the summons pressure
  continuous rather than turn-gated.

**Not Yet (reaction).** As the Adjournment, above. Once per round, and it costs
harvest.

## ENCOUNTER POSTURE

**Effective durability.** 700 base, plus up to 200 from Harvest in a room where
a clan is dying, is a 900-point ceiling — deliberately level with the Ordinant's
forward-authoritative package. The difference is that the Ordinant's pool is
front-loaded and hers is *earned during the fight, by the pair's own work*. She
gets tougher the deeper in they go.

**Against Doran** (AC 25, 370 HP, initiative +21, WIS +11, INT +10, CHA +4).
He will find her and he will out-damage her; that is not in question. Called to
Account is the only thing in her kit that meaningfully costs him a turn, and
Corpse-step is the only thing that keeps her out of his reach. She survives him
by paying — harvest into Not Yet, legendary resistance into whatever Wren lands
— and by making the room expensive enough that reaching her takes rounds.

**Against Wren** (220 HP, ward 75 with overflow nullification, DC 22).
Legendary Resistance is not a general Wren counter: it triggers only when
Ithreva herself fails a saving throw. Wren's attack-roll damage asks for none,
and an attended object's failure does not belong to Ithreva. At T549, ten
maximized, empowered *scorching ray* attacks landed with no save; the Ledger
failed its own CON check while Ithreva saved against the same Portal Shear.
Ithreva kept all three resistances and lost the book. Her dread, compulsion, and
Called to Account also slide straight off Wren's Mind Lock.

**The moral trap.** Doran solves a v2 stone giant's 326 hit points in about a
round and a half. Every giant he solves is a victim. The failure state of this
fight is not losing — it is winning cleanly and efficiently and then finding out
in the aftermath what the intact ones were. The free-willed giants, the ones who
can speak, are the ones Ithreva broke; they are alive because they were made
useless. There is no clean run of this encounter and the file does not provide
one.

**Do not** add hit points to fix a pacing problem. The levers are the thrall
bodies, the Ledger objective, Poison Air on the approach lanes, and the harvest
economy. Those are the encounter.

## THRALL BODIES — CERTIFIED, T543

Three v2 thrall stone giants were run live against Doran and Wren at Goldenfields. Findings, for the twenty still standing.

**The bodies are correctly costed and should not be touched.** One v2 stone giant is approximately one full round of Doran's output and no more. He produced 244 on a plain five-swing routine, 274 with a Trip Attack opening and prone advantage, and 316 across a nine-swing Action Surge burst that killed on the seventh. Twenty of them is therefore twenty rounds of Doran uninterrupted, which is the actual arc arithmetic and is exactly the pacing problem the ENCOUNTER POSTURE section says to solve with the ledger and the summons rather than with hit points. It holds.

**They are correctly dangerous to Wren and not to Doran.** They hit AC 18 reliably at +10 and could not touch AC 25 in eight attempts. The rock's DC 18 STR rider is the sharpest thing in the block: Wren's Strength save is +3, her only shallow number, she does not get her own Liberation auras, and the Robe of the Archmagi covers spells and magical effects but not a thrown rock. One rock removed her from flight and put her prone in her own fire. That is the correct asymmetry and it should be used deliberately in the main engagement.

**Area damage is a losing trade against them and this should be allowed to bite.** A twinned, empowered *fireball* cost six sorcery points and a third-level slot and produced twenty-six and twenty-one points against two 326-HP bodies. Wren's value in this encounter is control, extraction, and the environment, not damage, and the runtime does not need to be adjusted to teach that — the numbers teach it.

**The environment is the real cost lever.** The fireball ignited standing late-season harvest and burned roughly ninety feet of frontage before she stopped it. At a site whose entire significance is that it feeds the North, incidental Sanctum area damage is more expensive than anything the giants did. Design the main engagement so this recurs.

**No carry-through kills occurred.** Grand Cleave was stopped by the first Huge body per §3 of its resolution, so §4's destruction clause did not fire and the body remained intact. All three bodies are recoverable and inside the *raise dead* window. **A raised thrall returns free** — [RULED 2026-09-05], closing this gate. That is precisely why Ithreva does not use raising as a tool: it hands the subject back their own will and undoes the hold she paid for. Revival itself carries no drama on the Sanctum side; see `DM041_0` REVIVAL IS ORDINARY.

## DESIGN SEAMS — GM SIDE

The **first-round trap** is intentional. Not Yet requires 30 accumulated
temporary hit points, so on round one Ithreva is at her most fragile and cannot
buy her way out. A pair that opens hard on her, before the room has produced
corpses, will do real damage. A pair that clears thralls first is filling her
second health bar for her. Neither the file nor Ithreva will explain this.

The **Ledger bait** is a real vulnerability, not a fake one. She will move to
protect the book and she can be pulled out of position by threatening it. Let
that work.

The **advertisement objective** means she does not need to hold the site. If the
fight turns decisively, her win condition is already banked — the atrocity
happened, it will be talked about, and dread was manufactured. Play her exit
accordingly. She does not beg and she does not bargain.

**Retinue.** Roughly twenty enthralled stone giants at v2 scale, thirty-plus
remaining Thayan agents including Red Wizards, and knight-tier defenders in the
eighth-level band. None of the defenders parley. Recruits and camp followers
may. The Sanctum clears the site, kills the agents, and reclaims the holdings.

## CLOSED GATES

Identity, lineage, patron, displacement chain, kit, runtime numbers, legendary
package, thrall doctrine, seat at Goldenfields, phylactery placement, roster
admission, and certification are settled. Goldenfields is released; all 131
evacuees and both missing grain workers were recovered alive. **The hold's**
eventual liberation mechanism and the in-absentia theory are not backfilled, and
neither is a forward route. Read that literally: this is the mechanism by which
*the hold* is freed, first left open in `DM002_3` after the north-road fight. It
has nothing to do with Wren's Liberation domain, and any "Liberation answer"
phrased as an open question about her domain is a summarizing artifact with no
referent — closed as a non-gate, 2026-09-05. The live residue is small and not a
blocker: does the hold free itself, or does it need the Sanctum to return? Deadstone Cleft and residual stone threats are contained
institutionally by permanent Sanctum constructs.
