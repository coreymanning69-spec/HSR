# M126 — GRID 7

Grid 7 was not a place that expected to be walked through on purpose.

It had the specific character of neighborhoods that had been left to solve themselves — the kind of dense, crooked infrastructure that forms when people need to live somewhere and no one with resources has decided to help them do it. Buildings leaned against each other out of structural necessity. Wiring looped overhead in slack, improvised arcs, some of it live, some of it long dead, most of it impossible to tell apart from the outside. The alleys were narrow enough that two people walking side by side was a negotiation.

# M127 — RISA RETURNS

Risa moved through it the way people move through the place they grew up — which is to say without looking at it too hard, because looking at it hard meant seeing all the things that had gotten worse since she left. The corner where the water pipe had cracked three years ago and nobody had fixed it. The building on the east side of Alley 9 that was held up by the building on the west side, both of them too tired to fall. The soup vendor's cart that had the same broken wheel it had always had and always would.

She hadn't been back since she'd gotten the Company job. The job was not much, but it was enough to live on the other side of the wall that separated Grid 7 from the parts of the city that had functioning electrical maintenance.

She led them in anyway.

Behind her, Ember was doing what Ember did: looking at everything. A stack of crates got a thorough examination. A mural painted across a crumbling wall — someone's stylized idea of a better city, done in four colors of spray paint by someone who had a good eye and not much else — got a long, tilted-head look that suggested she was trying to decide whether she recognized the technique.

Soliera walked behind all of it, quiet, Her bare feet finding the alley floor as though the cracked stone and scattered debris had simply agreed to be smooth for Her. She did not look at the broken pipe or the leaning building or the dead wiring. She looked at the people: a woman hanging laundry from a third-floor window, a cluster of children frozen mid-game, a very old man sitting in a doorway who tracked the procession with eyes that had seen too many things to be easily surprised and were working very hard right now.

The alley got narrower. The people in it got quieter.

Not afraid — or not only afraid. There was something else moving through the grid ahead of them, faster than they were walking: the news. That something had come off the main drag into Grid 7. That it was walking this way. That you should probably see this for yourself.

# M128 — SLUM ECOSYSTEM RESPONSE

The ecosystem responded the way slum ecosystems do when something significant enters their space — not through any organized decision, but through distributed, immediate, self-interested reflex. Goblins working a corner scatter-checked and decided to be somewhere else without discussing it. Watchers on balconies went still and stayed still, tracking. Message runners who had been moving on other business found that their routes now had a new priority. Somewhere deeper in the grid, the signals that the theft network used to communicate passed along: hold. In the corporate tower feeds upstairs, sensors began pinging anomalies in Grid 7 that nobody in the monitoring room had a good category for.

Ember was delighted by all of it. She moved through it in predator-joy mode — not threatening, not hunting, but with the bright, forward energy of something that finds the world continuously interesting and has no reason to hide that. She was already ahead of Risa.

Sera walked behind her and to the side, hands loose, watching the balconies and the corners with the relaxed attention of someone whose perimeter is always clear because everything inside it has already made its decisions.

By the time they turned into the deeper channel of Alley 12, the balconies above had people on them.


M130–M133: TEHILUM

# M130 — TEHILUM AT THE WALL

He was sitting at the base of a wall.

Not doing anything. Not waiting for anything specific. Just there, the way people sit in the absence of anywhere better to be — spine against the crumbling plaster, knees loose, hands resting on the ground beside him because there wasn't anywhere useful to put them. He was a cat-man: the ears came through as flattened, one bent at a permanent wrong angle, the fur on his forearms patchy where something old and untreated had left its marks. His clothes were falling apart at the seams in the way that meant they had been repaired as far as they could be repaired and then given up on.

He was looking at the ground when Ember found him.

# M131 — EMBER CALLS OUT

"Hiii!" she called out, bright and easy, waving both hands. "Come here!"

He looked up.

For a moment, the specific problem of what he was looking at did not fully register. His eyes moved across the group — the girls, Amara, Risa — and landed on Ember, who was beaming at him with the absolute, uncomplicated goodwill of something that has never once in its existence sorted beings into categories of worth.

Then he scrambled to his feet and pressed himself back against the wall.

Sera watched him. Her head tilted a fraction. Not threatening — assessing. She stayed where she was, easy, waiting.

Amara came up beside Ember, leaned down slightly.

"People here are mixes," she said, quietly, meant for Ember first. "Half this, half that. All kinds. He's a gentleman."

He looked at Sera, and then at the figure behind Sera, and something in his chest that had been clenched for a very long time did something complicated and uncertain that he didn't have a word for.

He couldn't look directly at Soliera for very long.

Not out of fear. It was more like trying to look at a window with the sun directly behind it — your eyes adjusted eventually, but for the first few seconds there was just light, just warmth, and the knowledge that something was there.

# M132 — THE GLANCE

Soliera looked at him.

It was not a long look. It wasn't even a deliberate look, exactly — just Her gaze passing over him the way Her gaze passed over everything, with that quality of full attention that meant whatever She saw, She saw entirely. And what She saw was: bent ear, absent incisor, fur dull and patchy where it should have been clean and full, a body running on less than it needed and had needed for longer than was reasonable.

The looking lasted perhaps two seconds.

The bent ear straightened. Not slowly — not with any visible process — just: wrong, and then not wrong, the cartilage remembering its correct shape with the quiet certainty of something returning to its natural state. The absent incisor filled the gap it had left. The fur on his forearms came clean, the patches filling in, the sheen returning.

He didn't feel it happening. He felt what came after.

Which was: a sudden, bewildering lightness in the body. The low-grade ache that had lived so long in his jaw that he'd stopped registering it as ache and had started registering it as simply how his jaw felt — gone. A tightness behind his eyes that he would have described, if asked, as just the way the light always was — gone. Things that had been wrong for so long that he had filed them under permanent, under just me, under this is how it is.

He put his hand to his jaw. Ran his tongue over the place where the tooth had been.

His legs stopped working correctly. He sat down against the wall again, not by choice, and his eyes were doing the thing eyes do when they've decided that whatever is happening is too large for normal processing and have rerouted to something older and simpler.

Ember immediately started toward him.

"He's happier!" she said, delighted, already closing the distance with both hands out in the universal gesture of someone who wants to touch something interesting. "Can I touch him? Amara, he's different now."

"Gently," Amara said, close behind her. "He's had a surprise."

Ember crouched in front of him — she had to bring herself down to his level because he was against the wall and not going anywhere — and held her palm open, the way she'd learned worked better than reaching, letting him decide.

He looked at her hand. He looked at her face. He looked at her hand again.

He put his arm out slowly, like a man who has learned that most things that seem like offers are not, and is deciding, just this once, to find out.

Her hand came down on his forearm, palm flat, warm.

He did not burn. He had not expected to survive not burning.

He made a sound that was not quite a word and not quite anything else, and then he was crying, in the particular way of people who have not cried in a very long time and have forgotten that it was allowed.

Ember sat back on her heels, looked at him, looked at Amara.
