### LOCATION 10: BALDUR’S GATE (City of Blood)
**[MAP POSITION]** Southern Sword Coast, south of Waterdeep along the Coast Way. Large port city at the mouth of the Chionthar River.
**[STATUS]** FIRST VISIT
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** The Ark (sea route south from Waterdeep, the most dramatic arrival). Coast Way road (three days from Waterdeep).
**[WORD OF MOUTH]** Regional/commercial version. Baldur’s Gate knows the Sanctum and the trio by name. The Flaming Fist maintains an intelligence file and the Duke council has discussed them. Public versions are theatrical and commercial; institutional versions are more accurate.

**Setting Changes (20+)**
1. Baldur’s Gate is the largest commercial city on the Sword Coast south of Waterdeep. Its commercial culture is explicitly amoral: everyone trades with everyone; reputation for deal-completion matters more than reputation for virtue.
2. The city is structurally divided: Upper City (nobles, wealthy, clean streets, strict gate access), Lower City (commerce, working population, chaos managed by Flaming Fist patrols), Outer City (slums, non-citizen population, refugee communities). Moving between tiers requires documentation, money, or knowing the right people.
3. The Flaming Fist — the mercenary company that polices Baldur’s Gate — has an intelligence arm. They have files on the Sanctum. Their assessment: “Unknown military capability. Extreme economic reach. No hostile activity on record. OBSERVE AND DO NOT ENGAGE.” The file is updated quarterly. The quarterly update is always the same sentence.
4. Baldur’s Gate’s Duke council does not know how to categorize the Sanctum. They’ve discussed it at three closed sessions. The current working category is “foreign power, unusual nature.” This is accurate and tells them nothing useful.
5. The Chionthar River ferry system — connecting the city’s harbor mouth to interior river traffic — is the commercial backbone of the southern Sword Coast. Goods from the entire Western Heartlands funnel through here. Any entity that wants to influence southern Faerûn’s economy eventually has to deal with Baldur’s Gate’s ferry market.
6. The Outer City contains the most diverse population in the Realms: refugees from every conflict in the past thirty years, plus longer-term immigrant communities from Calimshan, Amn, the Dalelands, and further. The Sanctum’s reputation in the Outer City is stronger than anywhere else in the city — because the Outer City’s population travels widely and hears more stories.
7. There is a community in the Outer City called the Rivington Ward that has, based entirely on second-hand accounts of Sanctum activity, begun organizing along Sanctum civic principles: cooperative labor, shared resource management, community building projects. They have never had contact with the Sanctum. They built a rough approximation of a Sanctum model from stories. It’s working better than anything the city officially provides.
8. The Flaming Fist commander (Marshall Ravengard, or his successor depending on political events) has a professional interest in the girls that is not quite fear and not quite admiration. He wants to know if they can be hired. He knows they can’t. He wants to know if they can be allied. He knows that’s the wrong framework too. He fundamentally wants to know what they want, because everything he understands about power centers on motivation and he cannot read theirs.
9. The Guild (Baldur’s Gate’s criminal underworld, operating as a de facto fourth governance tier) has discussed the Sanctum in the context of competition for Outer City populations. The Guild provides protection, employment (of a kind), and social structure to people the city ignores. If the Sanctum becomes active in Baldur’s Gate, the Guild’s social position in the Outer City becomes complicated.
10. Candlekeep — the great library fortress on a peninsula southwest of the city — is the closest major institution to Baldur’s Gate and operates as a cultural counterweight to the city’s pure commercialism. Candlekeep has sent scholars to every significant Sanctum-adjacent event in the past decade and has the most accurate academic record of divine Mythos activity outside the Vault of Sages.
11. The city’s harbor district operates at a commercial velocity that makes Waterdeep’s docks look leisurely. Thousands of ships per year. Commodities traded by the ton by mid-morning. The harbor master’s office runs three shifts.
12. Baldur’s Gate has a specific street food: a flatbread folded around spiced meat and pickled vegetables, sold from street carts at every major intersection. It costs one copper. It is consumed by every economic tier of the city. When the girls arrive, Ember will encounter her first one within five minutes and it will be a significant moment.
13. The city maintains an unusual relationship with Elturel to the north: Elturel is the militantly virtuous city; Baldur’s Gate is the pragmatically commercial city. They require each other economically and resent each other culturally. Since Elturel’s very specific historical crisis (it was literally dragged to Avernus), the political relationship has been complicated and the cultural resentment has been replaced by something more complicated.
14. A section of the harbor — the Briny Depths district — is controlled by a mercantile family called the Bhallars who have been operating in Baldur’s Gate for six generations. The Bhallars are legitimate. They are also the most effective intelligence network in the city, collecting commercial information as a byproduct of their trade operations. They know more about what moves through Baldur’s Gate than the Flaming Fist does.
15. The city’s noble families (those with Upper City residence) have watched the Sanctum’s commercial activity with the specific concern of people who have built their position on controlling information and access. The Sanctum’s road infrastructure and construct distribution networks represent a form of commerce that bypasses the control points these families have spent generations building.
16. There is a theater in the Lower City that has been performing a play about “the Northern Gods” for eight months. The play is part accurate (it gets the broad events right), part embellished (it gives Sera a dramatic villain speech at Tiamat’s defeat that she definitely did not give), and entirely entertaining. It’s the most popular show in the city. The playwright has never been north of Daggerford.
17. Baldur’s Gate’s law operates on a tiered system: Upper City law (strict, enforced by city watch), Lower City law (Flaming Fist patrol, more flexible), Outer City “law” (effectively none — Flaming Fist rarely ventures there). The Sanctum’s civic model (consistent law, construct enforcement, no economic tier of protection) is philosophically antithetical to Baldur’s Gate’s structure. This will create interesting conversations.
18. A Harper cell operating in Baldur’s Gate has been specifically assigned to monitor the girls’ travel arc. They are in contact with Elara in Waterdeep. They are trying to stay ahead of the itinerary. They are two days behind and getting further behind because they didn’t account for the Ark’s travel speed.
19. The docks have a ghost: a woman in merchant clothes who has been seen on the south pier every third night for thirty years. She is not hostile. She points at the water. She has been pointing at the same spot for thirty years. Three separate dock investigations have found nothing at that location. The spot is exactly where a ship went down in 1472 DR carrying a specific cargo that has never been publicly disclosed.
20. The Outer City’s Rivington Ward, when the girls arrive, will send a delegation of two: an elderly woman named Mira who organized the community, and a twelve-year-old boy named Daveth who was elected to go because “he’s the best talker.” They want to show the girls what they built. They are nervous and trying not to show it.

**Hooks & Quests (TR-29 through TR-32)**
* **TR-29:** Rivington Ward. Mira and Daveth’s meeting with the girls. The community they built is functional and genuine. What does the Sanctum offer people who have already built something that works?Acknowledgment?Resources?Formal structure?The answer to this question will be decided in the session.
* **TR-30:** The theater play. Someone tells Sera about her villain speech. She wants to see it performed. This is not negotiable. The resulting session beat (Sera watching actors perform a version of herself) is pure character material.
* **TR-31:** The ghost on the pier. Ember notices her immediately. “She’s pointing at something. We should look.” The cargo on the 1472 ship: classified Flaming Fist documents related to an incident that Marshall Ravengard’s predecessor buried. The documents still exist, preserved by whatever keeps the ghost pointing.
* **TR-32:** The Guild’s response to the girls’ visit. Not hostile — the Guild is smart enough not to be hostile. But they want to understand what the Sanctum intends for Outer City populations. This is a direct conversation between Sanctum values and criminal-social-support values that should not resolve cleanly.
