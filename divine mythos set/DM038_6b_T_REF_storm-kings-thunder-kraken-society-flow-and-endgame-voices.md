### Sansuri — Sanctum-Aligned Conch Holder

Sansuri is allied with the Sanctum through a transactional bargain, not through
devotion or complete disclosure. She wants knowledge, wealth, and status; the
Sanctum is the highest available source of all three. She therefore gives Niv
the Conch and treats access to the Sanctum as the best bargain available to her.
She remains a possible route for Sanctum influence over the wider Giants, but
that route does not replace legitimate giant authority.

Sansuri knows that the Thayan remnant approached her. She does **not** know that
the remnant is loosely allied with the Drow and the Kraken Society. Those three
forces operate mostly concurrently rather than as one tightly coordinated
command. Their convergence is intentional endgame structure: the separate
fronts build toward the later multi-front boss and wave fight. Wren and Doran
have not yet seen King Hekaton; the Maelstrom and Hekaton route remains playable.

### Grand Dame

**Arrival beat:** The Grand Dame is social camouflage, not the prison. The riverboat should tempt the DM to play intrigue, but the Divineverse function is narrower: locate and expose the Kraken Society operational face without confusing the lead site for the holding site.

**Investigation shape:** Let mortal procedures matter: invitations, Golden Goose chips, tables, staff doors, private rooms, ledgers, and Drylund's social control. Doran and Wren own this layer and may fumble it. The scene must preserve a fail-forward path to the minimum handoff packet — Morkoth, its patrol waters near the Purple Rocks, and Tholtz Daggerdark — through Drylund, cabin records, staff testimony, or the Society's logistical trail.

**Critical moment - Drylund:** Khaspere Drylund is the executor of Hekaton's imprisonment chain. He is willing, informed, and profiting from proximity to power. He can reveal the handoff packet or attempt to bargain. Slarkrethel's telepathic kill remains available as source structure, but it lands after enough of the packet is spoken or recoverable; the death confirms the hierarchy without eating the clue.

**Resolution shape:** Grand Dame compromised. The Morkoth handoff packet is complete and a route to interception exists. Drylund's fate and any changed clue element route to the active story ledger. The Grand Dame remains distinct from the Morkoth in all notes.


### Finding the Morkoth (the search)

**Travel encounter, not the prison itself:** Source-verified: before the Morkoth is reached, the Trackless Sea search surfaces a specific roster of other vessels and crews — the actual texture of "search / interception" in the Kraken Society Flow table below. None of these are the prison ship. Use as many or as few as the search needs.

**Mixed roster, not a monolith:** **Zaldar Floshin** (half-elf mage, NG), **Arrow** (sprite), and **Ilkara Levari** (half-elf spy, CG) read as an independent, broadly friendly crew — plausible sources of a lead if approached rather than boarded by force. **Tazlan Rilzeer** (bandit captain, NG) and **Tharkil Morn** (priest, LG) run a second, good-aligned vessel — more of the same "bandit" label doing a lot of work, matching the completed Redreef/Kodd contrast preserved in the Chapters 1–9 archive. **Klarz** (hobgoblin captain) commands a hostile hobgoblin/bugbear crew; **Sharlasta Stormsword** (Illuskan human bandit captain, LE) and **Captain Drashk** (tiefling bandit captain, N, with an imp aboard) run openly hostile or transactional ships willing to trade information for the right price or threat.

**Divineverse note:** The search is a mortal-scale investigation problem before it is a combat problem. Doran and Wren should be doing the actual work of reading which ships are worth approaching, which are worth avoiding, and which are worth pressuring — the girls do not shortcut this by simply knowing which ship matters. Any of the friendlier crews (Zaldar's or Tazlan's) can plausibly hand over a piece of the Morkoth's patrol pattern without a fight if the trainees ask well; the hostile crews are sorted the way any armed opportunist at sea is sorted, on knowledge and willingness rather than the "bandit" label alone.

**Resolution shape:** The search converts into an actual heading toward the Morkoth's patrol waters near the Purple Rocks. Track which vessel(s) were engaged, spared, or looted, and any name recorded as a live contact, in the active story ledger only if it becomes consequential.


### Morkoth

**Arrival beat:** The Morkoth is not a mystery once reached. It is a crime scene in motion. The crew may fight, bargain, flee, or follow Tholtz's orders, but the central fact is Hekaton's captive body aboard the vessel.

**Moral sort - the crew:** Separate paid sailors, Society operators, magical enforcers, and Tholtz Daggerdark. Ember does not flatten the crew into one verdict. The question is knowledge and willing participation in sovereign imprisonment. Tholtz is fully inside the Society's occult command chain and understands enough to be culpable even where Slarkrethel's highest purpose remains opaque.

**Critical moment - Hekaton:** The chains are a category error made physical: a sovereign authority treated as removable cargo. Soliera correcting it is the point of the chapter. Hekaton does not need to be diminished for the rescue to matter. His authority has been displaced, not destroyed.

**Slarkrethel pressure:** If Slarkrethel acts through storm, psychic command, ship destruction, or direct kraken intervention, frame it as an ancient opportunist cutting losses and protecting deeper operations. Do not make the kraken the secret root cause of SKT. Iymrith engineered the court crisis; Slarkrethel exploited it.

**Resolution shape:** Hekaton freed if the rescue succeeds. His condition, the Morkoth's state, Tholtz's fate, and any Slarkrethel contact route to the active story ledger. If the table creates a failure state, Serissa remains capable of continuing the restoration path; do not collapse the arc into helplessness.


### Return to Maelstrom

**Arrival beat:** Hekaton's return changes the room before he speaks. The court's delayed deference problem ends or becomes visible as treason. Serissa does not lose dignity when her father returns; she is recognized for having endured the crisis.

**Political correction:** Hekaton must learn the true causal chain, including Neri's murder and the manipulation of his daughters. The correction is not only strategic. It is familial, sovereign, and theological at once.

**Critical moment - restoration:** The ordning restores through legitimate authority returning to the correct seat once the criminal obstruction is removed. If Hekaton lives, he resumes the anchor role. If he does not, Serissa's legitimacy can carry the restoration forward after Iymrith is removed. Either version preserves lawful structure rather than replacing it with divine fiat.

**Resolution shape:** Court stabilized. Iymrith finale path confirmed. Storm giant escort selected if needed. Artifact state checked. Any changes to Hekaton, Serissa, Mirran/Nym, Uthor, throne, or scepter route to the active story ledger.


### Iymrith's Lair

**Endgame routing:** The Court is the exposure and first-confrontation theater;
the lair is the Chapter 12 destination for the last major Iymrith fights. The
Champions, Kraken Society, Thayan remnant, and Drow operation remain active
concurrent pressures around this sequence, so the return to the lair is expected
to be a combat-heavy endgame rather than a solitary dragon scene. Iymrith's
death is a likely resolution, not a written result before play.

**Arrival beat:** The desert should feel like Iymrith's preferred answer to consequence: distance, dryness, old stone, and buried evidence. She chose a place where structures vanish under sand unless someone remembers where to dig.

**Approach choice:** Source combat offers direct assault, stealth, giant-size potions, storm giant escorts, and lair hazards. Divineverse does not need these as limitations, but they remain useful as scene theater. Use them to show scale, not to manufacture anti-feats.

**Critical moment - Soliera's consideration:** Soliera studies Iymrith's manipulation because it is clean, patient, and structurally elegant. This is not mercy. It is recognition of craft before disposal. Iymrith receives the dignity of being understood and none of the safety she expected from being clever.

**Iymrith's final read:** She should understand too late that the thing in front of her does not share a dragon's clock. Her patience, contingencies, buried treasure, gargoyle surveillance, and old lair logic all assume time as protection. Soliera removes that assumption.

**Resolution shape:** The Chapter 12 lair sequence is the intended final major Iymrith battlefield. If the party wins there, Iymrith ends and Neri is answered; if play produces a different result, route the actual outcome to the active ledger rather than treating this reference as a prewritten death. The Hekaton/Serissa alliance with small-folk becomes structurally credible rather than sentimental. Remaining giant lord cleanup, dragon legacy, or Slarkrethel long-game hooks remain available but no longer define the SKT root crisis.
