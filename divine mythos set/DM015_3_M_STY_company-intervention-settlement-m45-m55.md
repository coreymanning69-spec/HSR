M45 — Veyren Meets Captain Bucky

The convoy square was a riot of movement — not panicked, but taut and controlled, like a machine revving high. Soldiers barked orders into comms, drones hummed overhead, screens in mobile trailers flashed with feeds from UAVs and body cams. Trucks and response cars pulled into formation, men and women unloading crates, field kits, antenna gear. For a town of two thousand, it looked like an invasion force had landed overnight.

At the center stood Captain Bucky.

Salt-and-pepper hair cropped short, fatigue uniform crisp despite the chaos, his posture ramrod straight. He wasn't young anymore — pushing sixty, lines carved deep at his eyes and mouth — but his chest was broad, his voice carried, and when he barked an order, half a dozen teams snapped to it. Piercing blue eyes flicked across feeds, maps, and troop positions with clinical efficiency. This wasn't his first crisis, and it showed.

Veyren thundered up, boots heavy from his sprint, sweat streaking his chest where plates had rubbed him raw. Support staff spotted him before he reached the command knot. A shout went up:

"Tier Four incoming! Veyren, clear a line!"

They had a kit open before he even slowed — modular armor plates, fresh gauntlet shells, a ration stack, water, regen tonics. Logistics hands called out numbers as though running a drill.

Veyren didn't wait for permission. He slid the battered shell of his gauntlets loose, tossing broken bits to the side, and began peeling away what was left of his torso plating. What remained was the body beneath — thick mesh wrapping his torso and gut, a one-piece compression weave that hugged tight. Not for comfort. Not for modesty. For function: to hold his organs where they belonged when blades or claws opened him up. It was a brutal uniform, more butcher's apron than soldier's silk, and every soldier present knew exactly why he wore it.

He rolled his shoulders, cracked his neck, and looked straight at Bucky.

"Captain Commander," Veyren said, voice deep, teeth flashing in a grin that never quite reached his eyes.

Bucky's gaze snapped up from the maps. For half a second the meticulous officer measured the man — the sweat, the scars, the mesh holding him together — and then gave a single, sharp nod.

"Veyren. Good. You're here."

Behind them, the square thrummed with rising energy — soldiers forming ranks, cars lining up, UAVs sweeping tight arcs over the Titan's bowed frame. The entire world outside the tavern was a chessboard wound tight.

And only a block away, three divine girls ate lunch in perfect calm.

M46 — The Knock

Veyren approached the wreck of the tavern doorway. His hand came up, and he rapped "lightly" against the frame — but seven hundred pounds of armored gauntlet made a light knock into a quake. What was left of the door collapsed, splintering across the floor.

He frowned, then stepped inside, ducking his broad shoulders. To him, that hadn't counted. So he reached out again and rapped on the wall.

The sound echoed.

Sera's head snapped up. Her voice was sharp, unimpressed.
"Yes. Why are you making noise?"

Veyren straightened instinctively, like a boy caught. His voice was steady, but heavy with discipline. "It's polite to knock, ma'am."

He didn't even see her move. One blink and she was at his side — not across the room anymore, but right beside him at the broken threshold, sapphire-blue eyes tilted up toward his chest.

"There's that word again," she said, voice flat. "What does ma'am mean?"

Veyren's heart jolted in his ribcage. He'd been briefed on her speed, but knowing and feeling it were different things. His pulse kicked faster, adrenaline punching through his veins.

He realized everyone on comms had gone silent. No chatter. No backup. They knew she could hear every word. He was truly alone.

He dipped his head slightly, choosing each word.
"Ma'am is a word you use respectfully. For someone above you. It's how I address a superior."

Sera blinked at him, then her lips curved. "Well, okay. I think that works out. Because she's pretty cool." Her hand tipped lazily toward the table. "That's Soliera. And I'm Sera."

The name landed like a thunderclap.

Veyren's eyes widened, breath hitching. His gaze flicked past Sera to the bread-breaking figure at the table. "You mean… that's Soli? I didn't kno-"

The sentence never finished.

Sera's body flicked, almost playfully. In the same instant, half of Veyren's upper body was gone. His reaching arm, shoulder, and chest vaporized into gore, splattered hot against the tavern wall.

His legs still stood for a breath, quivering, before the regenerative mesh seized and fought to hold his body together.

Because you don't nickname God.

M47 — The Nickname

The tavern detonated in motion when Sera's flick landed. A wet thunderclap against the wall, meat and blood spattered in grotesque shapes. Amara cried out, staggering back from the table, hands clapped to her mouth. Steven froze stiff, dish rag still in his hands, knuckles white. His eyes locked on the streaks up the plaster, and his brain screamed everyone's dead, everyone's dead—but Veyren's legs were still standing.

Then they buckled.

He collapsed to his knees, mesh-weave groaning, torso re-knitting in spasms of wet flesh and sinew. The sight was obscene, miraculous, unstoppable. His hands trembled, pressing together, bloody and incomplete, lifted like a plea.

"I'm sorry," he choked out, voice breaking. "I didn't mean it. She just looks like someone I know."

Sera crouched, her sapphire-blue gaze angled sharp, the skirt of her silk fanning around her knees. "Now how does that work out? And why," her voice dropped to a hiss, "would you call her the nickname I call her?"

His breathing was ragged, his pulse still pounding. The regeneration stitched itself outward from his shoulder, sculpting muscle, tendon, bone as he spoke.

"I—I know her. But she's only a young woman. A young girl. And she's far away, at an outpost she's built. She looks just like your Lady. Just the same."

"Lady," Sera echoed, straightening. A smile ghosted her lips, bright and dangerous. "Capitalized. Good." She tilted her head. "So. You're her friends?"

"Yes, ma'am—" His heart lurched; his mouth corrected before she could move again. "Yes, Lady Sera. We are friends. Very good friends. I've known her for years."

The tavern's silence throbbed. Ember's eyes were wide, sticky fingers halfway raised, watching the scene as though it were a play. Soliera kept eating her bread.

Outside, comms lit up in a crackle of overlapping voices.

"Did he just say—"
"Wait, he knows her?"
"Who the hell does he mean—"
"Cross-confirm: subject resembles Soliera? Are we hearing that right?"

The Company staffers in Base Two exchanged looks that carried dread. None of them said the word Prime. Not yet. But the seed was in the air.

Two Solieras.

And one small woman with a stillness that did not tolerate confusion.

M48 — Two Solieras

Sera dropped into a squat beside Veyren. Her silk spilled outward, the hem drawing a soft circle around his ruined, reknitting frame. She was close enough for him to smell the faint heat rolling off her skin — velvet and ozone, sweet and dangerous.

The Velvet Spiral bled into him like smoke in water. His regen fought to pull him upright, but his will faltered under the pressure, corrosion in his resolve eating from the edges. Instinct howled at him: stay low, don't bare your teeth.

Her smile curved — not kind, not cruel, but something older. "So," she purred, sapphire-blue gaze fixed. "There's another Soliera? Maybe that's why we've enjoyed this place so much."

At the table, Ember tilted her head. Her orange-bright eyes flicked between Soliera Divine's calm face and the bruiser kneeling half-healed on the floor. "Hmm…" She hummed it like a tune. "So maybe we have to go meet her? Could she be why we came here from Undermountain?"

The words hovered in the thick air.

Soliera looked at Ember, not Sera, and gave the faintest nod. Bread still in her hand, her presence sealed the thought as truth: of course it was tied to the other Soliera. Of course this is why the verses touched.

Veyren stayed bowed, nose near the boards, blood drying in streaks. Every nerve in his body screamed the same thing his instincts did: don't move, don't rise. He knelt under the weight of three goddesses and the crushing certainty that one wrong word could end him again.

M49 — Apex Instinct

Veyren's breath came rough, chest heaving as his body knitted, but it wasn't just the pain. It was her. Sera's Spiral coiled around him, a pheromone storm — half weapon, half invitation. Every animal instinct he owned was burning: fight, submit, rut, obey. He wanted to see her, to look at the doll-bodied apex who had erased half his frame like a casual shrug.

He forced his head up.

Her smile caught him in the act, bright and razor-edged. "Oh! Do you like what you see?"

His grin was raw, almost feral, stripped of protocol. "Yeah. I do. I like a woman that can kick my ass."

The response cost him, but it was honest.

Sera tilted her head, then raised her foot — seven inches of porcelain-perfect flesh, toes painted dark — and pressed it onto the broad shoulder that remained. Her body weighed ninety pounds. It didn't matter. Veyren's frame collapsed back to the floor like a felled beast.

She purred down at him, voice velvet and final. "Hmm. I think we're gonna get along fine, friend."

Her foot lifted, the pressure gone. But the sensation lingered in Veyren's marrow: small, delicate, and utterly undeniable. His panther-wolf-lion blood howled the truth — he had found a higher apex. A new pack leader. Instinct begged him to roll, to bare his throat, to submit.

At the table, Soliera dabbed her fingers on a napkin, finishing her bread with the same serenity as if none of this mattered.

Ember, jacket stained and bright-eyed, tugged at her hem. "So… does that mean we're going somewhere?"

Sera glanced at her, still crouched, one hand on her knee. "Mm. I don't know. I think we have to take care of the people outside first."

The words settled, the promise of violence hovering like storm air.

M50 — The Pull of Power

Veyren knelt low, head bowed, body half-rebuilt. Every muscle in him screamed to rise — to look, to drink in the doll-sized predator crouched beside him. His instincts were on fire, wolf-panther-lion urging him to follow the line of her legs, silk pooling over porcelain thighs, up and up until he found the secret of her smile.

But the memory of her flick lingered like a phantom ache. She could cut him in half again, easier than breathing. That danger thrilled him. He was a bruiser, a tank who read the world through force. And here was infinite force in silk, small enough to crush him without effort. His animal mind called it death, his human mind called it doom, but something deeper whispered: apex.

So he stayed low. Smitten. Terrified. Wanting.

The Company outside saw none of the nuance, only the terror. Their most durable operative knelt in submission, drenched in blood, held down by a girl who couldn't weigh ninety pounds. Drones hovered in nervous arcs, lights silent, recording every twitch, but no one dared push a button.

And then came Captain Commander Bucky.

Salt-and-pepper hair, fatigues sharp, chest squared, eyes cold and piercing. He didn't bark an order this time. He walked. Crisp steps across the square, weaving through soldiers who parted for him without a word. His presence was its own kind of gravity.

He approached the tavern's broken frame, the door long since reduced to splinters. The weight of his command aura pressed in — the kind only a lifetime of authority builds. He didn't yet cross the threshold, but he was close enough that the gods inside would feel the change in the air.

Another apex had stepped into the scene.

And the town held its breath.

M51 — Names and Standing

Sera tilted her head toward the kneeling bruiser, sapphire-blue eyes softer than before. Her voice came with a lilt, playful but edged.
"Well, I've told you my name. I'm Sera. So who are you, Mister Doesn't Die?"

Her gaze lingered on him — not indulgent, not forgiving, but no longer quite so lethal.
