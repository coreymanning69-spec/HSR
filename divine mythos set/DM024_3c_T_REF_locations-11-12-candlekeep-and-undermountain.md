### LOCATION 11: CANDLEKEEP (The Seeker’s Fortress)
**[MAP POSITION]** Southwestern Sword Coast, on a peninsula southwest of Baldur’s Gate. Accessible only via the Way of the Lion road.
**[STATUS]** PRIOR VISIT AT T433 — Avernus consultation with Sylvira, Traxigor, and Lulu. The itinerary presents a future return, not Ember's first arrival.
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** The Ark can anchor offshore; skiff to the peninsula. Road from Baldur’s Gate (the Way of the Lion, one day’s travel).
**[WORD OF MOUTH]** Specialized/academic. Candlekeep’s Avowed have research and direct Avernus-era witness; they hold Faerûn's most accurate academic file on the trio.

**Setting Changes (20+)**
1. Candlekeep requires a gift of knowledge to enter: each visitor must donate a book or scroll of knowledge not already in the collection. The girls bringing Ember means whoever is managing the gift needs to bring something genuinely new to a library that has been collecting for centuries.
2. The Avowed have a formal academic category they created specifically for the divine Mythos events: “Cosmological Intervention, Active.” It is the first category ever added to their classification system in 200 years.
3. There is a specific reading room in Candlekeep that contains the collected works of Alaundo the Seer — the prophet whose predictions form the foundation of Candlekeep’s entire existence. Some of his prophecies remain uninterpreted. A small number are currently being reevaluated in light of recent events. The Avowed have not publicly stated what they think the girls represent in prophetic terms. They are very carefully not doing this.
4. The library has a restricted section. This is not unusual — most libraries have restricted sections. What is unusual is that Candlekeep’s restricted section has a subsection that even the senior Avowed don’t have access to. The key to this subsection has been held by the First Reader for five hundred years and has been transferred to each successive First Reader without the holder ever reading the contents. The current First Reader knows this is relevant to the girls’ visit and has not decided what to do about it.
5. Candlekeep’s defensive capability is significantly underestimated by everyone who hasn’t tried to breach it. The fortress has held against armies. The practical ceiling of its defensive capacity is unknown because nothing has ever gotten past the outer wards.
6. The Avowed who has studied the girls most extensively is named Rial. She is a halfling, approximately 60 years old, and has been working on her thesis about the theological implications of the Well of Dragons event for three years. She is going to be very difficult to stop talking once she starts.
7. The library’s collection includes a copy of every significant document the Sanctum has issued — treaties, civic decrees, trade agreements. These were obtained through commercial channels (Candlekeep pays premium prices for primary source documents) and are displayed alongside commentary. The commentary from ten years ago reads very differently from the commentary Rial wrote last year.
8. Ember’s specific request upon arriving: “I want to find a book that doesn’t know what it’s about yet.” This is either a completely meaningful request or a completely Ember request. Rial will immediately understand what she means and will take her somewhere specific.
9. The Avowed practice a daily ritual: at dawn, each scholar-monk reads something they have never read before. The goal is perpetual intellectual exposure to novelty. This practice has produced, over centuries, a community of people who are genuinely comfortable with the unknown in a way most humans aren’t. When Sera walks in, the Avowed response will be curiosity rather than fear.
10. There is a specific prophecy of Alaundo’s that has been internally flagged by the senior Avowed as “potentially active.” It concerns a child and a question. The Avowed know about Ember. They are keeping the prophecy quiet because they don’t know if knowing changes anything and because getting the prophecy wrong would be worse than not having acted on it.
11. Candlekeep has one thing the girls cannot ignore: the largest collection of theological lore in the Realms, including every documented account of divine intervention, divine creation, divine death, and divine succession across every pantheon in history. If Soliera wants to understand the framework in which she exists, this is the place.
12. The library’s inner gardens — accessible to residents and long-term guests — contain plants from every climate zone in Faerûn, maintained by druidic and botanical magic. They are peaceful in a specific way that is different from the Sanctum’s peace. This peace is built on the accumulation of centuries of quiet work rather than on divine authority. Soliera will likely notice this difference.
13. The Avowed maintain a system of communication tubes that connect every major reading room in the fortress. They were installed two centuries ago and no one knows who invented them. They work. The tubes carry small scrolled notes. The scholars use them constantly. The system produces a faint whistling sound throughout the building at all hours.
14. A visitor arrived at Candlekeep six weeks ago with a gift of an original manuscript that the Avowed were initially very excited about. Upon closer examination, the manuscript is forged — expertly, but forged. The visitor has not been found. The manuscript, despite being fake, contains one genuine piece of information in it: a map coordinate that doesn’t correspond to any known location.
15. The restricted sub-section’s contents (the one the First Reader can’t read): it is a collection of pre-Faerûn-recorded-history documents about the previous iteration of the world — before the current divine pantheon established itself. The documents are written in a script that predates every known language. The First Reader’s decision about whether to share this with Soliera is the most significant decision any holder of the key has ever faced.
16. Candlekeep’s food is institutional, consistent, and slightly better than expected: an old tradition of the Avowed is that a cook as skilled as any scholar is worth as much to the community. The kitchen has been operating at a high standard for three hundred years. The Mirabar engineer, if she were here, would approve.
17. Three of the senior Avowed have independently written letters to the Sanctum’s Waterdeep embassy in the past year asking for an academic exchange program: Sanctum personnel visiting Candlekeep to assist with documentation of their own activities, in exchange for Avowed scholars being granted research access to the Sanctum’s Hall of Records. The letters are in Velin’s queue. She hasn’t decided yet because she’s not sure what Soliera would think about scholars documenting her.
18. The Avowed maintain a specific Silence Day: one day per month, no speaking in the library. Communication via written notes only. The Avowed consider this a way of practicing precision — if you have to write it, you think more carefully about whether you mean it. The girls will arrive on a Silence Day or they won’t, depending on what creates the better session beat.
19. There is a room in Candlekeep that contains exactly one book. The room is not restricted. The book is available to any visitor. It is small, hand-written, and its cover is blank. The book contains the author’s account of a single day in their life — an ordinary day, by all descriptions. The author is unknown. The day described is hundreds of years ago. Scholars have studied the book for decades trying to identify who wrote it and why it was donated to the most significant library in the Realms. They have not identified a significance. The mystery endures.
20. Ember’s interaction with Candlekeep’s collection will produce, within approximately two hours of arrival, a question that no Avowed has an answer to. This is not a certainty. It is a reasonable prediction given her operating principle. The question will either be theological, philosophical, or about that specific book in the single-book room.
21. At T433 Soliera gave Candlekeep *Candlelight; For Ember*. It contains complete knowledge across multiverses and planes, but only as information about candles; it is not a general oracle, weapon, command channel, or substitute for judgment.
22. Sylvira witnessed the gift and Traxigor chose restoration beyond his otter form during the consultation. Their standing is expertise and lived witness, never authority over the Girls.
23. The Emberians form later as a formal religious order around the book. Their name, origin, and doctrinal object are fixed; founders, rites, hierarchy, membership, property, and founding date are not. Wren is a Sanctum Archpriestess, not an Emberian officeholder or the order's namesake.

**Embassy Status**
NO EMBASSY. Candlekeep is neutral institutional territory. No political entity embeds here. The Sanctum does not break this precedent.

**Hooks & Quests (TR-33 through TR-36)**
* **TR-33:** The First Reader’s decision. Do they share the pre-history documents with Soliera?This is less a quest and more a session beat of consequence. What does Soliera do with knowledge of the world before this one?
* **TR-34:** The forged manuscript’s genuine map coordinate. Where does it point?Who put the coordinate in a forged document?Why go to the trouble of an elaborate forgery to deliver one real piece of information?This hook trails into whatever arc comes after the tour.
* **TR-35:** Rial’s thesis on the Well of Dragons wants primary source confirmation of three specific details that no external record has correctly captured. She needs to ask the girls directly. She is simultaneously certain this is the most important thing she has ever done professionally and uncertain she can actually form the words.
* **TR-36:** Ember in the single-book room. She reads the ordinary day account. She asks who wrote it. No one knows. She thinks about this for a long time. Then she asks: “Was their day good?” No one can answer that either. This is a small beat but it’s an Ember beat.


### LOCATION 12: UNDERMOUNTAIN (Below Waterdeep)
**[MAP POSITION]** Directly below Waterdeep, accessible via the Yawning Portal pit. The established cross-verse portal is on Level 20. The connected levels and Underdark veins have expanded into a near-city with Sanctum infrastructure.
**[STATUS]** Active, heavily populated Sanctum-linked city. The Level-20 portal and Halaster contact are established; trade and conversation with Oak City are developing slowly.
**[TRAVEL ACCESS]** The Yawning Portal. Durnan’s rope. No teleport directly — Undermountain suppresses most external teleportation.

**Key Data Points**
1. Halaster Blackcloak (the Mad Mage) remains a local, depth-variable presence in the expanded city. The established T429 contact occurred at Level 20; no separate Level-23 portal or required Halaster floor is canon.
2. Halaster’s fundamental interest in the girls is not threat-based. He collects interesting things. He has been collecting interesting things in his dungeon for centuries. The girls are the most interesting things to cross into his awareness in a very long time. His approach to them is therefore closer to a collector’s excitement than a villain’s calculation.
3. The single Level-20 Mergeverse portal remains stable and institutionally important. Its continued use is shaped by the slowly developing Undermountain–Oak City relationship.
4. Skullport (accessible via Undermountain upper levels, Level 3 area) is its own arc: the underground port that handles Waterdeep’s unofficial commerce. Bolts operates here. The Skullport session can be brief (resupply, information gathering) or extended (the plot hook TR-25 resolves here if followed).
5. Halaster has seeded the dungeon with his own logic — rooms that correspond to his obsessions, his experiments, his games. He is aware that the girls can walk through his dungeon without engaging any of it. He is curious whether they will or won’t. He has placed specific objects at specific locations as tests.
6. The Halaster encounter at Level 20 feels unlike any other NPC encounter in the tour: he is harmless to the Girls in any direct sense yet genuinely unpredictable in motivation. He remains a local figure in the expanded Undermountain city rather than the keeper of a second portal.
