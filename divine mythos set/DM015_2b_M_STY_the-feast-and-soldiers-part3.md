M33 — Arrival Signals

The barkeep's voice cracked as he dipped into a bow, rag clutched like a lifeline. "Sorry, I, uh…" The words fell apart in his throat. He didn't know what to say.

Sera grinned wide, waving him off with the lamb bone still in hand. "Don't worry! We like you. You make good food. By the way, I'm Sera."

The tension in his chest loosened only slightly. He shuffled back toward the bar, bow still half-held, face pale with relief and disbelief.

Outside, the street was alive. Flashing lights spun against the pale asphalt, red and blue molten in the glass. No sound carried; the sirens had melted silent at Soliera's glance, but they pulsed anyway, a reminder that the town was still convulsing under the presence of gods.

Then came the sound that mattered: a low rumble, deep and smooth, an engine idling just beyond the broken tavern doors. Not a farm truck, not a town car — this was wealth and authority distilled into steel. Someone important had arrived.

At the table, Ember tilted her cup, mesmerized by the slosh of dark liquid inside. She tipped it too far, missed the return flow, and a spill of sticky sweetness cascaded over her small hands and down her yellow silk.

She looked down, giggled at the bright contrast of dark stain on pale gold, then wrinkled her nose at the sticky wetness. With a little squeal, she hiked her skirt too high, shaking her hands and trying to free herself of the mess.

Amara and the barkeep both whipped their heads away at once, cheeks burning, catching the flash of white beneath before turning to stare hard at the floorboards.

Behind them, Sera laughed aloud. Soliera tore another piece of bread in silence, the faintest smile tugging at the corners of her lips.

M34 — The Suit

Outside the broken frame, the sound of heels clicked crisply against the pale asphalt. Calm, deliberate steps, too measured to be the panicked shuffle of townsfolk or the heavy stomp of armored officers.

Inside, the Trio barely noticed. Ember was still shaking her sticky fingers, wrinkling her nose at the candy stain on her silk. Soliera chewed her bread with oceanic stillness. Sera leaned back on the loveseat, toes flicking lazily in the air, humming under her breath.

Then came three light knocks against the ruined door, polite as if it hadn't been shattered.

A man stepped in — nondescript, pressed gray suit, clipboard in one hand, a spotless white hat angled just so. He carried himself like a safety officer or a negotiator, one of those Company-trained mediators sent in when things had already gone too far.

His eyes swept the room.

First to Ember, giggling as she tried to pat her skirt dry. Then to Soliera, unmoved, her lavender hem brushing the leather seat. Then to Sera, sprawled like a cat, toes still flexing idly above the armrest.

And then he saw the humans — the barkeep trembling at the bar, Amara standing stiff with her staff, both wide-eyed and pale. His brows lifted, silent question hanging in the air: Are you alright? Are you hostages?

Sera answered before either of them could speak.

"Hello!" she chirped brightly, lamb bone still in her hand. Her grin stretched ear to ear. "You must be the king!"

The man froze mid-step, clipboard sagging slightly at his side.

M36 — Stress Test

The man's lungs dragged in a shaky breath. He hadn't been trained for this. Negotiation drills, yes. Riot dispersal, yes. But not porcelain dolls that moved faster than thought, not goddesses who silenced alarms with a glance.

He pressed a hand against the floorboards and tried to rise.

Slow, careful. Not looking toward the lavender figure at the table — he'd already learned that mistake. His gaze stayed low, trained on the broken wood beneath his palms.

Sera's steps circled tighter, silk whispering as she closed the loop. Her sapphire-blue eyes followed his every inch upward, flat and unreadable.

The barkeep swallowed audibly in the corner. Amara held her staff like it could anchor her against the dread knotting her gut.

The man got one knee under him, then the other, boots creaking. His clipboard lay discarded by the door. He reached for it, trying to move like he still had some authority.

Sera's grin twitched. "Careful," she said softly.

It wasn't warning. It wasn't mercy. It was the reminder that falling had been his only reprieve, and standing might well be his last mistake.

M37 — Floor and Flesh

Sera crouched low, skirts pooling around her like violet water, unashamed and unbothered. The man kept his gaze pinned to the ruined floorboards, but the pressure of temptation clawed at him. Pale porcelain skin gleamed in the corner of his vision — flawless, impossible. His throat locked.

He tried to speak, but what left him was only a wet dribble of noise.

Sera scoffed, a sharp puff through her nose. "Never seen a girl like me, huh?"

She dropped further, laying her small body down on the boards beside him, cheek against the splinters. With a little shimmy, she slid her head until it was directly under his gaze, sapphire-blue eyes staring straight up into his visor.

"So," she murmured, voice playful but not kind, "are you the king?"

The room held its breath.

On the loveseat, Ember had set the yellow silk aside — discarded, not removed in modesty; she registered the material and decided she did not need it for now. She had folded her legs under her, the same compact posture Sera used when she lounged, kintsugi lines a steady warm gold along her thighs. She pressed her shoulders back into Soliera's calm side and idly kicked one heel against the leather, watching the room. Her stillness was not absence. It was attention without a question yet attached.

At the bar, Amara rummaged through the lost-and-found chest, cheeks pink. Beside her, the barkeep finally found his voice again. "Amara," he said quietly, "my name is Steven. I've seen you here before and wanted to get a bit more acquainted but…" He shrugged, a nervous smile tugging at his lips. "I guess these aren't such bad circumstances."

Amara paused, holding up a folded shirt. She glanced at him, smile softening despite the chaos. "Nice to get the name of the man who's been the only one running this whole place."

And then — all focus shifted back to the floor.

Sera, sprawled like a cat, demanding answers with her head tilted under the negotiator's gaze.

The clipboard man swallowed, words jammed in his throat. He had seconds to decide what he would say to a god who looked like a doll.

M38 — Alone

The man's throat bobbed as Sera's sapphire-blue eyes burned beneath his visor. His tongue stuck. He stuttered, the words fumbling out in broken pieces.

"I— I'm… I'm not— no, I'm not the k-king. We don't— we don't have one, I…" His breath rasped, each syllable caught between confession and fear. "I'm… just here to… to talk, that's all—"

His earpiece crackled. A voice cut through, clipped and professional.

"Base Two to recon. We've got your SOS. No authorization to engage. You are alone. Repeat: you are alone."

The words chilled him more than Sera's stare. No backup. No cavalry. Just him on the floor, his clipboard somewhere out of reach, a child-shaped god circling him with flat disinterest, asking questions he couldn't answer.

Sera's lips curved slightly, catching the static in his ear. "Alone, huh?" she murmured, her tone not cruel — but the weight of inevitability hung in every syllable.

Amara glanced up from the bar, the shirt still in her hands. Steven exhaled sharply beside her. Ember hummed happily from the loveseat, swinging her bare legs as she tucked herself against Soliera.

The clipboard man's fingers trembled against the boards. His training had never prepared him for this.

M39 — The Threat

"Okay," Sera said at last, voice flat. She rocked herself upright, skirts falling back into place as she sat.

Her head tilted slightly, sapphire-blue eyes narrowing. "So the people in your ear… they're the commanders?"

The clipboard man swallowed hard, helmet dipping in the smallest nod.

Sera's expression soured, lips pressing thin. "This is too much work," she said, almost pouting. Her gaze sharpened like a blade. "I'm about to just kill you all, and we can try again tomorrow…"

The words dropped like a stone into the tavern.

Amara gasped aloud, her hand flying to her mouth. Her staff clattered faintly against the floor as she turned toward the loveseat. Ember looked up at the sound, brow furrowed.

"Why'd you gasp?" she asked, tilting her head, voice small and curious. Her bare legs swung idly against the leather as if the threat hadn't been spoken.

Amara forced her feet forward, clutching the oversized white-and-blue jacket she'd pulled from the lost-and-found. She wanted to wrap it over Ember's shoulders, to shield her from cold and eyes alike. But each step closer brought her nearer to Soliera's lavender hem, to the silent god who watched without blinking.

Her knees wavered. Even in innocence, even with good intent, approaching Soliera without invitation felt like trespass. She hesitated mid-step, breath catching.

The clipboard man lay frozen, every muscle screaming not to move, not to breathe. The barkeep muttered a prayer under his breath, voice trembling.

And Sera watched them all, boredom and threat balanced in her small frame, lamb bone still in her fingers like a scepter.

M40 — Threefold Tension

The clipboard man's voice cracked as he blurted out, desperation making him loud:
"Wait! Wait. I've got a direct commander. He's about a mile away in a mobile command center. He's the one who denied my SOS request!"

Sera's head cocked, her hair falling to one side as she looked at him flatly.
"Okay? So. What's a mile? And what's an SOS?"

The man faltered, lips working, his training useless in the face of questions that bent language itself.

Meanwhile, Ember slid down from the leather loveseat, bare feet padding softly on the wood. Her chest was uncovered, only the pure white silk of her small underclothes clinging to her hips. The aerials of her chest weren't human pink but a brilliant orange, glowing faintly against her smooth black skin. The contrast was impossible, stunning in its strangeness.

The barkeep had already turned away, eyes locked on the bar top, face red, breath shallow.

Amara stood frozen, jacket held forward like one might offer a blanket to a nervous dog. Her hands trembled as Ember walked up to her, eyes wide and trusting.

Ember raised both arms, wordless invitation to be dressed.

Amara's breath caught. She swallowed hard, gaze dragging over the otherworldly colors, the silk, the innocence in Ember's expectant eyes. Her body screamed at her to move, to clothe the girl, to care for her.

Her mind whispered: why am I gawking? why am I swayed so easily?

She hesitated in the space between action and collapse.

And in the center of the room, Sera's sapphire-blue gaze pinned the clipboard man, waiting for his answer.

M41 — Commands and Contrasts

Sera's voice was sharp but casual, like she was reminding a child about manners.
"Tell your commander to walk through those doors, or I'm losing my patience."

The man fumbled at his mic, stammering into the air. Before he could form words, the clipped voice in his ear answered:
"Acknowledge. Commander's en route."

Sera's eyes narrowed, but her smile flashed, sudden and pleased. "Cool. Now you can go. And don't make any more noise, or I'm gonna have to kill you."

He didn't argue. He didn't even breathe loudly. He left the clipboard where it had fallen, left the spotless white hat where it rolled near the door. Head down, shoulders stiff, he backed out through the shattered frame as though one wrong exhale would end him.

Inside, another drama unfolded.

Amara reached forward at last, draping the oversized jacket down over Ember's small shoulders. The blue and white fabric swallowed her, falling almost to her knees — too big, too soft, but covering her like a makeshift dress.

As she adjusted it, Amara noticed her palms tacky with the girl's spilled drink. Ember's dark skin gleamed faintly, sticky sheen catching the light. Before Amara could flinch, Steven appeared at her side, wordless, holding out a rag still damp from the well. His hand shook slightly, but his eyes were steady: a small act of service in the presence of gods.

Ember giggled, tugging the jacket collar close, pleased with her new outfit.

And Amara froze.

Her fingertips brushed Ember's bare arm through the sleeve as she straightened the fabric. Warm. Shockingly warm, softer than silk, though her skin looked like polished obsidian. The contrast rattled her — stone should be cold, unyielding. Ember was not.

Things don't have to make sense, Amara reminded herself, breath shaky. Not with these three. Not with what they are.

At the loveseat, Soliera sat back with quiet serenity, chewing another piece of bread, as if the entire exchange — the threat, the plea, the fear, the laughter — were simply part of the same calm meal.

And in a way, for the divine trio, it was.

M42 — Honorifics

"Alright, Sera, come back and eat," Ember called, tugging her oversized jacket closed with sticky fingers. "You've been doing too much."

Sera's eyes flicked over, brows raised. "Well, you've been busy getting dressed. You haven't been eating either."

Ember giggled, shoulders shaking under the borrowed coat. "Yeah, that's true. But I still want to try some of your juice."

Sera snorted, then began to pad back across the boards, silk trailing. As she passed, her sapphire-blue gaze lingered on Amara. "Thank you. I appreciate you taking care of Ember."

Amara swallowed, cheeks hot, nodding once.

Sera turned to Steven, still holding the damp rag like an offering. "Thank you, too. You're one of the first men I've met that's not a complete bad person."

Steven blinked, taken aback. His voice cracked as he stammered, "Well, thank you, Miss— Ma'am— Lady Sera."

Her grin blossomed, teeth flashing. "Oh, there you go. Lady Sera. That's what they normally call me. Queen Sera, Goddess Sera, Lady Sera… whatever. I think they're called honorifics, and I like them."

She ended with a happy little smile, flopping back into her seat, lamb bone in hand once more.

Ember crawled up beside her, giggling as she reached for Sera's cup. Soliera broke another piece of bread, still silent, still unshaken, her presence anchoring the room like a quiet ocean tide.

M43 — Lunch

Amara had finally sat, sliding onto the seat beside Ember instead of across by Sera. The oversized jacket she'd draped over the girl was already stained — sticky smears, crumbs ground into the white and blue fabric, a dark splash across the sleeve where Ember had missed her cup again. Ember didn't seem to mind. She tugged the collar close like it was treasure, bright-eyed and messy.

Sera had worked her way through a shocking amount of food, lamb bone picked clean, half the bar's trays scattered in disarray. She licked her fingers, then turned her sapphire-blue gaze toward the barkeep.

"So, misteRRR," she purred, drawing out the R with feline ease, "since you've done such a good job… is there anything you want?"

Ember perked up at once, her little head tilting, smile bright. "Well, how about Amara? Amara, do you want anything?"

The two humans stiffened, glancing at each other. Amara's lips twitched nervously before she let out a shaky laugh. "I mean… I want to live through this."

Steven, rag still in hand, chuckled low. "That's a good thing. I want to live through this, too."

Sera blinked, her head cocking like a curious bird. Then her brows drew together.

"Live through what?" she asked, genuine confusion tinting her voice. "We're only eating lunch."

The words fell into the silence, absurd and terrifying in equal measure — because to Sera, it was only lunch. To the mortals, it was life and death hanging by a thread.

M44 — The Question of Kings

Amara leaned forward, fingers laced in her lap, heart still thudding in her chest. But she found her nerve at last. They'd been talking twenty minutes now, and if these girls meant to kill them, surely they would have already.

"Sera," she asked softly, "so… what's your plan with the people that are coming?"

Sera blinked, then snapped her fingers like she'd been reminded of something small. "Oh, that's right! Well, hopefully the king's coming, because I need to tell him how rude it was to blow down this door. This isn't even his building." She paused, tilting her head. "But if he's the king, I guess it is his building. Either way, we were in it, and these people have just not done a very good job."

Steven coughed into his fist, nervous. "Well, Lady Sera… there's actually no king here. We're not a monarchy, which means we don't have a king."

Sera's sapphire-blue eyes flicked to him, curious but not angry. "Okay, fine. Then how about your queen?"

Amara chuckled despite herself, shaking her head. "We don't have a queen either."

Sera sat back, unimpressed. "Well… I guess we'll see who they bring, because from what I'm understanding, they should have somebody on the way." Her gaze drifted back to the table, scanning the trays. "Although, actually, it's been a little bit, huh?" She leaned toward Ember. "Do you think we need to go get them?"

Ember looked down at her sticky hands and the mess smeared across her jacket. She giggled, shoulders bouncing. "No. I'm having so much fun here. Besides, Amara is right here with us."

Sera's pout melted into a smile. "Yeah, that's right. That's a good point."
