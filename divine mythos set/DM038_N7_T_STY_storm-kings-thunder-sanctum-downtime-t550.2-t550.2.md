# DM038_N7 — Storm King's Thunder — Sanctum Downtime (T550.2)

Continuous from DM038_N6's T550.1 departure boundary. This is the arrival
interlude. No combat, no rolls, no state change to either sheet.

## T550.2a — Out of Kit

A room that had not existed that morning: heated floor, glass wall over the town,
unlit hearth. Both Stewards out of signature gear entirely — Doran's plate on a
rack across the room with the knee-plate stick figure facing in, Wren's coat
folded over a chair with every pocket empty, no staff, no crown, no glasses.
Sanctum-issue robes in their sizes, provided without anyone asking.

Wren reading a Sepulchral-era threshold-geometry treatise, pipe going, buzz mild.
The author had understood that a door is not a hole and that the far side must be
found rather than made, then turned wrong in chapter six into a mathematics of
distance-as-resistance. A margin note in an unrecognized hand: *but what pushes?*

Doran finishing a stretching routine — a slow controlled handstand into an aerial
split, lowered a degree at a time. No injury in it; habit. He sat where he could
see both doors and Wren named it. Wren observed that in a house that decides how
many floors it has, the glass wall may not be a wall; Doran ruled that worse.

**Korin's method, as taught to Doran.** Resistance over load — bodyweight, another
body, a rope, a wall, slow and under tension. *You can't feel your body under a
bar; you feel the bar.* Under resistance you feel every part that is cheating; stop
cheating and the strength arrives on its own. Korin never trained him to be big.
He trained him to be correct and treated the size as weather. Doran's summary:
*the boulder's easy — the handstand tells the truth.*

Wren rolled her sleeve and flexed a slender, hairless, freckled half-elf arm in
counterargument. First real laugh out of either of them since the north road. Wren
declined the idea of strength training on instrument grounds — if something reaches
her, the argument was lost several steps earlier — but conceded running in
principle and refused it in scheduling.

## T550.2b — The Wander

Doran's proposal: walk until they see somebody worth talking to. Neither had
anything to do, and Wren had to check honestly before she believed it.

**Filtered air.** Wren's pipe smoke stops existing roughly four feet up. No haze,
no residue, no smell in cloth or hair, anywhere inside the walls. Never once
offered to her as a feature. She refuses to ask Niv how it works on the grounds
that an answer would convert a kindness into a system.

**Statue gallery.** Founders and unplaced figures rendered with observed rather
than stylized precision. Children's drawings on the plinths and lower robe folds —
stick figures, a sun, two figures whose joined hands are one blob, seven
round-headed people with the same wide mouth, a long-legged bird with an arrow
pointing at a solemn statue's face. Some scratched, some chalk. The same hand as
the figure on Doran's knee plate; neither Steward said so. A cleaning crew works
around every drawing with a soft brush and lets the chalk ones go when they go.
Doran, faintly scandalized at the idea of restoring one: *it'd be a different
drawing.*

**Classrooms and bunk halls.** Silt-deposition rates in three hands; Dwarvish
conjugation; one room with forty chairs in a circle instead of rows. Hundreds of
beds, white silk, marble, no dust, identical folds — and a carved bird on a sill,
a child's boot beside a very large boot, somebody's hairpin waiting on a shelf.

**Doran on growing up in it.** He did not know it was strange. He thought food
simply arrived and floors were warm everywhere. First trip past Waterdeep he saw a
man walking around with a bad tooth that nobody was going to fix and that the man
did not expect anybody to fix. He gave the man coin and got Korin's version of
trouble: *what will you do tomorrow when there are forty of them.* He still has no
answer. That was the day he understood where he was from.

**The unfinished hall.** Doorless opening, two thirty-foot leaves unhung on
cradles, ceiling sweeping away, far wall three hundred yards off and hazy with
real distance. Roughly a hundred constructs working without collision, without a
foreman, almost silently. Mixed crews among them — a dwarven woman and a human man
arguing amiably over a curve, two half-orcs laying facing stone by hand and slowly
and on purpose, a tiefling in the ribs with a plumb line, a goliath and a gnome
sharing a bench and a flask.

Wren's read: in Waterdeep that bench is a fight, a fee, or a district somebody
isn't allowed into after dark. Here the Sanctum removed every material thing they
could have been fighting over and left them to find out whether they get along.
Cost of entry is one rule — respect — and everything else in the house is theirs.
*People have burned cities over less than what's in one of those bunk halls, and
here it costs manners.*

She named the whole place a chapel. Doran agreed it was closer to a church or a
temple than a house, said that should be wrong because people live here, and
observed that it works anyway. Wren: *it works because the god is home and does
the dishes.*

## T550.2c — The Bath

A forty-foot columned opening onto the hall, undoored, because the Sanctum has
never seen the point of a door on something nobody is ashamed of. Lavender, steam,
warm linen. Green marble steps, women bathing unhurried, children among them in
the shallow end on a project involving a copper cup. Men beyond a low wall on the
far side, no gate between; either side is open to any group that wants the same
water.

Doran looked for about a second and a half and then looked at the ceiling. Not
shyness — the line drawn into him before he could read: this is a bath, those are
people, and he is a guest in their evening. He did not blush and did not hurry.
Wren did not tease him about a thing he got right. She looked longer, appreciated
it the way you appreciate weather, and walked on.

Wren's sense-memory anchor for the Sanctum: lavender and hot stone.

## T550.2d — Letha at the Window Seat

Sunset carrying the length of the corridor. Not performance and not practice —
following a line to see where it goes and abandoning it when it goes nowhere;
she loses the thread, laughs at herself, picks it up a third lower.

Letha on a half-round landing in a loose white robe, one foot tucked, tail over
the seat back, Sunset lazy across her lap. She had heard them a hundred feet out
and had not bothered to stop.

Greeting as given: *Hey, guys! Good to see you back. And I heard we didn't kill
everyone, so that's a good turnout!* She read them properly — *all right*, not
*fine*, and said she picked the word she meant. A hundred and thirty-one out, both
grain hands, Miros upright and talking.

**Goldenfields was watched.** Marrik's projection in the east hall, a Tenday
evening, a couple hundred people. Not a review — they wanted to see them come back.
The room cheered when the Ledger came apart without understanding what it was, and
a very old dwarf near the front stood up. There is a boy in the lower school
drawing Doran on everything, badly. Letha's ruling on that: the white armor is a
poster and always was — get used to it or take it off, and he isn't taking it off.

**Korin, current.** Alive, semi-retired into teaching, the Ranger Corps his. Eight
days out north with a class of thirty, several of whom cannot hold a bow yet,
walking them to the treeline and back in stages, carrying nobody's pack and
letting nobody else carry one either, and asking each of them every night what
they saw that day. Back in six days. He knows they are home. Told before he left
that Doran had been made Steward, he said *good*, said nothing for about ten
minutes, and then said *good* again.

Letha keeps forty of his carvings on a shelf. He has either not noticed or has
decided not to say, which with him is the same thing. Doran has four, starting
with a bad bear made the morning Korin selected him; he has never asked what any
of them mean, because if he asked and Korin said nothing, he would have to give
the meaning up.

Letha's standing joke: six years and she still cannot get *ma'am* out of him.

## T550.2e — The Arrival

Sera, from down the hall: *Leethaaaa!*

Ember's glow rounds the corner first — gold over coal-red, throwing the column's
shadow and sliding it away. Then both of them: Sera barefoot and already grinning,
Ember adjacent rather than behind, kintsugi brightening toward gold, wildfire hair
moving in still air, ozone and warm sugar, contained heat.

Room law fires. Not fear — the light gets kinder, the stone warmer, and a
permanent low tension at the base of Wren's skull lets go without asking her. Both
Stewards are on their feet before either decides to be. Doran comes to actual
attention in a bathrobe.

Sera crosses the seat back in one practiced motion and perches at Letha's
shoulder, close enough to be in her horn. Letha moves her elbow two inches and
keeps playing. **The perch ritual runs correctly: no acknowledgment, no
negotiation, load redistribution only, and the meeting continues.**

Sera into Wren's personal space, under her chin and looking up, cataloguing
without any concept of privacy. Wren blushes from the collarbone up, does not step
back, and grins in spite of herself. Doran, from three feet back: *I'm here too.*
The pause runs slightly long. Letha does not intervene.

**Soliera comes down the hall.** No sound, no announcement — pressure arriving
everywhere at once, vast, edgeless, and warm. Wren's name for it: peace by
absolute power, delivered as a physical fact rather than a promise. Barefoot,
silk with gold sacred geometry, dark braid over one shoulder that is Sera's work,
walking at an entirely ordinary pace. She stops at the edge of the landing, sets a
shoulder against the column, folds her arms, and watches her house be loud.

**She does not speak for the remainder of the beat.** Corridor lights she has
already passed stay warmer than they were. Letha rises, inclines her head, and
does not stop being comfortable.

Sera turns from Wren and her register changes completely. Ember takes the vacancy
instantly, closer than Sera stood, lines bright gold, and holds it. Doran looms.

## T550.2f — Tag, and the Eraser Question

Sera proposes tag, citing the earlier game. Doran, grinning: he can swing the
Cleaver one-handed now at the same speed. Sera: *I don't think that's going to
make a difference. But if you want to have some fun, we can still go play tag.*

Letha invites herself. She has never once seen either of them work in person — only
through a scrying window with two hundred people breathing in her ear. To her they
are still recruits regardless of the title. Doran, pleased: he does love showing
off, and if he puts new holes in something there will be something for Korin to
look at when he gets back. Letha: Korin would stand at the lip of it, say *hm*,
sit down, and start whittling.

Ember asks instead for the Eraser, having noticed Wren has more experience now and
noting they have not discussed it in a while.

**Wren's stop.** Pleasure first — a wizard asked to show her work has no defense
against it — then the arithmetic. Portal Shear is one centimetre by fifty feet,
passes through intervening matter, and does not stop at walls. Behind the wall:
the bath, then a dormitory, then three hundred yards of unfinished cathedral with
a hundred people in it. She has one until she sleeps. Her ruling to Ember: before
she shows anything, somebody has to tell her where in this house she is allowed to
point it. She was conspicuously not looking at Soliera when she said it.

She also let slip the capstone shape — four centimetres, two hundred feet, one
wave — and that she has had it in her head for months and cannot cast it yet.

## T550.2g — The Grass Courtyard

Sera proposes snow. Ember refuses snow and picks the grass courtyard. Sera agrees
with both fists together in an *aha*. Letha puts Sunset down, which she does for
almost nothing, and comes along.

Out the mountain side, which should be the cold side. Bright honest afternoon sun,
early-autumn clouds lit gold underneath, hundreds of feet of deep cropped grass.
Pale stone benches worn smooth. Six or seven low wide hard-branched trees set at
generous intervals — low enough for children, spreading enough for a group to sit
under in a circle. Behind and above, the real mountain with real snow on it,
several thousand feet up. No wall, no dome, no seam. Wren stopped in the arch and
hated that she always stops in the arch.

Doran asked after his kit. Sera went and returned around the far side of the
courtyard carrying the entire three hundred pounds in one armload — plate stacked
past the top of her head, helm on top, daggers in the middle, the Giant Cleaver
laid lengthwise across the whole tower — walking at a stroll and chatting over her
shoulder. She tossed it down. The pieces cannot scuff and did not; the Cleaver
landed flat with a single solid impact felt through the ground ten paces away. One
bird left a tree. Nothing else in the courtyard reacted.

Wren, barefoot in a borrowed robe, allowed she was fine as she was. Ember: *of
course you're fine. The magic comes from me — or, no, it comes from you. And then
you ask, and we just say yes.* Then: *I love having a priest. Or — is it priestess,
because you're a girl?* She did not wait for the answer, having asked, and wandered
toward the open field to give Doran room to dress.

**Beat closes here.** Doran mid-dress, Ember's question unanswered, tag unplayed.
