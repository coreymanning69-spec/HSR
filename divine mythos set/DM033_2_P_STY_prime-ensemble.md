## IV. EVAN — THE QUIET BACKBONE

**Archetype:** Father figure / the world before / the one who stayed
**Tier:** 1 (Baseline human, civilian)

Evan raised Soliera Prime as his own after her mother vanished. He is a farmer, an irrigation expert, and a man who understands systems — water tables, crop rotation, the logic of things that grow slowly and reliably. He is not magical. He is not enhanced. He is the most ordinary person in Soliera's orbit, and that is exactly his function.

He was given a pendant by Soliera's mother before she disappeared. He doesn't know what it does. He's never tried to find out. He wears it because she asked him to, and because some promises don't require understanding to keep.

He was one of the first people to understand, quietly and without drama, that Arden was not a guardian in any sense that fit a human framework. He didn't say anything about it. He adjusted. Evan is a man who deals with what's in front of him — if the divine shows up in his backyard, he feeds it breakfast and asks if it takes tea.

He knows Soliera is not normal. He has known since the first winter in Cradle when the frost didn't touch their garden. He has never worshipped her for it, never feared her for it, never used it. He treats her with a casual, steady awe that is the most dignified response the narrative offers.

**Current status:** Evan lives at Outpost Seven. Church contact kept him away
from Soliera for a time; the older record calls that interval his
"disappearance." Soliera eventually decided she had spent long enough without
her father and asked him to come—not as a directive or protection order, but
because she wanted him there. He came. He has unlimited clearance at the
super-secure Outpost for no documented reason other than existing there under
Soliera and Arden's presence. He remains a completely ordinary man inside that
security envelope. No one at the Outpost has found it productive to raise this
as a concern. He tends a small garden in the outer compound that is,
statistically, performing better than any equivalent plot in a similar climate
should.

He carves wood in his spare time — small things, careful work. He has left carved sunflowers at the chapel threshold without announcement or attribution. Children at the Outpost identify him freely as "Soliera's papa," which is close enough to accurate that he doesn't correct it. He sweeps the chapel steps each morning before anyone else is awake, not because anyone asked him to and not because he worships there, but because it needs doing and he was up. He feeds birds from the outer wall and walks the gardens in the evening, often humming to himself. He is, by institutional metrics, the least remarkable person at Outpost Seven. Operatives with enough time there learn to watch what they say around him anyway — not out of fear, but out of the quiet understanding that he is the one person Soliera has chosen to keep close, and that means something nobody has found a classification for.


## V. VERIS — FIRST THING NOBODY DESIGNED

**Archetype:** Engineered equal / trauma-forged mirror / the only successful prototype
**Tier:** 3.5 (Enhanced human / experimental divine-counterbalance intent; enhanced-human result)

### Origin

Veris is what happens when the Company, the Church, and a coalition of independent operators all look at Soliera Prime and collectively decide: *we need a solution to this.* Since Arden cannot be engaged, and Soliera cannot be directly touched, the only viable approach, in project language, was to engineer something close enough to her in conceptual weight to matter — not to fight her, but to neutralize her *influence*.

Every faction contributed what they had. The Company brought surgical enhancement tech and behavioral conditioning infrastructure. The Church contributed miracle-class blessing attempts, relic exposure protocols, and tiered soul-anchoring theory. Independent operators brought folklore, folk magic, theoretical divine genetics, and a significant amount of guesswork. The result was Veris — the only successful prototype of Project DIVIDE.

She was born into a lab in the sense that her first memories are of rooms with controlled temperature and people with clipboards. She has reinforced skeletal structure, modified tendon sheaths, cybernetic organs that supplement rather than replace organic function, and an instinctual self-repair capability that operates below conscious thought. She was trained from infancy. Not educated — *trained*. Combat, threat assessment, emotional suppression, mission compliance.

Her appearance is not imposing at first read: roughly 5'4"–5'5", white-blonde hair, freckles, a compact frame, and the stillness of someone whose body was calibrated before she had words for ownership. DIVIDE regulated her endocrine cycles, fertility, and thermal baseline as performance variables. She is sterilized. Her hormones were managed. Her body runs cooler than ordinary human comfort because cooler muscle function gave the program better output. To Veris, this is not shocking information. It is weather. It is how her body has always been.

She has been opened, rebuilt, measured, and medically audited enough times that "privacy" does not arrive in her mind as an instinctive right. The horror of this is not the gore of it. The horror is how administrative it was, and how normal the result feels to her.

She is extraordinarily dangerous. She is also profoundly broken in the specific way that people become broken when they have never been allowed to be a person.

### Behavior and Current State

Veris arrived at Outpost Seven under a civilian cover that dissolved faster than anyone expected. She is not good at pretending to be ordinary. She holds her body like someone waiting for the next drill. She over-explains. She under-emotes. She uses operational language for personal interactions because operational language is the only register she was taught.

Proximity to Soliera Prime is disrupting her baseline. Not through any active effect — just through the persistent experience of being around someone who is genuinely, unperformatively kind. Veris doesn't have a framework for it. She keeps waiting for the agenda behind it. There isn't one. This is destabilizing in ways that feel, to Veris, remarkably similar to a system error.

**Recent beats:** Veris, Soliera, Cassian, and Veyren spent a day swimming together in the outer compound — Cassian's idea, Veyren's enthusiasm, Soliera simply present, Veris watching what "recreational" looked like and attempting it. It wasn't a disaster. After, Soliera walked her through creating her own room at the Outpost — not assigning her a billet, but helping Veris build a space that was hers. The closet came last. Infinite, context-aware: appropriate clothes because belonging is a function of Soliera's reality field, not wardrobe planning. Veris stood in it for longer than she would admit.

The recovered bedroom opening fixes the room's physical register. The closet is a
single doorway flush with the wall, infinite space folded down because Veris told
Soliera she had never chosen her own outfit. The walls are light blue. The bed is
large and soft beneath a fluffy gray-and-pink blanket. The room is feminine without
apology. Unlike the Divineverse household, Veris sleeps clothed: tank top and
underwear, no bra, a small private concession to comfort rather than a display.

That night: rooms across the hall from each other. "Goodnight, Soliera." — said across the hall, into the dark. "Goodnight, Veris." — said back, heard or not. Neither hears the other. Both sleep.

The arc pauses there.

**The arc destination:** They will fall in love. It will be the first thing in Veris's life that nobody designed.


## VI. PROJECT DIVIDE — INSTITUTIONAL RECORD

**Classification:** Cross-faction Tier-escalation program / divine counterbalance initiative
**Status:** Suspended following prototype assessment. One product delivered. All others failed.

**Recovered synonym:** Countersign Divide. Treat this as the same program as Project DIVIDE. "Cosign" and "Cosan" are archaeology drift and should not be reused as live labels.

### The Problem

Soliera Prime's existence presented a classification crisis for every major power structure in the known world. She could not be tiered. She could not be licensed. Arden could not be engaged, contained, or negotiated with. Her effect on local reality was measurable and inexplicable by existing frameworks. She was not hostile, which made conventional threat response inapplicable. She was not controllable, which made institutional management impossible.

The consensus position — reached independently by the Company's Executive Tier, the Church's restricted advisory councils, and multiple independent operator coalitions — was that a counterbalance was needed. Not a weapon. A weight. Something conceptually proximate enough to Soliera to exert influence on the equation she represented. The counterweight was the program's intent. Veris was the result.

### The Architecture

Project DIVIDE was a coalition effort. No single faction controlled it. Each contributed within their operational specialty:

**The Company** provided: surgical enhancement infrastructure, behavioral conditioning facilities, mission architecture, logistics, and the threat modeling that defined what "counterbalance" needed to mean in operational terms. Company scientists designed Veris's hardware — the skeletal reinforcement, the cybernetic organ suite, the tendon modifications.

**The Church** provided: relic exposure protocols, tiered soul-anchoring theory, miracle-class blessing attempts, and access to Church Keys as developmental conduits. The Church's interest was in producing something that could function in proximity to divine-level phenomena without collapse — essentially, a stable vessel for Church-tier intervention against an unclassifiable entity. Their blessing attempts on Veris produced partial results; she is not a Saint and does not carry a formal Church designation, but something in her architecture responds to miracle-class stimuli in ways that remain poorly understood.

**Independent operators** provided: folk-magic theory, divine genetics speculation, historical precedent from pre-Company records of divine encounters, and a great deal of guesswork. Their contributions were the least quantifiable and, by most assessments, the most structurally important — the parts of Veris that are not purely engineered are almost certainly the parts that work.

### The Results

DIVIDE ran multiple prototype streams. Veris is the sole survivor of successful development. The others — unknown number, records restricted — did not stabilize. Whether this means death, catastrophic failure, or something else is classified above most operatives' clearance. The Company and the Church have agreed, without formalizing the agreement, not to discuss it.

Veris was assessed, upon completion of training, as a Tier 3.5. This was considered a success relative to original projections. It was also considered a failure relative to what DIVIDE was supposed to produce. She is not proximate to Soliera Prime in conceptual weight. She is not a counterbalance to anything at divine scale. She is a remarkably effective enhanced human who was built for a purpose she was never going to be able to fulfill, and nobody involved told her that. The pathos of Project DIVIDE is the gap between the institutional name and the person who survived it.

DIVIDE has been suspended but not officially disbanded. Its files remain active. Veris's continued proximity to Soliera Prime at Outpost Seven is being monitored by both Company and Church observers through methods they believe are undetected. Arden is aware. He has not acted. Monitoring does not constitute intent.


## VII. CASSIAN — PEACEFUL KILLER

**Archetype:** World-weary expert / the one who chose this / anchor of mortal consequence
**Tier:** 4 — borderline 4.5

**Physical:** 5'9", 220 lbs. Brown hair. Dark combat slacks. The kind of build that looks unremarkable in civilian clothes until you see him move.

Cassian is famous across continents in the way that very dangerous things become famous — quietly, through reputation, through the absence of enemies who could contradict the stories. He is a veteran Company operator who has cleared dungeons solo, eliminated operatives twice his Tier classification by way of precision rather than power, and once cleared a room of twelve armed men in under six seconds in a Company internal review that would prefer not to elaborate on the specifics.

He operates at Outpost Seven now. Not because he was assigned — he chose it. He has seen enough of the world's machinery to recognize what Soliera represents, and he has chosen to be near it not out of worship or strategy, but out of something that functions like gratitude.

He is soft-spoken. His humor is dry and rarely flagged as humor until a moment after the fact. He is precise in everything — movement, language, violence. He fights the way a master calligrapher writes: each stroke intentional, nothing wasted, the result cleaner than it has any right to be. He kills when it needs doing and doesn't discuss it.

His bosses call this the **Gravemaker Doctrine**. The name is institutional, not theatrical: an average-looking man in plain clothes steps into a room, reads every angle at once, and leaves behind only the outcomes he already planned for. The contrast is the point. Nothing about Cassian announces apocalypse until the room notices it has been solved.

He sees Veris and recognizes something. The stiffness. The over-calibrated threat assessment. The exhaustion underneath the competence. He does not push. He simply makes space, consistently, and waits for her to notice it's safe. The swimming day was his idea. He did not explain why.

He is not Soliera's guardian. Arden has that covered with no room for collaboration. Cassian is something else — a human being who has seen what power costs and chosen, having paid most of those costs, to spend what's left on something worth protecting.

**Voice and tone (canon reference):** When asked about Evan by a younger operative who couldn't understand why a baseline civilian occupied such space at the Outpost, Cassian's answer was: *"He's not here to protect her from the world. He's here to protect her from being alone in it."* And after a pause: *"I've seen gods break under less."* He did not elaborate. The conversation ended there.


## VIII. VEYREN — FERAL STRENGTH

**Archetype:** Animalistic bruiser / comic counterweight / joy in combat
**Tier:** 4

**Physical:** 6'6", 350 lbs. Built like a lion's musculature wrapped around a bodybuilder's frame — which is approximately the correct description. Reflex/attack predator: uses his hands as claws, bites, tears, slices with fingers, opens gaps with grapples and closes them with impact. Regeneration is substantial enough that fighting him to a conclusion requires either ending him very fast or not engaging at all.

Veyren does not scheme. He shows up and handles things. This is not a lack of intelligence — it is a preference so deeply integrated into who he is that intelligence and directness have become synonymous for him. He assessed Soliera by being around her for three days and concluded she was worth protecting, not because Arden required it, but because that's what his instincts said. He trusts his instincts.

He spars with Varn and treats Veris's combat capability with genuine respect — expressed through attempting to fight her, grinning when she does well, and telling her she hits harder than most Tier 4s, which is not a compliment he gives casually. Veris does not know what to do with this. He finds this funny in a non-cruel way.

The swimming day: Veyren's enthusiasm was structural. He was already in the water before anyone else arrived and had opinions about the depth. This is consistent with everything else he does.

He is friendly chaos in a cast that trends toward control. He makes the Outpost feel lived-in.


## IX. VARN — ORC FORGE MASTER

**Archetype:** Divine-forged follower / blacksmith myth / silent observer
**Tier:** 3.8–4

**Physical:** 7'2", broad and dense with the kind of mass that comes from decades of forge work rather than combat training, though the distinction becomes academic in practice. Orc, with the bearing of a craftsman who knows exactly what he's worth and doesn't need to announce it.

In Varn's culture, metal, heat, strength, and craft are religious language. The
right to craft high-quality weapons and armor is a privilege—earned,
ritualized, and held with reverence. A master blacksmith is not a tradesman. He
is a keeper of sacred function. The blade he forges is the life it might save.
He takes this seriously in a way that most cultures cannot map.

Varn was working at his distant forge when Arden came into existence. He felt
the event through the coals and fire before he had a name for it. That
forge-signal eventually brought him to Outpost Seven, where he expected to meet
something divine and confirm his understanding of the divine. He met Soliera
Prime instead. She was eating fruit and asking Veyren to stop climbing on the
wall. His first physical calibration was a spar with Veyren; they proved
approximately equal.

He has recalibrated. His reverence for Arden remains, but it has been contextualized. The guardian serves the girl. The girl is the point. Varn is now in the process of understanding what it means that the god he came to find exists entirely in relation to a human teenager who is not, in any external display, terrifying. He watches her. He waits. He is not a fanatic — he is a craftsman, and he is taking accurate measurements before he decides what to build.

He will forge for her if she asks. He suspects she won't ask directly. He suspects she'll wander into his forge space one day and look at the materials and say something curious about a particular alloy, and he'll start building.


## X. GOLD — FIRST COMPANION

**Archetype:** Summoned companion / emotional anchor / first thing she called by choice
**Classification:** Not a guardian construct — a companion. This distinction is load-bearing.

Gold appeared shortly after Soliera Prime completed her first formal magical education at the Outpost — a structured sequence working through all six major magic schools. The summoning happened almost immediately after the lessons concluded, before the ink was dry on any kind of assessment. The teacher present was visibly impressed, which is notable given the caliber of instruction at Outpost Seven.

Gold is dog-like in form and disposition: protective, warm, emotionally present in a way that constructs are not. Has a flight mode. Responds to Soliera's emotional state more than to explicit commands. Gold was not designed, not assigned, not approved through any Company or Church process. Gold exists because Soliera reached for something and something answered.

The distinction from Arden is structural: Arden is protection — absolute, silent, operating on hostile intent, requiring nothing from Soliera to function. Gold is *company*. Gold is what she wanted, not what she needed. That gap between need and want is the entire point of Gold's existence in the narrative.

**Status:** Present at Outpost Seven. Gold stays around the school as Soliera
moves through her education and has effectively become the school's unofficial
mascot: a small summoned companion rather than a developing cosmic office. The
relationship is ongoing and warm. No institutional classification. No threat
assessment has been filed. Arden has not reacted to Gold's presence in any
documented way.


## XI. FATHER ARREN — MIRACLE MAGIC TEACHER

**Archetype:** True believer / quiet witness / faith without agenda
**Tier:** 2–3 (Church-trained, access to minor miracle work)

Father Arren is one of the teachers assigned to Outpost Seven's expanded educational wing. He teaches Miracle Magic — the Church's formally structured access to divine-adjacent spell work. Most of the Outpost's instructors are human; a few are not. Arren is human, ordained, and by institutional measure, unremarkable.

His distinction is his read of Soliera Prime. Where the Church's official
position is cautious surveillance—classify, monitor, do not engage without
authorization—Arren watches her lessons and recognizes that she does not
channel divine power or cast magic in the way the Church teaches. She simply
makes the intended truth real. His private theological shorthand is direct:
Soliera is the female version of Jesus. He teaches her without agenda, without
the sideways attention of someone building a report. He is one of the few
people at the Outpost whose interest in Soliera is purely devotional, and whose
devotion is genuinely, quietly joyful rather than fearful.

He does not discuss this openly. He simply teaches.

**The sunflower:** One morning Arren arrived at the chapel to find a small carved wooden sunflower left at the threshold — no note, no claim. A child told him she had seen "Soliera's papa" place it down and walk off humming. Arren held the carving that evening and did not reach for any institutional framework to file it under. His private assessment: *That man does not ask for miracles. He becomes them, quietly.* He has kept the carving.

**Status:** Active at Outpost Seven. His relationship with Soliera is one of respectful warmth — he does not overwhelm her with reverence, which is part of why she lets him teach her. His position within the Church's formal monitoring structure is standard. His actual feelings about it are his own.
