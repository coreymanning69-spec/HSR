## M181 — Letha & Veyren, First Night

Cassian stands.

It is a reflex, the kind that outlives the contexts that trained it — standing for his parents' guests, for visiting officers, for anyone who deserved acknowledgment before they could insist they didn't need it. He does not say anything about standing. Letha does not need him to. He stands because the reflex is honest, and then he sits back down.

The evening deepens. The streetlamps outside have settled into their steady electrical glow and the house has reached that hour where the light inside becomes warmer than the light outside and the windows turn into mirrors at the edges.

Soliera is on the silk bench with her book. She has not moved in forty minutes, which is how she reads — completely, without the small adjustments ordinary people make to remind themselves they are still bodies. The God-at-rest register: she is here, she has allowed the room to contain this, no presentation required. Cassian has the side table and a cup of coffee that has been at the same temperature since Velin enforced the second pot and then disappeared into the upper floors with characteristic efficiency. The cup is not getting cold because Cassian does not let cups get cold; this is the sort of discipline that extends automatically to every context, including the one where Veyren is developing an increasingly obvious problem.

In the corner of the room, Ember has the wide couch and is using all of it. She had been snoring an hour ago — consistent, unbothered, with the regularity of someone who has no unresolved questions about the evening. Now she has stopped. At some point she and the couch reached an arrangement.

Veyren has shifted positions three times in the last twenty minutes. He is not uncomfortable — his body does not really do uncomfortable — but he is *aware*. Letha had settled earlier, at the point where the evening made settling the natural choice, and the robe had settled around her honestly, the way cloth does when it does not have an agenda. His senses run a constant background process. The process was designed for other applications and has been doing something else entirely for several hours — the warmth coming off her skin, the slow regulation of her breathing, the specific quality of her stillness, which is the stillness of someone who is also aware. Every time he catches himself looking he looks away. Every time he looks away he looks back.

Letha knows. Of course she knows.

Cassian picks up his cup. He does not look at Veyren. He studies something interesting in the middle distance, which is a skill he has developed to considerable depth and deploys on Veyren's behalf more often than Veyren knows.

From the direction of the rug — at the exact angle that makes it easy to watch something without appearing to — Sera says, quietly: "Good boy."

She does not elaborate. She does not need to. It lands with the precise gentleness of someone who knows exactly how much weight one well-placed observation can carry. She picks something up beside her on the floor and looks at it for a moment. Then she says nothing else, which is the correct follow-through, and the room resettles around the observation as if it had always been there.

Veyren breathes.

At some point — the exact moment being less important than the fact of it — Letha looks over at him. Not the sideways awareness she has been running all evening. Directly. With the quality of someone who has decided that the game where they both pretend not to have decided something has run its course.

Veyren's hand moves before his decision does. He reaches over and tucks two fingers behind the hinge of her ear — not her ear exactly, the warm junction where the jaw meets the hair — and pulls, gently, the way you redirect something you want to keep close.

"You're only supposed to look at me like that," he says, "if you mean it."

Letha's tail curls once at the tip. She does not stop looking at him.

"I know," she says.

From the silk bench, Soliera turns a page. Cassian refills his cup without looking. The expression he does not make is not a smile, exactly. The house holds the evening around them like a fact.

After a while, Letha stands. Veyren stands. Neither of them says anything about standing.

Cassian refills his cup. Soliera does not look up. Ember's breathing is slow and even from the couch. The streetlamps outside have the patient, indifferent glow of infrastructure doing its job.

The house breathes.


*Coda — night, after:*

Sera is the last one in the common room. She sits for a while in the specific quiet of a house that has just resolved something — not a loud quiet, just the kind where the furniture feels slightly more settled than it was before. The bench still holds the warm impression of Soliera's evening. The side table holds Cassian's cup, rinsed and returned to position.

She does not hurry. She lets the room be settled for a moment the way you let something good be true before you move.

When she goes upstairs the house is already in its night grammar — Ember's breathing behind the couch she never quite vacated, the rooms along the upper hall behind closed doors. Soliera is in the bedroom's country and does not require an announcement. Sera crosses the room in the dark, barefoot on the floor that is warmer than floors should be, and the night does what the night does, and the house does not keep records of this.

Outside, the town goes on. The Sound Curtain holds its geometry. The second pot is cold on the kitchen counter and no one is coming back for it.


## M182 — The Morning After

The dressing ritual happens on the bedroom floor.

This is not information the household needs. The household does not have it, which is the way it works — the bedroom is its own country with its own sequence, and what happens there before the day starts belongs to that sequence. Sera dresses accent-inward: ribbons before clothes, the small things before the shape of the thing. She dresses Soliera first. Always. This morning she takes her time, because the morning has a long way to go before it becomes a day and neither of them is in a hurry.

When Soliera is dressed, Sera may wander.


In the kitchen, Veyren is shirtless. This is not a statement. He got up, he started coffee, and Velin's second pot was already brewing when he arrived — which meant Velin had been here before him, which meant the kitchen had already been organized in the Velin way, which is a way that communicates without words that whoever organized it was here first and whoever arrived now is welcome to the results. He found the eggs anyway. His physiology requires them in quantity; this is not a thing the physiology negotiates.

He plates the eggs. Six for him. Two for Letha. He slides her plate across the counter and their fingers don't touch this time but the distance is close enough that the warmth between their hands is a shared thing.

Letha has the chipped cup. She did not ask which cup to take. She just took it, and this is a thing Veyren notes and does not say anything about.

Velin moves through the kitchen without looking at either of them. She has the apron on and her tablet in the other hand and is running three tracks simultaneously, all kitchen-adjacent — this is the version of her that operates at this hour before she has sorted whether the morning is household work or administrative work or whether the distinction is worth maintaining. The second pot is confirmed and cycling. She does not sit down. The cook does not sit before the second pot. This is not a rule she has ever needed to state. It is a rule the kitchen has learned to accommodate around her.

Somewhere near the supply shelves, Ember is on the floor. She had been snoring forty minutes ago — the consistent, unbothered kind that comes from someone with no unresolved questions about the night. She is not snoring now. She is here, on the floor, with a piece of bread in each hand, holding them the way someone holds two things they are in the process of comparing. She has not explained what she is comparing.

The Sound Curtain holds. Outside, the town has been awake for over an hour. Inside, the house is quiet in the specific way of a space that has made a structural decision about noise.


Then Sera appears at the counter like she materialized. Barefoot, silk — the dress Niv made, with the shoulder slip — arriving without sound, without transition, the way weather arrives.

Letha looks up.

"Edible," she says.

The word exits before she has fully committed to it. What happens in the next second makes this completely evident: the blush, which is dusky bronze and thorough and travels from her jaw to her collar, and then the project of looking at her coffee cup as if she said something administrative.

Veyren makes a sound that is not words.

Cassian, who has been at the table with his mug and his own particular version of attending to things that are not his business, does not look up. He sets the mug down with a small, precise motion and says, with the measured delivery of a man who has been in Veyren's operational proximity long enough to develop a clinical vocabulary for these moments:

"Breathe. Breathe slower."

Veyren breathes slower.

Sera grins. She does not say anything about what Letha said. She does not have to. She takes an egg off Veyren's plate with her fingers. Eats it standing. Grins at him.

"Good boy. You cook now."

The blush hits his neck. "I've always cooked."

"You cook *here* now. That's different." She takes another egg. "That means you're staying."

The word *staying* sits in the kitchen and the kitchen does not make a production out of it.


Velin takes the apron off.

Not when the cooking is finished — when the serving is finished, which is a distinction she holds carefully and which the household has learned to read as signal. She folds it over the counter edge. Smooths the silks underneath. The house rule restates itself without Velin restating it: the cook does not sit until the second pot is confirmed and cycling. The second pot is confirmed and cycling. Velin sits.

The table fills.

Ember gets to her stool first, already eating something — not the eggs, something she identified from the lower shelf on the way past. Sera beside her. Soliera comes last — the room settles into its real shape around her the way it always does when she arrives, without announcement, without presentation. When Sera pulls the stool out for her one-handed without looking up from what she's doing, the motion is so practiced it barely registers as an event. Letha beside Ember, tail easy along the rung of the stool. Veyren next to Letha, still shirtless, coffee in hand, the calibration lag still running in the small unconscious adjustments he keeps making to his baseline — sitting here, at this table, in this kitchen, being the kind of man who slid a plate across the counter and knew which cup pours right. Cassian opposite, plate precise, mug at the eleven-o'clock position he always sets it at.

Soliera reads something.


Cassian has not moved from his chair.

He sees Veyren's hand go to his shoulder — the left, not the right. The motion is small and private and habitual, the kind a body makes when it is checking something it checks quietly. The fingers press and release and then the hand is back around the mug.

He files it. Does not mention it. Soliera's smile has not changed. Ember has stopped snoring. The house breathes.

In Velin's notebook, on the kitchen counter, there is a letter. It has been there since M180, routed through the person who handles thresholds, calendars, and consequences rather than handed directly. Velin has not opened the notebook. The morning is not ready for it yet. The morning is still doing the work of establishing that everyone is here and fed and that the calibration lag is a thing kitchen tables can address over time. The letter will wait.

The Sound Curtain holds. The town is outside. The family is inside.

The house breathes.

## M183 — The Japanese Flag (Setup)

STATUS NOTE:
M183 is locked as the breakfast-letter reveal and formal TR-JP handoff. The
exact wording of Kiyuru's physical letter is not quoted in this file; the canon
event is the breakfast reveal, the household processing, and the routing bridge
that begins TR-JP after M183.3.

Breakfast is still in the domestic register M182 established: Velin's second pot
is law, the table is crowded, and Veyren is present in the vulnerable aftermath
of choosing Letha and being chosen back. The Sound Curtain holds the house in
its own weather. The town remains outside. Inside, the room is still calibrating
around coffee, food, Sera's outfit, Letha's blush, and the fact that some mornings
ask people to be normal immediately after their lives have changed.

Velin chooses that morning to open the notebook.

The letter has been there since M180, routed through Velin instead of handed
directly to the girls. That routing matters. Kiyuru does not force himself into
the house's center; he sends the invitation through the person who handles
thresholds, calendars, guests, meals, protocol, and consequences. Velin makes it
breakfast business because breakfast is where the household becomes visible to
itself. The Japanese flag is not presented as a sightseeing suggestion. It is a
theological and institutional contact point: Executive J's lane, Kiyuru's shrine
line, the imperial treasures, and Japan's already-active category for divine
visitation all arriving at the table at once.

Cassian has already been briefed. That fact lands cleanly because it is Cassian:
of course he has the file, the risk map, the diplomatic outline, and the version
of events that can survive institutional language. Veyren has not been told. That
also lands cleanly, but with weight. The trip appears on top of a morning where
he is still processing Letha, still shirtless in the house's grammar, still newly
inside a partnership that is casual, valued, and not worship. Japan immediately
tests that grammar by separating them. Letha goes into Kiyuru's orbit. Veyren
stays.

Sera has not been told. Ember has not been told. Ember will lose her mind when
the shape of it reaches her: a whole country with shrine rules, old treasures,
children and elders to bow back to, and a machine-pilot girl waiting somewhere in
the Japan lane. Soliera remains silent. Her silence does not read as ignorance.
It reads as the table discovering the schedule after the authority has already
allowed the door to exist.

The Kusanagi thread is what turns the invitation from optional diplomacy into
the bridge. At M180, Kusanagi went quiet in Sera's hand. The sword that had been
humming for centuries found what it was looking for and stilled. Kiyuru carries
two of the three imperial treasures; Yasakani no Magatama remains at the
Emperor's shrine. That unresolved third-treasure geometry points toward Japan
before anyone says the word itinerary. The Company arm can brief and coordinate,
but the shrine country is already awake to what the girls are.

By the end of the breakfast reveal, the handoff is formal: M183.3 closes the
Mergeverse setup and opens TR-JP. This is not the Faerun itinerary continuing by
map order, and it is not a detached Primeverse dossier entry. It is a
Mergeverse-triggered Travel Record cluster with its own regional namespace:
TR-JP-01 begins after M183.3. Cassian is briefed. Veyren stays. Letha enters
Kiyuru's orbit. The Japan opening proceeds from there.
