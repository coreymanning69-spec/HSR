# DM038_N4 — Storm King's Thunder — Played Narrative (T541–T542.0)
## T541 — Lyn Armaal / Cloud-Giant Arc Close

Doran and Wren finish the cloud-giant arc and teleport out of Lyn Armaal mid-fall to the Sanctum. Sansuri stands as a hopeful ally. The Sanctum's willingness to help the cloud giants is public to everyone positioned to know it.

Felgolos is held as possession rather than child, so a settlement exists. Wren offers Sansuri Giant Queen candidacy; Sansuri accepts under the conditions of no slaves and no torture. Sansuri also discloses undeclined Thayan-remnant contact. No further cloud-arc dialogue or downstream Kraken outcome is authored here.

### T541.0 — The Search

Doran and Wren left Silverymoon under their own power and hunted the sky for a
castle that does not stay where it was last seen. Five days of grid work above
the Dessarin (1d6 → 5) before the cloud resolved out of ordinary weather into
something that held an edge: three conjoined towers of smoothly mortared stone,
a gatehouse shoved out the front, and a slender spire rising out of the central
keep with a lens in it. Not weather. Architecture riding weather.

### T541.1 — Contact

The approach angle came in badly (1d20 → 5) — the geometry put them low and
across the gatehouse face instead of clean to it, which meant the wall saw them
first and had the better position for four full minutes. Five cloud giants on
the crenellations, none of them moving.

Doran hailed. What answered was not the wall. From somewhere deep inside the
mortar something screamed — too big, too low, too much chest behind it, the note
bending as it ran out of air. Wren's crown motes broke their orbit spacing for
the first time in days, tightened, and resettled. Neither Steward said *dragon*
out loud. Both of them knew the shape of it and what it means when a dragon's
voice is doing that. The five on the wall did not react at all. It was ordinary
to them.

Four minutes of nothing. High up, the lens turned and caught the sun, and Wren
registered the specific unpleasantness of being *appraised* rather than assessed
— the white and gold, the six stars in the weave, the seven motes that were not
going out, the mirror-white seven-foot thing beside her returning the entire sky.
Filed. Priced. (Telescope read 1d20 → 12: significant, curious, no alarm.)

The screaming stopped inside that window. It stopped because whoever was doing
it was interrupted — because someone left the work to go look through a lens.
Whatever was in that castle in pain got a rest that day on account of the two of
them being interesting. Neither Steward missed the implication.

### T541.2 — The Stair

Non-hostile approach, so the house gave the house's answer: land unmolested,
castellan escort to the audience chamber. Cressaro came down to meet them —
twenty-four feet, plain bracers, an administrator's voice, correct rather than
warm (1d20 → 13). He named the hold as Lyn Armaal and the lady as Countess
Sansuri, and delivered her instruction verbatim: nothing armed loose on the
stair. Not a disarm order. *Loose.*

The stair itself was cloud sculpted into four-foot risers, slush-cold and opaque
and semisolid, and it held weight.

Wren put the Sanctum's condition on record before crossing the threshold, framed
as advance notice rather than accusation: the Sanctum does not leave a slave in a
room, and it does not leave a prisoner being hurt for what it knows. Cressaro did
not deny that a prisoner existed.

Then the precedent, recited aloud to a cloud giant officer for the first time —
Svardborg settled with Storvald's jarlship intact, Ironslag settled with Zalto
alive and signed, the hill giant steading still standing with its chief alive.
Frost, fire, hill. Nobody died at the table.

### T541.3 — The Gatehouse Gallery

Corridor of plinthed objects under continual flame in gold sconces — the
collection announcing itself before its owner did. A gate guard tried hierarchy
on them, looming and declining to move aside (house push 1d20 → 13, actor 1d4 →
1). It failed in four seconds and cost neither Steward a word. Their threat
calibration is permanently broken by years of Sanctum proximity; a twenty-four
foot man in a hallway does not register on people who take breakfast near Sera.

Asked how long the countess had been at her survey, Cressaro answered the
logistics honestly and too fast (1d20 → 7) — mornings, again after the household
settles, and *some nights it runs very long* — then heard himself, and did not
correct it. Consistent with the man: logistical questions answered faster than
moral ones.

### T541.4 — The Audience Chamber

Lapis lazuli tilework, marble bleachers, an alabaster throne set with
seventy-two gemstones, two cloud giant statues holding mithral-tipped spears,
and murals overhead of cloud giants on rocs and in griffon-drawn sky-chariots
hunting silver dragons. Before the throne, a ten-foot hole in the floor ringed
in gold marble, white alabaster, and lapis. Four aarakocra simulacra stood at
attention with their backs to it.

Sansuri received them in the grief mask. The opening register landed at the floor
(1d20 → 1): no greeting offered, and in place of one she had **Auren Vel** price
her guests' possessions aloud, in front of them, before welcome.

Auren was exact under pressure, which is her tell. She read the robe as Archmagi
work carrying six stars not standard to the pattern. She identified the Crown as
holding without concentration, which should not be possible. She read the plate
as mithral *remade rather than forged.* She reported the Cleaver's mass as wrong
for its size and declined to price it at all.

Sansuri's closing instruction on the appraisal: *then you'll look at it properly,
later.*

Doran read the room at Insight 32. Three things landed. The grief mask is genuine
mood rather than costume, and the mood is frustration carried over from a morning
that failed her. The appraisal was reflex and not strategy, and she knew it
herself the moment she said *later.* And Auren Vel is not staff. She is inventory
that talks.

Wren read surface only at 14: the composure is performance and the mask is doing
the work.

### T541.5 — The Cleaver, the Apology, the Business

Doran gave her the closer look she had asked for.

He set the Giant Cleaver point-first into the audience-chamber floor — the same
gesture he made in the dirt at Amphail at T539.6, offered here as compliance
rather than de-escalation. The physics are not a flourish and are not adjustable:
two hundred and eight pounds of mithral that Ember compressed out of a much
larger mass until it was heavy enough, six feet of it, unbalanced, and no
scabbard exists in the world that fits it, so ground and floor are its canonical
rest state (DM041_C §Giant Cleaver). It dropped roughly four feet under its own
weight. It went point-first through lapis tile and into the stone beneath and
stopped seven inches down, standing upright on its own. A star-crack opened in
the tile around it. The gold marble inlay lifted at the edge.

Then he leaned on the crossguard.

His position was two things at once, and neither was a posture. The apology for
her floor was sincere and unqualified. And: *Lady Ember made this for me with her
own hands and you will never have it.* He does not like people thinking they can
take his things, and he said so in a sovereign's hall while standing on her
ruined tile.

Sansuri received the apology at the ceiling (1d20 → 20), and it was her best
moment in the arc. She conceded the earlier discourtesy unprompted and with
precision: pricing a guest's possessions in her own hall before welcoming them is
what one does to cargo, it is beneath the house and beneath her, and having done
it out of temper does not excuse it. She called the floor and the appraisal even.
She recovered the room entirely, and Doran's read of her rose materially on the
strength of it.

He retrieved the Cleaver one-handed afterward, with no visible load, and returned
it to the back-carry. The hole stayed.

Wren then delivered the Sera warning as courtesy rather than threat: the
Sanctum's gods arrive unannounced, Barefoot Death is unpredictable and takes no
instruction, she has been flirting with what remains of the silver dragons, and
four walls of giants hunting silver would likely come down on principle.

### T541.6 — The Audience

It turned into genuine mutual respect, and it turned because the disclosures were
real. Named in that room: the Vonindod's sentience, and that Soliera gave it life
— the first time that has been said aloud outside the Sanctum. Bane's champion
and Lolth, named as conspirators to a giant sovereign. Eltabbar offered as a
credential rather than a boast. The Sanctum's jurisdictional claim over metallic
dragons, stated plainly. Fragment provenance, which Sansuri had been chasing from
a telescope platform, handed to her.

Wren floated the Giant Queen candidacy with two conditions attached: no slaves,
no torture. Sansuri accepted the candidacy under those conditions and opened the
alliance on her own initiative.

Sansuri disclosed in return: Thayan remnants have approached her. Undeclined —
and unrefused in writing.

The order-of-things exchange closed it. Wren attributed the North's dislocation to
ripples rather than plan: her Ladies moved, and everything downstream is still
finding its level. It will not stop. It will settle. Those are different.

### T541.7 — Felgolos and the House

Felgolos was never named aloud, never confirmed, never produced. Sansuri promised
the house would be settled and clear before the next meeting, then walked toward
the down stair. Doran read the promise at Insight 35 as sincere, and ambiguous in
the worst direction. Felgolos is held as possession rather than child, which is
what makes a settlement available at all rather than a reckoning inevitable.

The tour ran afterward. Auren Vel, appraiser, catalog entry two hundred and six —
a woman who knows every object in the hold including the ones with mechanisms,
and who wants an exit that does not involve falling. Count Thullen in the
greenhouse, tending growing things in the middle of all of it. Cloud giant
children confirmed aboard, which changes the arithmetic of every future option
involving this castle. An entire deck skipped on the tour without comment.

At the threshold Wren said the last thing, and said it kindly: Sansuri would like
Sera, they organise the world by what is worth keeping and neither has ever been
talked out of anything, and she should ask for her when she sends word. Delivered
in total ignorance of what is held below.

Then Auren, near the bleachers with her ledger, professional and brisk and
entirely unremarkable to anyone not listening (1d20 → 18). The offer to catalogue
the sword stands. She does thorough work. Everything in this castle has a number.
**Entry two hundred and six.** She would have liked to show them that one.

Doran heard it land. He did not turn his head. **Not yet acted on.**

### T541.8 — Departure

They left the way the room offered: through the ten-foot hole in the throne room
floor. Doran went first, which is the whole statement — seven feet of plate
walking into a ten-foot gap in a sovereign's audience chamber and simply stepping
off, no pause, no glance down, hands loose. Wren stepped off after him.

The castle's underside slid up past them, then the cloud, then daylight and a
great deal of open air, and for about four seconds two Sanctum Stewards were
falling out of the sky under Lyn Armaal with the moor turning below.

Then the air folded and there was nothing there at all.

Four giant lords. Four sit-downs. No blood at any of them, and this one did not
even need the sword — which Doran privately regrets by about five percent. Wren
did not say the other thing out loud, walking out of the fold onto solid grass.
But she noticed it: a year ago she would have been shaking. Today she gave a
cloud giant countess a crown and a condition in the same breath and never once
wondered whether she was allowed to.

### T541.9 — Coda: Sansuri Alone

The hall was very quiet after they went.

She stood where she had stopped and looked at the floor. Not at the hole they
left through. At the other one — seven inches into stone through four hundred
years of lapis, a clean star-crack radiating out, a lip of gold marble lifted and
settled again, and a fine pale dust that had already stopped moving.

What she worked out was not about the weight. It was the arm. He opened his hand
at a chosen height and two hundred and eight pounds of unbalanced razor went
exactly where he intended and stopped upright without wobble, and came back out
one-handed with nothing in his shoulder. Which means he could have placed it
anywhere in that room, to the inch, from any posture, with either hand.

She had been running the arithmetic the whole time. Five on the wall, twelve more
inside a horn call, the batteries, the griffons, a castle that can be elsewhere by
nightfall. There had never been any arithmetic.

And worse: no threat was ever made in that room. The warnings were courtesies.
They apologised and meant it, accepted her apology and meant that, called her the
most capable giant in the North and offered her a crown, and took not one inch of
her standing. Placed absolutely and gently, with all her dignity left on. Harder
to answer than a threat.

Auren was writing in her ledger nearby, eagerly. Sansuri noted it and thought
nothing of it.

She did not put the mask back on. She turned and walked toward the stair that
goes down.

## T542.0 — Sanctum Meeting and Departure Split

The divine trio and the Stewards meet at the Sanctum. Doran and Wren are excited to see what the stone giants have to see and say. Sera and Ember wave as the groups leave, looking forward to seeing cloud giants again at some future point.

Soliera, Sera, Ember, and Velin depart for Silverymoon. Niv and Pell begin tighter Sanctum control. Korin and a team of 150 trained knights and agents depart to strengthen the defensive capabilities of local villages, Ten Towns, and farther communities. Doran and Wren leave through the teleportation network directly toward Goldenfields. Their arrival and every stone-giant interaction remain unplayed.
