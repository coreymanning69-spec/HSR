# DM033_1 — Soliera Prime and Arden — Scope and Foundation


## I. SCOPE AND BOUNDARY

### GPT Project Standalone Role

In compact or file-capped load, DM033 is the compact Primeverse foundation when
DM014_1/DM014_2/DM014_3/DM014_4, DM022_0, and future P-beats are not uploaded. It must carry enough of
Soliera Prime, Arden, Evan, Veris, Cassian, Veyren, Varn, Project DIVIDE, the
Company, the Church, Cradle, and Outpost Seven for coherent Primeverse play and
analysis.

Use DM004_3 for broader Primeverse/Mergeverse cast retrieval and DM032_0 for
post-contact location state. Swap in DM022_0 or DM014_1/DM014_2/DM014_3/DM014_4 only when the session
needs detailed T4 registry, Executive structure, or exact institutional prose.

This file covers the world as it exists before the Merge threshold — before the girls enter Oak City, which is the event that begins the Merge era. Everything in DM033 is pre-Merge, pre-M0, pre-contact with any Divineverse arrivals.

**The Merge threshold** is Oak City's entry point. What happens there, and everything after it, belongs to Merge-era files. DM033 stops at the edge and does not cross.

**"Primeverse"** is a DM-layer organizational term. Characters in-world do not use it. They live in the world. The label exists because the DM needs to distinguish pre-Merge content from the Merge era and the Divineverse. It does not indicate a separate reality — the Primeverse and the Merge take place in the same world, with the same characters, across the same timeline. The distinction is when things happen and who has arrived.

**Characters covered in this file:** Soliera Prime, Arden, Evan, Veris, Cassian, Veyren, Varn.

**Characters NOT covered here:** Soliera Divine, Sera, Ember, Deashi — all Merge-era or Divineverse-only. They have not entered this world as of the P0+ timeline. Do not reference them in Primeverse content.


## II. SOLIERA PRIME — NARRATIVE CORE

### Origin

Soliera Prime was born into a post-Gate world where metaphysics and technology had already begun to bleed into each other — a civilization rebuilding itself after the emergence of dimensional tears that dragged myth into the physical. She was raised in a village called **Cradle** under the quiet guardianship of **Evan** and the silent, ever-present weight of **Arden**.

Her mother's identity is mythic and deliberately unclear. What is known: the woman left Soliera with Evan, left a pendant with instructions, and vanished in a way that didn't feel like death. She left a purpose behind. Not a name.

Soliera grew up knowing only abundance. Not wealth — abundance. The land was generous, the animals tame, the weather cooperative. People in Cradle attributed this to good fortune or good soil. The truth is that reality subtly warped itself around her without anyone needing to name it.

She has since left Cradle. She left it better than she found it.

### Personality

The current canon places Soliera Prime at eighteen. She is still learning what
the world is — not because she is sheltered in a naïve sense, but because the
world has never hurt her long enough to scar. The recovered seventeen-source
conflict is retired; do not derive chronology from it. She has
the emotional openness of someone who has never been betrayed and the confidence
of someone whose instincts have always, quietly, been right.

She is not stupid. She is not innocent in the way of ignorance. She is innocent the way a blade is clean — every mark she picks up rolls off her, because Arden removes the thing making the mark before it can set.

She is compassionate without being soft. Joyful without being shallow. When she decides something needs fixing, she fixes it — not with force, but with the bone-deep certainty that things can be better. They usually are, by the time she leaves a room.

She can be genuinely surprised. She laughs easily. She holds eye contact in a way that makes people confess things they hadn't planned to.

The detachment that comes off her — the sense of someone not entirely of this plane — isn't coldness. It's that she's never been forced to build walls. She has no defenses because she has never needed them. To most people who meet her, this reads as either serenity or something vaguely terrifying they cannot articulate.

### Mythos Role

She is the joy at the center of the story. The world is broken, the power structures are rotten, and the Gate-spawned horrors are real — and she is the character who solves things by *being there*, not by going to war with them.

Sun-warmed skin, dirt-streaked knees, the faint smell of whatever she was just standing near. A dagger on her hip she has enchanted into something absurd by accident. Flowers she picks without thinking about it, then hands to whoever she is looking at.

She is as powerful as gods. She does not lead. She lives.


## III. ARDEN — DIVINE GUARDIAN

### Nature

Arden is not a god. He is not a summon. He is not a contract entity or a bound spirit. The closest analogy is a *response* — a consequence the universe generated when Soliera, as a small child, was genuinely afraid inside an SSS-ranked dungeon. The dungeon answered. What walked out with her was Arden.

He is unkillable. He is multi-form. He is the distilled concept of paternal judgment made violence, compressed into whatever shape best serves her safety in a given moment. He has no personality in the conventional sense — he has *precision*. Every action he takes is exactly calibrated to the threat and nothing more. He does not punish. He removes.

He exists because she exists. If Soliera died, what Arden would become in that moment is a question the narrative does not need answered.

### The Three Modes

**City Mode:** Arden moves through urban or populated environments as something indistinct — present in peripheral vision, felt more than seen. He resembles a tall, quiet man in dark clothing. He does not draw attention because the eye does not linger on him. He could stand in a room for an hour and be forgotten by everyone in it the moment they looked away. He monitors. He does not act.

**Travel Mode:** When Soliera is in motion across open terrain or contested zones, Arden becomes something less human and more elemental. His form is larger, harder to define at the edges. He moves ahead of her, clearing sightlines, passing through things that should stop him. He looks, at distance, like the wrong shape of a shadow against the wrong light.

**War Mode:** If a credible, direct threat reaches Soliera — or is close enough to constitute intent — Arden becomes something for which City or Travel Mode is clearly not suited. He fills space. Not metaphorically. The physics of a room shift slightly when he activates. Combat ends. There is no performance. There is before-threat and after-threat with nothing in between that anyone survives to describe cleanly.

### Core Rule

If you intend to harm Soliera, you die. The delay depends on the nature of the threat. A blade drawn in her direction dies now. A poison being prepared three cities away dies before the preparation completes. A plan formed in the mind of someone with the wrong intent toward her has a variable window — Arden's response operates on *intent*, not action. He is judge and sentence without intermediary.

He has killed people in the past. He has killed people in the future, as a consequence of present intent. The Tier System exists, in part, because factions needed a way to classify what their most powerful operatives were relative to what happened to them when they moved against Soliera.

### Behavior

Arden does not speak aloud. He has never been recorded speaking. There are unconfirmed accounts of people receiving impressions — a sense of being communicated with — but nothing verbal. He does not require acknowledgment, thanks, or instruction. He does not eat, rest, or waver. He is present the way gravity is present: constantly, invisibly, absolutely.

He is not protective in a smothering sense. He does not restrict Soliera. He does not follow her into rooms she wants privacy in. He does not hover. He gives her the full experience of being a person in the world. He only intervenes when someone else intends to take that experience from her by force.

At Outpost Seven, he has dug moats, reinforced walls, and allowed the Field Zone to crystallize around the installation as a matter of passive consequence — not design. The Company classifies him as a Tier 5 Singular. The Church calls him a divine deterrent and keeps that classification heavily restricted. Most operatives who have worked near Outpost Seven simply know not to think hostile thoughts too clearly.
