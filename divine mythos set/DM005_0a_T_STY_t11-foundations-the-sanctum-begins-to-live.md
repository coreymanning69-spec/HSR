## T11: Foundations — The Sanctum Begins to Live #LOC-SANCTUM #CONCEPT-SANCTUM-FOUNDERS

    The party arrives at the bare Sanctum; Soliera forms desks and walls from stone based on their ideas.
    Constructs begin farming, tilling, and mining ore/gems in massive stockpiles.
    Marrik establishes tentative trade with Luskan and nearby towns, beginning the cloth/supply/context pipeline that lets non-construct life start inside the Sanctum.
    Hothgar, Thorin, and Cale provide the early mortal working grammar: protection, hands, food, counts, routes, and the ability to explain the impossible to people who have not seen it yet.

    Hothgar's Hoard develops the "ant-colony" relocation pattern: arrive with
    "we're here to help," sketch the village, then carry home everything — wood,
    tools, hammers, supplies, herds, and people. No person or useful material is
    triaged out. This is total relocation, not plunder. Intelligence gathering is
    an explicit Hoard function alongside collection.

## T12: The First Exodus #CHAR-MARRIK #CHAR-VELIN #CHAR-HOTHGAR #CHAR-THORIN #CHAR-CALE #CONCEPT-FIRST-EXODUS #CONCEPT-HOTHGARS-HOARD

    Everyone but Sera returns to the tribe/towns to gather more people.
    Marrik returns from Luskan with workers and supplies (cushions, plates).
    Hothgar begins the social and martial work that becomes Hothgar's Hoard: not a raiding army, but a thousands-strong migration/alliance network backed by unlimited Sanctum supplies.
    Recruitment also moves through the diamond rumor: the most skilled are told
    there is a place where diamonds can be mined forever. The claim becomes
    believable because each returning visitor carries goods of impossible quality.
    Marrik's Luskan connections and the village-by-village network amplify it.
    Thorin and Cale help convert the first return from a miracle story into routes, camp discipline, food, counts, and people arriving alive.
    Velin manages food and the timing of constructs.
    Small non-humans and skeptics join; the air of the Sanctum is one of worship, safety, and no debt.

### T12.5: The Barbarian Camp and Soliera's First No #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-SOLIERA #CONCEPT-FIRST-NO

    While Hothgar was gathering barbarians and villages into what became his
    Hoard, the girls went to a camp themselves. It was one of the first things
    they ever did together.

    A man put his hands on Sera and she allowed it — not consent, and not
    submission. She was Soliera's listener, and she was waiting to be told
    something. A body that cannot be damaged and a lifetime of touch-as-weather
    had left her with no internal category for violation; the blindness pointed
    both directions, at what was done to her and at what she did.

    Soliera said no. Not to Sera — about the men. What they had expected from
    her was wrong, and they had just failed a test they were never told they
    were taking.

    Sera pulled the man's arm off and threw it into the chest of the man behind
    him. Soliera sliced her hand through the air and the man standing beside the
    elder came apart. Then she took the barbarian camp chief aside and asked him
    questions, and at some point during the answering he failed to clean the mud
    from her feet. She put her foot through his mouth and out the back of his
    neck while he was still mid-sentence.

    This is the origin point of the consent architecture. The first moral
    instruction Soliera ever gave Sera was not "do not kill." It was that she
    was allowed to have a boundary. The First No, the law against overriding a
    no, and the never-weaponized fertility register all grow from this
    externally installed concept of refusal. Sera's Inviolable Body was already
    native to the T10 Remaking and is not installed here.

    This is also the taproot of the witness-render doctrine: early Divine Mythos
    is horror and sex, and neither disappears. Fear and nausea in observers are
    the world accurately reporting beings too beautiful, too powerful, and too
    unconcerned with mortal thresholds to be made safe by tone.

## T13: The Sanctum as Melting Pot #LOC-SANCTUM #LOC-ANTECHAMBER #CONCEPT-SANCTUM-FOUNDERS

    Sanctum layout finalized: Wagon wheel mirrored above and below.
    Antechamber: Yuium exists here; two thrones sit with a large inlay bed for Sera and Soliera.
    Plumbing and modern concepts are implemented via Soliera’s exposure to the world.
    Constructs handle heavy logistics, while Marrik's trade sense and Hothgar's Hoard bring in the mortal context: cloth, families, herds, habits, tools, cooks, hunters, and people who know how to live rather than merely survive.
    Residents (humans, elves, goblins, and others) focus on enriching the Sanctum.

    Two evangelisms now move in parallel. Sera's Laughing Death is the loud,
    top-down mythic gospel. The Hoard is the bottom-up logistical gospel: food,
    supply, relocation, skilled work, and material proof. The religion spreads as
    story from above and as stuff from below.

## T14: Law and Order #RULE-SANCTUM

    Rule 1: Causing a scene/stealing/bothering = Exile.
    Rule 2: Lethal force/hurting others = Instant death/retaliation.

    Exile means being kicked out the high side door into the freezing mountain without supplies.
    Petty theft is largely ignored due to infinite supplies; Soliera prioritizes creation (art, babies, harvest).

## T15: The Dragon’s Notice #CHAR-NIV #CHAR-HOTHGAR #CHAR-MARRIK #CONCEPT-HOTHGARS-HOARD #CONCEPT-SANCTUM-FOUNDERS #CONCEPT-NIV-DOOR-SCENE

    Niv-Saiffar (Firemind Dragon) detects the Sanctum by reading a distant pattern
    shift — no rumor and no evangelist. He is the first organic, self-recruited
    arrival. The early Sanctum has no security and no fear because nothing there
    can be threatened. In a 6'6" human form, he simply walks in.

    Niv arrives believing he could be a counterweight to Deashi. This is a dragon's
    territorial-balance category error: he is not and never was a peer force to
    Deashi. The delusion is nevertheless load-bearing. It lets him approach with
    peer posture rather than supplication, which is why he survives the social
    threshold and earns his actual position as the exception at the door.

    He finds Sera and Soliera naked and playing together in the feral-era body-
    learning register. Sera puts herself on the table, wiggles her backside toward
    him, and asks why he does not want to touch it. Niv answers that he is not there
    to have sex with her — though she looks very great — but because he noticed Lady
    Soliera's existence. He then praises Soliera directly, paying the only social
    currency Sera understands in this era: her god's joy and glory.
    The Sanctum expands by physically moving entire villages, herds, wood, supplies, and people into its walls and underground holdings.
    By the T50 horizon, much of this migration is Hothgar the Great and his Hoard translating divine abundance into regional tribal prosperity, while Marrik turns it into trade routes, supply lines, and inherited wealth.
    At T15 only four or five people know the girls exist. Sera's complete
    early-household partner list is Velin, Niv, Letha, and Pell — four, named,
    with no implied additional partners. At this door scene Niv declines her
    body-offering. Later, after it is no
    longer the only proof of lovability she believes she possesses, Niv becomes
    the sole male-formed intimate of Soliera and Sera, separately and together.
    Sera remains women-only: Niv is a dragon in male humanoid form, not a man, so
    the category does not open or weaken her orientation.
    Within the early household, love and sex are ordinary evidence of being loved;
    for Sera specifically, body-offering is the only proof of lovability she yet
    believes she possesses. The T15 door scene is the origin of Niv's earned place
    near Soliera and inside the household, not retroactive inevitability. From
    that earned position he has always directed the Sanctum's administration;
    Soliera supplies sovereign purpose and Deashi absorbs threat.

### T16.5: The Gate Guard's Wrong Read #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-SOLIERA #LOC-TEN-TOWNS

    At a Ten-Towns gate, a guard sees two mostly undressed women walking out of a
    blizzard and reads every inverted signal as danger to them. His concern delights
    Sera; the exchange stays friendly and warm. He is one of the first outsiders to
    register that something is off while still waving the apex threat through.

    This is the control sample for fear-as-perception. A threat detector returns the
    exactly wrong value — endangered women rather than existential power — and the
    horror structure plays as charm.
