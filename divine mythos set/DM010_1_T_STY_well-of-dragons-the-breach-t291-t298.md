T291: Fear Thickens — Then Fades #LOC-WELL-OF-DRAGONS #ARMY-MORALE #THEME-FAITH

The Well of Dragons is not a place that wants to be approached. The walls are obsidian reinforced by a century of draconic ritual — stained in the colors of five chromatic lines, each stone placed by cultist hands over decades of patient, faithful work. The air smells of sulfur and something older underneath it, a pressure that doesn't live in the nose so much as behind the eyes. The Draakhorn sounded like a throat without a god. Every soldier in the formation has felt it since dawn. Some have felt it since yesterday.

No one breaks formation.

The divine path still glows beneath their feet — the soft gold-green luminescence that has followed them since the march began, Soliera's work, the literal ground of this entire enterprise. It doesn't dim at the walls. It doesn't shy from the smell.

The factions are visible only in fragments now: Harper silver-thread at a collar, a Gauntlet holy symbol bright against dented mail, Enclave green bands tied around healer wrists, Zhentarim serpent pins hidden until the light catches them wrong, Vanguard field pins warming under breastplates. The mustering-field arguments are gone. The leaders are gone from the shape of the moment. What remains is what they sent into the line.

Somewhere in the third rank, a soldier — no rank attached to this, no name recorded — gets tired of being afraid and says so aloud.

*"Fuck Tiamat. We chose this. We're not even gonna die."*

It is not a war cry. It is just a man who finished with his fear. The ripple moves through the formation the way belief always moves — not loudly, not all at once, but steadily, person to person, shoulder to shoulder, until it reaches the flanks and doubles back through the middle. By the time it arrives at the vanguard, it isn't one man's frustration anymore. It's something else. The myth has shifted. They are not marching into the god of dragons' fortress. They are witnesses who finally caught up with something that was already decided.

The spider constructs do not feel the ripple. They only adjust formation by two degrees, finding the angle where the next volley will waste itself on metal instead of flesh. Faith changes the soldiers. Logistics changes the battlefield.


T292: She Says His Name #CHAR-SOLIERA #CHAR-DEASHI #DIVINE-CALL #NONVERBAL

Soliera walks at the front. She does not look at the walls or the cultists deploying on the ramparts or the five colors of dragonfire being arranged above the inner courtyard. She looks inward for exactly one moment.

She says one word. Not loudly. Not toward anyone visible. The name drops from her like a stone into still water — no echo, no ceremony, just the act of speaking it.

*"Deashi."*

That is all. She walks on.

One soldier close enough to hear it does not know the name. He only knows Soliera has spoken to someone who is not there yet, and the fact is somehow worse and better than any trumpet could have been.


T293: Total Envelopment #CONSTRUCT-ARMY #COMBAT-TACTICS #UNIT-TYPES

The constructs deploy with the logic of inevitability. Spider units hit the obsidian cliffs before the first volley of dragonfire has finished falling — they run at angles geometry shouldn't permit and hold anyway, silent and methodical, scaling the faces of a fortress designed to resist armies and finding nothing in its design that anticipated this particular army. Lupine constructs fan wide across the outer approach, running perimeter corridors that turn the cultists' flanks into kill zones through attrition alone. No noise. No frenzy. Just function.

Behind them: the knight-class juggernauts. They march through volleys of fire and acid and lightning that accomplish nothing. Not because of wards. Because of Soliera's engineering, which is simply better than the Cult of the Dragon's ritual defenses, and because logistics beats faith every time when the logistics are this thorough.

Behind the constructs, the mortal gifts do their quieter work. Harper signals pass without catching hostile eyes. Gauntlet shields take angles that should have broken wrists and do not. Enclave bands pull exhaustion out of men who have no time to collapse. Vanguard lines hold because the armor distributes fear's physical consequences before courage has to make a speech about it.

Niv-Saiffar launches overhead. His first strike hits the outer defenses not like a dragon's breath but like a verdict — a full aurora of flame across the ramparts that communicates clearly what kind of moment this is. The outer wall begins to fail.

Inside the first breach, spider constructs fold flat and pour through murder-slits like poured tools, unfolding on the far side with blades already placed. The juggernauts do not batter the gate mechanism. They brace around it, seize the load-bearing pins, and make the entire door understand that it has become a solved problem.

The breach begins.


T294: Sera Breaches the Command Post #CHAR-SERA #COMBAT-BREACH #CHAR-SYLVANDOR

Soliera walks. Niv burns. Sera is the storm.

The command post is hardened — stone over arcane lattice, designed to survive a siege and staffed by the Cult's finest remaining logisticians. It has reinforced doors and defensive wards and three layers of armed guards on rotation. She does not use the door.

She removes a section of wall. Not dramatically. With the same casual efficiency someone might use to open a window, except the window is six feet of load-bearing stone and it leaves the building as a single piece at considerable speed. Dust, screams, scattered scrolls that catch fire on the way down. Inside: confusion, an overturned tactical table, a half-dozen guards who were not prepared for the entrance to come from that direction, and Sylvandor — the mage of the Spiral Veil, last functioning architect of the summoning's command structure.

He is already mid-syllable when she clears the threshold. The arcane circle beneath him is lighting up fast.

She is already across the room.


T295: "You" — Sylvandor's End #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-SYLVANDOR #COMBAT-EXECUTION

Sera's eyes gleam like stars behind glass. She picks Sylvandor up by the throat before he finishes the first syllable of his spell.

The arcane circle beneath him flickers — collapses.

*"I told you once,"* she says, and then she drives his head through a stone column.

The command post's defenders flood in from the adjacent corridor. She turns to face them and they stop. She is smiling. The smile is genuine. She does not rush after them.

Note: She never told him anything before, she just doesn't care. She considers everything the same and has been killing the Dragon Cultists for a while.


T296: Tiamat Tears Through #CHAR-TIAMAT #EVENT-PARTIAL-MANIFESTATION #ARC-CLIMAX #LOC-BLACK-DRAGON-MASK

The ritual chamber is below — carved into the volcanic rock the Well sits on, the largest summoning circle in recorded Faerûnian history. Every line burned in by blood and time and a century of genuine theological commitment. The cultists remaining at the circle have been casting for hours. They are exhausted. They are faithful. They are almost done.

And it works.

Not cleanly. Not fully. The ritual was designed to anchor Tiamat at her complete power, and the command structure that would have held that anchoring is now a room full of unconscious guards and one mage with a relationship to a stone column. But the summoning doesn't require the command post to function. It requires the circle, and the circle is intact, and the remaining cultists don't need to be told what's happening — they can feel her, pressing against the membrane of the ritual from the other side, and they pour everything they have left into holding the door open.

She comes through in pieces. First the pressure doubles — the air acquiring a density that has nothing to do with altitude, torches snuffing out and reigniting in wrong colors, the stone itself beginning to groan in the specific register of materials that are being made to hold something they were not built for. Then the heads emerge. Five of them, five different violations of what the room can physically contain, each radiating something distinct: fire-heat, ozone-crackle, dissolving-corrosion, killing-cold, the sweet-wrong smell of draconic venom. The body follows, never fully here — half-manifested, the physical form intersecting with the world at angles that make the eyes slide away — but present. Real. Five heads on a form that doesn't have a clean shape yet and is still more terrifying than anything else currently alive in this hemisphere.

She is not diminished by the partial arrival. She is furious at being interrupted.

Off to one side of the chamber, sitting on a carved stone shelf where it has been waiting, is the Black Dragon Mask. She does not look at it.

She does not look at the cultists either, though they are on their knees weeping with joy at what they called forth.

She looks at Sera.

Sound bends around her. Prayers come out of cultist mouths a half-beat late, syllables bruised by the pressure of a god forcing herself into a room too small for divinity. Blood rises in noses. Knees hit stone. No one present mistakes the partial arrival for weakness.


T297: The Fivefold Disintegration #CHAR-TIAMAT #CHAR-SERA #EVENT-DISINTEGRATION #EVENT-PRESSURE-LIFT #CHAR-NIV-SAIFFAR

Above the inner walls, Niv-Saiffar holds altitude and releases a full aurora of flame. Not destruction — judgment. The sky becomes a sentencing. Cultists who were running toward the inner courtyard stop running and start trying to remember if there is a direction that doesn't have fire in it.

Below, in the ritual chamber:

Tiamat does not announce what she is about to do. There is no posturing, no speech, no draconic declaration. She looks at Sera with all five heads simultaneously — five different sets of eyes converging on the same point — and then all five heads act at once. Fire. Lightning. Acid. Frost. Venom. Not in sequence. Not overlapping. Simultaneously, woven into a single act of coordinated divine annihilation, the kind of strike that ends things that are not supposed to end.

It is on purpose. It is deliberate. She chose Sera specifically.

Sera lets it hit.

The blast radius takes out the far third of the chamber. Stone cracks and the cracks breathe. The summoning circle scorches black at its outermost rings. The cultists closest to Sera are gone — not killed so much as simply removed from the category of things that exist. What remains where Sera was standing is a brief negative space, the shape of an absence, and then smoke and smell and nothing.

Outside the Well, the change is immediate.

The ambient weight the entire mortal formation has been feeling since dawn — that low constant certainty, the background pressure that said *Soliera is near, the world will hold, something is here that is larger than your fear* — severs. It doesn't fade. It cuts out, exactly like a candle snuffed in a room that immediately goes very dark.

Every mortal soldier outside the Well feels it at exactly the same instant.

They all think Soliera is dead.

For that instant there are no factions. Harper, Gauntlet, Enclave, Vanguard, Zhent, Brotherhood observer, mercenary, farmer with a spear — every category collapses into one human fact: the light they had been standing inside has gone out.

The formation doesn't break. It goes somewhere past breaking, into the territory of people struck with something they cannot process. Weapons lower without orders. Two soldiers in the third rank reach for each other without looking. Somewhere in the mid-distance, a banner-carrier sets the banner down carefully and then just stands there. The constructs keep moving — they do not feel it — and for a moment the mortal army watches the constructs continue to function and has nothing to say about it.

Someone near the vanguard starts a prayer and cannot remember who it is supposed to be addressed to. His hands stay locked around the haft of his spear, white-knuckled, as if letting go would make the dark permanent.

Above, Niv holds altitude. He does not continue the aurora. He waits.


T298: She's Back — The Heartbeat #CHAR-SERA #CHAR-DEASHI #CHAR-TIAMAT #EVENT-RECONSTITUTION #EVENT-TIAMAT-ERASED #BRIDGE-TO-DM010_2

The void reconstitutes.

It does not announce itself. There is a moment where there is nothing where Sera was, and then there is Sera — standing in the same spot, on the same stones, hair singed at the tips and otherwise entirely intact and entirely herself. She looks down. Checks her hands. Looks up at five heads and one partially-manifested deity and the ruin of the chamber around her.

She sticks her tongue out at Tiamat.

*"Won't get a second shot,"* she says. The joy in it is real. It is possibly the most genuinely delighted thing Tiamat has ever been told, and Tiamat is old enough to have heard most things.

She does not get a second shot.

Deashi is already here. He was here before the disintegration cleared — the name Soliera spoke a beat ago was not a hope, it was a fact with a short delay — and Tiamat feels his presence a half-second before she sees anything. That half-second is enough. She does not make a speech. She does not argue with the physics of it. She turns, recognizes what she is looking at — what Deashi is, specifically, what he means, what he does — and drives through the far wall of the chamber at maximum speed.

It does not help.

She blinks out. Not a retreat. Not a banishment. An erasure. The god of chromatic dragons, partially manifested in the most powerful summoning circle on the material plane, is simply no longer there. Deashi does not explain himself. He came because he was called, and he finished what needed finishing, and now there is a Tiamat-shaped hole in the far wall and nothing on the other side of it anywhere in any accessible layer of existence.

The surviving cultists — the ones far enough back to have lived through the disintegration and Sera returning — stare at the hole. Nobody from the army outside is in this chamber. No one is coming in. They are alone with an absence and whatever is building in the center of the summoning circle, which was supposed to anchor a god and now has nowhere to put what it gathered.

Soliera does not look at the hole. She looks at the Black Dragon Mask on its carved stone shelf — the obsidian face that carries everything the cult spent on the premise of a god who will not be arriving. Every death. Every act of faith. All of it poured into that inert dark surface, waiting.

She looks at it. She allows it.

Across the chamber, Deashi finishes. Both happen at the same time — his erasure complete, her permission given — and the mask begins to dissolve. Not shattering. Not crumbling. Coming apart into the shape of something that was always possible inside it, the sacrificial energy finding a form because someone permitted it to.

The ritual energy has nowhere to go. Then it does.

Instead of a scream: a heartbeat.
